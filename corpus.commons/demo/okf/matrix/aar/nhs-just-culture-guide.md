# NHS Improvement Just Culture Guide, After-Action Review Distillation

**Source:** NHS Improvement (2018). *A Just Culture Guide — Supporting consistent, constructive and fair evaluation of the actions of staff involved in patient safety incidents*. A3 portrait poster. Licence: **Open Government Licence v3.0**; the poster states no licence, and the basis is NHS England's website terms. **Scope:** open. Attributes itself to "the work of Professor James Reason and the National Patient Safety Agency's Incident Decision Tree" [V] (borrowed-through; Reason and the NPSA Incident Decision Tree are not held directly in this corpus).

## After-Action Review Relevance

The NHS Just Culture Guide is a single-page five-test decision tree for a call an AAR facilitator faces: whether an individual action warrants management intervention or whether the incident points to system-level corrective work. Its preamble states the prior the tree works from: "Action singling out an individual is rarely appropriate - most patient safety issues have deeper causes and require wider action" [V] (Source: NHS Improvement, *Just Culture Guide*, Preamble, paragraph 1). Two of the seven recommendation end-states (D and E) explicitly say action singling out the individual is unlikely to be appropriate, with wider actions that may include, but not be limited to, the individual; outside the deliberate-harm branch, only Recommendation G — reached on No to Q1, No to all three Q2 questions about indications of ill health or substance abuse, Yes to all three Q3 questions about protocols/accepted practice, No to all three Q4 questions, and No at Q5 — routes to individual management action, and even G says the patient safety incident investigation should indicate the wider actions needed. The guide is also a communication tool: "an important part of a just culture is being able to explain the approach that will be taken if an incident occurs" [V] (Source: Preamble, paragraph 3). In AAR work, it fires on Phase 0 (set the default-to-system posture before the conversation begins), Phase 3 (walk the decision tree when an individual-action concern surfaces), and Phase 4 (communicate the decision to stakeholders using the guide as a shared reference).

## Key Concepts for After-Action Review

1.  **Default-to-system prior.** "Action singling out an individual is rarely appropriate - most patient safety issues have deeper causes and require wider action" [V] (Source: NHS Improvement, *Just Culture Guide*, Preamble, paragraph 1). The poster states this prior in its preamble, before the five-test tree; in AAR use, set it before the tree is run, not after.

2.  **Five sequential tests, gated.** Q1 deliberate harm → Q2 health → Q3 foresight → Q4 substitution → Q5 mitigating circumstances. Each test gates the next; only one action or omission is taken through the tree at a time; multiple actions in one incident must be evaluated separately. (Source: Preamble, "Please note", bullet 4)

3.  **Q1 deliberate harm test.** Single question: "Was there any intention to cause harm?" [V] (Source: Q1) A Yes exits to Recommendation A, the only path that names referral to police. Even Recommendation A closes with "Wider investigation is still needed to understand how and why patients were not protected from the actions of the individual" [V] (Source: Q1, Recommendation A). A No proceeds to Q2.

4.  **Q2 health test.** Three sub-questions: are there indications of substance abuse, of physical ill health, of mental ill health? A Yes to 2a exits to Recommendation B (organisational substance abuse at work guidance); a Yes to 2b or 2c exits to Recommendation C (organisational guidance for health issues affecting work, likely to include occupational health referral). Both pair the guidance with a backward-looking system question: could the substance abuse or health issue have been recognised and addressed earlier? A No to all three proceeds to Q3. (Source: Q2)

5.  **Q3 foresight test.** Three sub-questions: Are there agreed protocols/accepted practice in place that apply to the action or omission? Were they workable and in routine use? Did the individual knowingly depart from these protocols? All three must be Yes to proceed to Q4; No to any routes to Recommendation D: "Action singling out the individual is unlikely to be appropriate; the patient safety incident investigation should indicate the wider actions needed to improve safety for future patients. These actions may include, but not be limited to, the individual." [V] (Source: Q3, Recommendation D)

6.  **Q4 substitution test — peer, training, and supervision probe.** Three sub-questions: Are there indications that comparable peers would behave the same way in similar circumstances? Was the individual missed out when relevant training was provided to their peer group? Did more senior team members fail to provide supervision that normally should be provided? Any Yes routes to Recommendation E — action singling out the individual is unlikely to be appropriate, with wider actions that may include, but not be limited to, the individual. A No to all three proceeds to Q5. (Source: Q4)

7.  **Q5 mitigating circumstances — the residual.** Single question: "Were there any significant mitigating circumstances?" [V] A Yes exits to Recommendation F: "Action directed at the individual may not be appropriate; follow organisational guidance, which is likely to include senior HR advice on what degree of mitigation applies." [V] (Source: Q5, Recommendation F) A No exits to Recommendation G.

8.  **Recommendation G — management action plus wider investigation.** Reached on No at Q5, after Q1 to Q4 have each routed onward. Its management action "could involve individual training, performance management, competency assessments, changes to role or increased supervision" [V], and "may require relevant regulatory bodies to be contacted, staff suspension and disciplinary processes" [V]. Still paired with the wider-investigation obligation. (Source: Q5, Recommendation G)

9.  **One action at a time, evaluated separately.** "The guide can only be used to take one action (or failure to act) through the guide at a time. If multiple actions are involved in an incident they must be considered separately" [V] (Source: Preamble, "Please note", bullet 4). An incident involving several actions is multiple trips through the tree, not one.

10.  **Re-entrant: revisit as more information becomes available.** "A just culture guide can be used at any point of an investigation, but the guide may need to be revisited as more information becomes available" [V] (Source: Preamble, "Please note", bullet 2). The tree is not a once-only assessment.

11.  **Communication tool, not just decision aid.** The guide "can be used by all parties to explain how they will respond to incidents" [V]. "An important part of a just culture is being able to explain the approach that will be taken if an incident occurs." [V] (Source: Preamble, paragraph 3) In AAR use, the guide can be shared with the team before the conversation begins.

12.  **Borrowed-through Reason / NPSA lineage.** The poster attributes itself to "the work of Professor James Reason and the National Patient Safety Agency's Incident Decision Tree." [V] Reason and the NPSA Incident Decision Tree are carried borrowed-through; the demo does not hold them directly.

## Questions to Ask During After-Action Review

### Phase 0: Scoping

| Need | Question |
|---|---|
| Has the default-to-system posture been set? | Before the first question, has someone read aloud: "action singling out an individual is rarely appropriate - most patient safety issues have deeper causes and require wider action" [V]? |
| Is the guide being used as a communication tool? | Has the guide been shared, so those involved know in advance the framework that will be used? The source names it as a communication tool to help staff, patients and families understand how the response can and should differ by circumstance. |
| Is the investigation sufficiently advanced to run the tree? | Are the facts available for each test, or will the tree need to be re-entered later as more information becomes available? |

### Phase 1: Timeline and Local-Rationality Reconstruction

| Need | Question |
|---|---|
| Are the facts being separated by action? | If multiple actions are under review, has each been decomposed into a separate trip through the tree? |
| Is the timeline surfacing health indicators? | Are there observations suggesting substance abuse, physical ill health, or mental ill health that would feed Q2? |
| Is the foresight test's protocol question answerable? | Are there agreed protocols/accepted practice in place that apply to the action in question? Were they workable and in routine use? These facts need to be established in the timeline before Q3 can be run. |

### Phase 2: Contributory-Factor Analysis

| Need | Question |
|---|---|
| Where do the contributors sit: in the individual's action or in the conditions? | Which contributors sit in the conditions the tree tests (protocols under Q3; peer behaviour, training and supervision under Q4) rather than in the individual's action? Both belong in the analysis. |
| Does the substitution test apply here? | Are there indications that other individuals from the same peer group, with comparable experience and qualifications, would behave in the same way in similar circumstances? If yes, singling out the individual is unlikely to be appropriate; the wider actions the investigation indicates may include, but not be limited to, the individual. |
| Was training missed or supervision absent? | The substitution test asks about both explicitly: whether the individual was missed out when relevant training was provided to their peer group (Q4b), and whether more senior team members failed to provide supervision that normally should be provided (Q4c). A Yes to either routes to Recommendation E: singling out the individual is unlikely to be appropriate, though wider actions may include the individual. |

### Phase 3: Just-Culture Sorting

| Need | Question |
|---|---|
| Q1: Was there any intention to cause harm? | Answer honestly; a Yes exits immediately to Recommendation A and potential regulatory/police referral. |
| Q2: Are there health indicators? | Indications of substance abuse route to organisational substance abuse at work guidance (B); indications of physical or mental ill health route to organisational guidance for health issues affecting work (C). Both are paired with wider investigation. |
| Q3: Foresight test — all three Yes to advance. | (a) Are there agreed protocols/accepted practice in place that apply? (b) Were they workable and in routine use? (c) Did the individual knowingly depart from these protocols? All three must be Yes to reach Q4. |
| Q4: Substitution test — any Yes routes to Recommendation E. | (a) Are there indications that comparable peers would behave the same way in similar circumstances? (b) Was the individual missed out when relevant training was provided to their peer group? (c) Did more senior team members fail to provide supervision that normally should be provided? Any Yes routes to Recommendation E. |
| Q5: Are there significant mitigating circumstances? | If the case has cleared Q1-Q4, this final filter determines whether management action is appropriate or conditional. |

### Phase 4: Action Design

| Need | Question |
|---|---|
| What recommendation end-state has the tree produced? | Seven end-states (A through G); name the specific recommendation and its operational guidance. |
| Does the recommendation name a wider-investigation obligation? | Every recommendation, including A and G, carries a wider-investigation directive: A–C say "Wider investigation is still needed" [V]; D–G say "the patient safety incident investigation should indicate the wider actions needed" [V]. Has that wider action been named? |
| Is the individual response, if any, matched to the case? | Recommendation G names training, performance management, competency assessment, role change and increased supervision, and says the case may also require regulatory contact, suspension and disciplinary processes. Which of these does organisational guidance point to for this case? |
| Has the response been explained to all parties? | The guide's dual purpose: "protecting staff from unfair targeting" [V] and protecting patients "by removing the tendency to treat wider patient safety issues as individual issues." [V] Has both been communicated? |

## What to Look For

| Signal | Diagnosis | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| The group moves from timeline to individual accountability without running the five-test tree | Default-to-individual failure — the preamble's prior that action singling out an individual is rarely appropriate was bypassed | Pause; establish the default-to-system prior; run the tree from Q1 |
| A protocol is cited as the standard but no one confirms it was workable and routinely used | Q3b is unexamined (workable and in routine use are both parts of 3b); Q3c, knowing departure, cannot be assumed either — the foresight test may not advance to Q4 | Establish: was the protocol workable? Was it actually in routine use? Both must be true before Q3 advances |
| The group runs all five individuals in an incident through the tree together | Multiple trips have been collapsed into one — the tree must be run separately for each action or omission | Restart: "We're going to run one action at a time through the guide." |
| The group treats Recommendation G as the expected outcome | G is reached only on No at Q5, after Q1 to Q4 have each routed onward — treating it as expected runs against the preamble's statement that action singling out an individual is rarely appropriate | Restate the default-to-system prior before the tree is run |
| New investigation facts emerge after the tree has been run | The path may have changed — the guide is re-entrant | Re-run the affected test with the new facts; the result may shift |

## When to Use This Reference

**Currency (checked 2026-10-17, from NHS England's patient-safety pages, not the poster):** NHS England has replaced this 2018 guide with its being fair tool, after a 2023–24 review found the guide no longer fit for purpose. Cite it as the 2018 guide, not as current NHS practice.

Reach for this source when:
- Phase 3 requires a structured framework for the individual-vs-system locus decision — the five-test tree is a decision aid built for this call.
- The default-to-system posture needs to be established at Phase 0 before any individual accounts are heard.
- The guide is needed as a communication tool — to share with all parties in advance so the framework is known before the incident is discussed.
- New facts emerge after the initial just-culture sort and the tree needs to be re-entered.

Do not use this source as the primary AAR protocol (TC 25-20 and LFUO carry that), and do not treat it as a substitute for investigation. The guide is not a replacement for an investigation; only a full investigation can identify the underlying causes.

## Worked Example

A hospital team reviews a medication incident in which a nurse administered the wrong drug from a look-alike packaging pair. The facilitator opens Phase 0 by reading the guide's default-to-system prior aloud and sharing the guide with the nurse, the charge nurse, and the unit manager before any discussion begins. (Source: NHS Improvement, *Just Culture Guide*, Preamble.) In Phase 3, the facilitator runs Q1 (no deliberate harm) and Q2 (no health indicators) and reaches Q3. Three sub-tests: (a) Are there agreed protocols/accepted practice in place that apply? Yes. (b) Were they workable and in routine use? No — the investigation establishes that the look-alike packaging had been flagged twice before and no mitigation had been implemented. The protocol required visual comparison of drug names, but the packaging was designed to make visual comparison difficult. Q3b is No; the tree routes to Recommendation D: "Action singling out the individual is unlikely to be appropriate; the patient safety incident investigation should indicate the wider actions needed to improve safety for future patients. These actions may include, but not be limited to, the individual." [V] (Source: Q3, Recommendation D.) The Phase 4 action targets the procurement process (look-alike packaging policy) and the patient-safety reporting mechanism (prior flags had not reached a decision-maker). The guide is shared with the nurse directly: the framing makes clear that the route landed at Recommendation D, not because the incident is ignored, but because the system conditions are the primary target.

## Anti-patterns This Reference Helps Avoid

- Defaulting to individual blame before running the five-test tree.
- Running the tree once and treating the result as final, when the guide may need to be revisited as more information becomes available.
- Bundling multiple actions in one incident into a single trip through the tree; each action must be evaluated separately.
- Treating Recommendation G as the default expectation, when the preamble says action singling out an individual is rarely appropriate.
- Using the guide as a substitute for investigation; only a full investigation identifies the underlying causes that need action.
- Skipping the communication-tool function of the guide; staff, patients, and families should see the framework before incidents occur, not during the worst moment.
- Treating the health test (Q2) as an obstacle to accountability rather than a route to organisational substance-abuse-at-work or health guidance (the latter likely to include occupational health referral).
- Conflating the foresight test's "workable and in routine use" [V] requirement with mere protocol existence — a protocol that is unworkable or not in routine use fails Q3b and routes to Recommendation D.
- Citing Reason or the NPSA Incident Decision Tree as if the demo holds them directly; both are borrowed-through this guide's footer attribution.
