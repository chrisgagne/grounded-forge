# U.S. Marine Corps, MCDP 1: Warfighting, Retrospective Distillation

**Source:** U.S. Marine Corps (1997). *MCDP 1: Warfighting*. Headquarters United States Marine Corps, Department of the Navy. Foreword signed by General C. C. Krulak, 20 June 1997. PCN 142 000006 00. Supersedes FMFM 1 *Warfighting* (1989). **Licence:** US Government work, public domain (17 U.S.C. § 105). **Scope:** open. The Notes cite Clausewitz, Sun Tzu, Boyd, Lind, Gelernter and others (borrowed-through; none is held directly in this corpus).

## Retrospective Relevance

MCDP 1 is Marine Corps warfighting doctrine, not a retrospective method, and it fires on the retro axis at moderate strength. Its retro contribution sits mostly in a few pages of Ch 3, with two tools from Ch 4 and one list of examples from Ch 1. Ch 3 describes the Marine Corps' own review practice, which the document calls a *critique*: held right after training, open to everyone, and focused on why people acted as they did (Ch 3, "Training"). The same chapter sets the error climate the review depends on: no "zero defects" mentality, leniency for errors that come from boldness, severity for errors of inaction, and a duty to give honest opinions before a decision is made (Ch 3, "Professionalism"). Ch 4 supplies two tools for experiment design: the *main effort*, one action recognised as most critical at the moment, and *commander's intent*, the purpose that outlasts the task (Ch 4, "Main effort"; "Commander's intent").

The load-bearing passage for a retro lead is the critique paragraph, quoted whole:

> Critiques are an important part of training because critical self-analysis, even after success, is essential to improvement. Their purpose is to draw out the lessons of training. As a result, we should conduct critiques immediately after completing training, before memory of the events has faded. Critiques should be held in an atmosphere of open and frank dialogue in which all hands are encouraged to contribute. We learn as much from mistakes as from things done well, so we must be willing to admit mistakes and discuss them. Of course, a subordinate's willingness to admit mistakes depends on the commander's willingness to tolerate them. Because we recognize that no two situations in war are the same, our critiques should focus not so much on the actions we took as on why we took those actions and why they brought the results they did.
> [V] (Ch 3, "Training")

Frame to bring: in a retro, MCDP 1 gives the lead a stance, not a procedure. The document says of itself that it *"contains no specific techniques or procedures for conduct"* [V] (Foreword). It tells the lead what the review is for, what climate makes it honest, and how to pick one experiment; the mechanics come from the Field Guide, Liberating Structures and the Open Practice Library. Throughout this distillation, *commander* maps to the lead or manager with authority in the room and *subordinate* to the team member; that mapping is the distillation's, not the doctrine's.

## Key Concepts for Retrospective

1.  **The critique: review even after success, and review promptly.** Critiques exist *"to draw out the lessons of training"* [V], because *"critical self-analysis, even after success, is essential to improvement"* [V], and they are held *"immediately after completing training, before memory of the events has faded"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 3, "Training"). In a retro: a good Sprint still gets a retro, and the retro sits close to the end of the iteration. MCDP 1's word for this practice is *critique*; it does not use the terms after-action review or retrospective.

2.  **Candour depends on the tolerance of whoever holds authority.** Critiques run as *"open and frank dialogue in which all hands are encouraged to contribute"* [V], and *"a subordinate's willingness to admit mistakes depends on the commander's willingness to tolerate them"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 3, "Training"). In a retro: the lead and any manager present set the ceiling on what the team will admit. The doctrine states the aim (all hands contribute) and gives no method for reaching it.

3.  **Review the reasons, not only the actions.** Because no two situations are the same, critiques *"should focus not so much on the actions we took as on why we took those actions and why they brought the results they did"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 3, "Training"). In a retro: the unit of analysis is the decision and its context, which is also the natural redirect when blame language names a person.

4.  **No "zero defects" mentality, with limits.** The doctrine holds that *"errors by junior leaders stemming from overboldness are a necessary part of learning"* [V] and continues:

   > We should deal with such errors leniently; there must be no "zero defects" mentality. Abolishing "zero defects" means that we do not stifle boldness or initiative through the threat of punishment. It does not mean that commanders do not counsel subordinates on mistakes; constructive criticism is an important element in learning. Nor does it give subordinates free license to act stupidly or recklessly.
   > [V] (Ch 3, "Professionalism")

   In a retro: an experiment the team tried boldly that failed sits on the protected side of this passage; a reckless one does not. Leniency here is not silence; the mistake is still discussed.

5.  **Severity for errors of inaction.** Against the leniency for bold errors, the doctrine says *"we should deal severely with errors of inaction"* [V] or timidity; *"We will not accept lack of orders as justification for inaction; it is each Marine's duty to take initiative as the situation demands"* [V]; and *"We must not tolerate the avoidance of responsibility or necessary risk"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 3, "Professionalism"). In a retro: the asymmetry widens the review to things the team did not do. The retro axis is blameless by default, so this distillation keeps the doctrine's seriousness about inaction as a topic and does not carry its word *severity* into sanctions. That translation is the distillation's.

6.  **Honest dissent before the decision, full support after.**

   > Until a commander has reached and stated a decision, subordinates should consider it their duty to provide honest, professional opinions even though these may be in disagreement with the senior's opinions. However, once the decision has been reached, juniors then must support it as if it were their own.
   > [V] (Ch 3, "Professionalism")

   The same passage says that ready compliance for personal advancement, *"the behavior of 'yes-men' — will not be tolerated"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 3, "Professionalism"). In a retro: disagreement belongs before the team commits to an experiment, and quick agreement is not the same as commitment.

7.  **Trust runs both ways and is earned.** The doctrine calls for trust by seniors in their subordinates' abilities and by juniors in their seniors' competence and support [AP]; *"Trust must be earned, and actions which undermine trust must meet with strict censure"* [V]; *"Trust is a product of confidence and familiarity"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 3, "Professionalism"). In a retro: a low safety score is not repaired inside one meeting; the lead can protect what trust exists by not undermining it in the room.

8.  **Self-induced friction is the part the team owns.** The doctrine gives five examples of what can cause self-induced friction: *"lack of a clearly defined goal, lack of coordination, unclear or complicated plans, complex task organizations or command relationships, or complicated technologies"* [V]. *"While we should attempt to minimize self-induced friction, the greater requirement is to fight effectively despite the existence of friction"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 1, "Friction"). In a retro: the five examples make a sorting prompt for deltas, separating what the team caused from what came at it from outside.

9.  **The main effort: one thing most critical now.** Of all the actions under way, one is recognised *"as the most critical to success at that moment"* [V] and *"receives priority for support of any kind"* [V]; faced with a decision, the rest of the command asks, *"How can I best support the main effort?"* [V] Choosing it means *"In effect, we have decided: This is how I will achieve a decision; everything else is secondary"* [V]. The choice *"involves a physical and moral commitment, although not an irretrievable one"* [V], and *"when shifting the main effort, we seek to exploit success rather than reinforce failure"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 4, "Main effort"). In a retro: one experiment is the main effort; the others support it or wait.

10.  **Task plus intent; the intent outlasts the task.** *"The task describes the action to be taken while the intent describes the purpose of the action"* [V]. *"Of the two, the intent is predominant. While a situation may change, making the task obsolete, the intent is more lasting and continues to guide our actions"* [V]. Intent can often be captured in an "in order to" phrase following the task [AP], and the statement should be brief: *"the more concise, the better"* [V]. *"The burden of understanding falls on senior and subordinate alike"* [V], and *"A subordinate commander who is not given a clear purpose for the assigned mission should ask for one"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 4, "Commander's intent"). In a retro: each experiment carries its purpose, so the owner can adapt when the task stops fitting the Sprint.

11.  **Decide on awareness, not habit.** *"We should base our decisions on awareness rather than on mechanical habit. That is, we act on a keen appreciation for the essential factors that make each situation unique instead of from conditioned response"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 4, "Decisionmaking"). In a retro: an experiment the team proposes every time, whatever happened, is a conditioned response; ask what in this Sprint calls for it.

12.  **Guidance that needs judgment, not procedure.** *"Our doctrine does not consist of procedures to be applied in specific situations so much as it sets forth general guidance that requires judgment in application"* [V] (Source: U.S. Marine Corps, *MCDP 1*, Ch 3, "Doctrine"). In a retro: use MCDP 1 for stances (why review, what climate, how to focus), and take the facilitation steps from the corpus's method sources.

### Phase 0.5: Experiment Review

| Need | Question | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Was the last experiment tried or not started? | Did we try it and it failed, or did nobody start it? The first is a bold error to learn from; the second is inaction, and we look at why it happened. | Ch 3, "Professionalism" |
| Did the experiment carry a purpose? | Did the experiment say what it was *for*, in an "in order to" form, or only what to do? If the purpose was missing, did anyone ask for one? | Ch 4, "Commander's intent" |

## What to Look For

| Signal | Diagnosis | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| The team proposes skipping or shortening the retro because the Sprint went well | Review is being treated as a response to failure | MCDP 1 holds that *"critical self-analysis, even after success, is essential to improvement"* [V] (Ch 3, "Training"). Keep the retro; spend part of it on why the good results happened. |
| The board holds only small, safe mistakes, or none | The tolerance signal from the person with authority is missing or has been contradicted | Ask the lead or manager to state tolerance for admitted mistakes before data gathering; the team's willingness to admit mistakes *"depends on the commander's willingness to tolerate them"* [V] (Ch 3, "Training"). |
| A failed experiment is being discussed as a failure of the person who ran it | A bold error is being treated as a fault | Name the doctrine's line: *"there must be no 'zero defects' mentality"* [V], and boldness is not stifled *"through the threat of punishment"* [V] (Ch 3, "Professionalism"). Then review why the experiment produced what it did. |
| "We were waiting for sign-off" recurs as the reason something didn't happen | Inaction for lack of orders; the purpose may not have been clear enough to act on | Ask what the team knew of the purpose, and whether anyone asked for it. ## When to Use This Reference

Reach for this distillation when:
- The lead needs a principled reason to review after a good Sprint, or to hold the retro promptly: the critique passage covers both (Ch 3, "Training").
- The team will not admit mistakes and a manager or senior person is present: the tolerance line (Ch 3, "Training") names where candour comes from.
- The experiment review has to separate a failed experiment from one nobody started: the leniency and severity pairing (Ch 3, "Professionalism") gives the two categories, and the critique's focus on why keeps the review from turning into blame.
- Quick agreement needs testing before commitment: the honest-dissent passage (Ch 3, "Professionalism").
- The board carries too many experiments or experiments with no stated purpose: main effort and commander's intent (Ch 4).

This source is a stance, not a method. It does not supply the retro's agenda, safety check, data-gathering structure, voting or confidentiality rule; those come from the Field Guide, Liberating Structures, the Open Practice Library and FLO. For blame language it complements, and does not replace, the NHS default-to-system prior and the LFUO counterfactual prohibition.

## Worked Example

A six-person platform team closes a two-week Sprint. Last retro produced two experiments: run database migrations with a new rollback-first approach, and pair on every code review. The migration experiment was tried and broke staging for most of a day. The pairing experiment was never started. The engineering manager has joined the call, and the opening safety check comes back with three 2s.

**Phase 0.** Before the retro, the lead briefs the manager on one line from MCDP 1: the team's *"willingness to admit mistakes depends on the commander's willingness to tolerate them"* [V] (Ch 3, "Training"). At the opening the manager says that the staging outage came from trying something the team agreed to try, and that the retro will discuss it, not punish it.

**Phase 0.5.** The lead sorts the two experiments. The migration experiment was tried and failed: the kind of error MCDP 1 treats leniently, because *"there must be no 'zero defects' mentality"* [V], which also does not mean the mistake goes undiscussed (Ch 3, "Professionalism"). The pairing experiment was not started: inaction, the category the doctrine deals with severely (Ch 3, "Professionalism"). The lead keeps the seriousness and drops the sanction: the question is why, not who. The team finds that the pairing experiment named no owner and no purpose, so nobody could tell whether a busy week was a reason to skip it.

**Phase 3.** One engineer says, "If Sam had checked the rollback script, staging would have been fine." The lead steers to the critique's focus: *"not so much on the actions we took as on why we took those actions and why they brought the results they did"* [V] (Ch 3, "Training"). The discussion turns to why the rollback script looked safe: it had passed on a smaller dataset, and nobody on the team knew staging held a copy of production-sized data.

**Phase 4.** Five candidate experiments are on the board. The lead uses the main effort:

> Of all the actions going on within our command, we recognize one as the most critical to success at that moment. The unit assigned responsibility for accomplishing this key mission is designated as the main effort — the focal point upon which converges the combat power of the force. The main effort receives priority for support of any kind.
> [V] (Ch 4, "Main effort")

The team picks one main effort and states it with its purpose: "Rehearse each migration on a disposable production-sized copy before it touches shared staging, in order to find data-volume failures before they block the team" (the "in order to" form is from Ch 4, "Commander's intent"). One other item stays because it supports the main effort: a volunteer documents how staging's data differs from the test data, so the rehearsal copy matches. The other three wait. Before the vote the lead asks who disagrees; one engineer says the rehearsal will cost a day per migration. The team keeps the experiment and adds a review point after two migrations. Everyone agrees to back it as their own (Ch 3, "Professionalism").

In this scenario the next safety check comes back higher; that outcome belongs to the scenario, not to anything MCDP 1 demonstrates. The review timing, tolerance line, leniency and inaction categories, focus on why, main effort and intent all trace to MCDP 1 (Ch 3, "Training"; Ch 3, "Professionalism"; Ch 4, "Main effort"; Ch 4, "Commander's intent"). The review point is borrowed from the Open Practice Library's Disagree and Commit, not from MCDP 1.

## Anti-patterns This Reference Helps Avoid

- **Reviewing only after failure.** Signal: "it went fine, let's skip it." MCDP 1 treats self-analysis *"even after success"* [V] as essential (Ch 3, "Training"); keep the retro and study the success.
- **Asking for mistakes without showing tolerance.** Signal: a thin or cosmetic delta list with a senior person present. The team's willingness to admit mistakes follows the tolerance of whoever holds authority (Ch 3, "Training"); get that stated before data gathering.
- **A "zero defects" retro.** Signal: every failed experiment is read as someone's failure, and the next proposals shrink. The doctrine rejects the "zero defects" mentality so as not to stifle boldness (Ch 3, "Professionalism"). Its limits hold too: mistakes are still discussed, and reckless acts are not licensed.
- **Turning the doctrine's severity into sanctions.** Signal: "why didn't you just do it?" aimed at a person. MCDP 1 is severe on errors of inaction (Ch 3, "Professionalism"); in a blameless retro the lead keeps inaction on the table as a topic, asks why it happened, and lets LFUO's counterfactual rule keep the question on why the call made sense to people at the time.
- **Quick agreement taken as commitment.** Signal: no objections, then no follow-through. Honest dissent is a duty before the decision, and support after it (Ch 3, "Professionalism"); ask for the dissent before the vote.
- **Five experiments, no main effort.** Signal: every item leaves the room with equal weight. Pick the one most critical now and make the rest support it or wait (Ch 4, "Main effort").
- **Experiments as bare tasks.** Signal: the owner abandons the experiment when the Sprint changes shape. Give each a purpose in "in order to" form so it outlasts the task (Ch 4, "Commander's intent").
- **Treating MCDP 1 as a retro procedure, or crediting it with retro vocabulary.** Signal: citing it for an agenda, a safety check or a technique. The document *"contains no specific techniques or procedures for conduct"* [V] (Foreword), and its word for its review practice is *critique*. Take mechanics from the method sources and use MCDP 1 for stance.

## Citation and Source-Integrity Notes

**Borrowed-through gaps.** MCDP 1's Notes cite Clausewitz's *On War* (15 notes), Sun Tzu's *Art of War*, John Boyd's lectures, William S. Lind's *Maneuver Warfare Handbook*, Gelernter's *Mirror Worlds*, Patton and others. None is held directly in this corpus. This distillation uses no borrowed passage; the nearest to its material is Clausewitz on ill-timed boldness, quoted at Notes 6 of Ch 3 [BT], which it leaves out. The Prime Directive in the Integration table is Norm Kerth's, borrowed-through the Field Guide [BT].

**Named limits of the source.** MCDP 1 is doctrine for war and for preparing for war. It *"contains no specific techniques or procedures for conduct"* [V] (Foreword), never uses the words *retrospective* or *after-action review*, and says nothing about software teams. Every retro application here is by analogy, and the mapping of *commander* to lead or manager and *subordinate* to team member is the distillation's. The critique passage concerns training, not combat; its leniency passage concerns errors by junior leaders stemming from overboldness, and it names limits (counselling continues; no licence for stupid or reckless acts). The source's tempo, maneuver and OODA-loop material concerns competing against an opponent and is not projected here.

**Evidence-marker continuity.** Every `[V]` quotation is copied from a `[V]` passage in the deep reference and matches the converted source word for word (line-break hyphens joined, curly quotes straightened); none of the quoted passages carries an OCR garble. The four blockquotes (critique, zero defects, honest dissent, main effort) are contiguous source text. The zero-defects blockquote joins three overlapping deep-ref `[V]` excerpts that are contiguous in the source (Ch 3, "Professionalism"). The two-way trust clause is paraphrased `[AP]` because the source line carries OCR garbles; the "in order to" phrasing is paraphrased `[AP]` because the source's example spans a page break. The inaction line is quoted only up to *"errors of inaction"*, with "or timidity" paraphrased, because the full clause crosses a page break in the converted text. Integration-row quotations carry the marker of their own deep references. Scenario details and outcomes in the worked example are the distillation's, not the doctrine's.
