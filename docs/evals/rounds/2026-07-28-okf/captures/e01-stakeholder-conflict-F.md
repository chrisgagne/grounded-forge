## Handling Conflict Between Two Stakeholders

Conflict between two stakeholders should be handled as a structured search for a workable joint outcome, not a contest in which one person must be defeated. The facilitator or decision owner should manage both the substantive disagreement and the relationship: open decision-making explicitly addresses competing needs and priorities, while a relational-contract approach frames terms as a joint problem-solving exercise built on transparency and trust. The discussion should therefore begin with a neutral tone, no blame, and a declared focus on solutions. (*Open Decision Framework*, `open-practice-library/068-open-decision-framework.md`; *Formal Relational Contract*, `open-practice-library/149-formal-relational-contract.md`; *An Elephant in the Room*, `open-practice-library/026-an-elephant-in-the-room.md`.)

Before bringing the stakeholders together, interview each one separately. Clarify the interview's purpose, how information will be shared, and the larger "why." Use open-ended, non-leading questions, active listening, and enough silence for reflection. Ask about desired outcomes, priorities, user needs, business goals, technical limitations, and concerns. This creates the secure atmosphere recommended for stakeholder interviews and collects each person's perspective without assuming that job title alone explains it. (*Stakeholders Interview*, `open-practice-library/194-stakeholders-interview.md`.)

Then convene a joint session with an impartial facilitator when the issue is sensitive. State the purpose in advance and agree ground rules covering confidentiality, respectful communication, equal opportunity to speak, and solution-focus. Invite both people to name the "elephant": what is being avoided, which tension is present, and what concern is difficult to voice. The facilitator should listen to each account, maintain an objective tone, and steer discussion toward underlying causes rather than judgment. (*An Elephant in the Room*, `open-practice-library/026-an-elephant-in-the-room.md`.)

Because agreements are living documents, stakeholders should define behaviours, make the agreement visible, hold each other accountable, and revisit it when circumstances change; vague promises such as "be open" should be made specific. (*Social Contract*, `open-practice-library/089-social-contract.md`.)

Next, build a common decision frame. Put the problem, immovable constraints, timeframe, scope, and desired outcome where both can see them. Surface aspirations, individual goals, concerns, and the joint goal; then identify guiding principles such as reciprocity, autonomy, honesty, loyalty, equity, and integrity. Transparent framing gives both stakeholders the same decision context and makes trade-offs discussable. (*Open Decision Framework*, `open-practice-library/068-open-decision-framework.md`; *Formal Relational Contract*, `open-practice-library/149-formal-relational-contract.md`.)

Generate several options before choosing. Test each with curious questions: "What has the rest of us overlooked?" and "What risks do you see?" Productive dissent requires positive, objective responses and curiosity from everyone rather than compulsion. Record feedback, investigate the options, show what changed because of that feedback, and explain why other suggestions were not feasible or used. (*Dissent Cards*, `open-practice-library/228-dissent-cards.md`; *Open Decision Framework*, `open-practice-library/068-open-decision-framework.md`.)

If full agreement remains unavailable, do not force consensus. Choose a bounded experiment that both can commit to, specify the evidence to collect, and set an agreed review point. Keep the delivery period short and divide a large problem into smaller tests so disagreement is temporary and course correction can rest on evidence. Finally, document responsibilities, timelines, and follow-up meetings; for an ongoing relationship, establish governance and regularly revisit goals, concerns, processes, and joint outcomes. (*Disagree and Commit*, `open-practice-library/227-disagree-and-commit.md`; *An Elephant in the Room*, `open-practice-library/026-an-elephant-in-the-room.md`; *Formal Relational Contract*, `open-practice-library/149-formal-relational-contract.md`.)
