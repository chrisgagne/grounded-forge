The central move is to stop treating tomorrow as a verdict on whether the CTO's original choice was "right." Treat it as a forward-looking, nonroutine decision: which available path now offers the best expected organizational outcome, given current evidence, future costs, execution capacity, and uncertainty? Novel, consequential decisions call for deliberate analysis rather than emotion-driven reactive judgment (`openstax-organizational-behavior/references/managerial_decision_types.md`).

A practical synthesis of the bundle is:

- Extend only if there is a plausible, testable route from corrective actions to improved outcomes, with named owners, measures, targets, review dates, and exit conditions.
- Terminate and re-tender if that route is not credible, or if the future differential costs and opportunity costs of extension exceed the alternative.
- In neither case should money, effort, reputation, or political capital already spent count in the economic comparison: those are sunk costs (`openstax-accounting-vol2/references/relevant_costs.md`).

## Questions to ask yourself before the meeting

| Diagnostic question | What it protects against and where it comes from |
|---|---|
| **What outcome are we actually buying?** Separate vendor inputs and activities from immediate deliverables, medium-term outcomes, and strategic impact. Ask whether the original causal assumptions still hold. A theory of change is explicitly a theory to test and refine, not a representation of reality (`barbrook-johnson-systems-mapping/references/theory_of_change_results_chain.md`). |
| **What does "underdelivering" mean operationally?** Identify the target, actual result, measurement definition, data source, reporting period, and owner. Include financial, internal-process, customer, and learning/capability effects so that apparent improvement in one area does not conceal damage elsewhere (`openstax-accounting-vol2/references/balanced_scorecard_perspectives.md`). |
| **Which statements are facts, hypotheses, interpretations, or forecasts?** Ask whether measures are reliable and valid, whether observed relationships are merely correlations, and what evidence would falsify the preferred explanation. This directly counters confirmation and observer bias (`openstax-psychology-2e/references/foundations_and_research_glossary.md`). |
| **What evidence would make me change my mind?** Ask this separately of extension advocates, termination advocates, and yourself. Confirmation bias is the tendency to disregard disconfirming evidence; a hypothesis should be falsifiable (`foundations_and_research_glossary.md`). If nobody can name reversal evidence, the positions are identities rather than testable judgments. |
| **Are we confusing the first or most salient explanation with the whole problem?** Reconstruct the timeline and examine requirements, vendor capability, internal staffing, interfaces, incentives, governance, and decision latency. Combine quantitative records with interviews and stakeholder knowledge; every evidence type has systematic gaps, so triangulation is preferable (`barbrook-johnson-systems-mapping/references/system_map_evidence_types.md`). |
| **Why did the actions make sense at the time?** Compare the CTO's, engineers', vendor's, and operational stakeholders' perspectives without selecting a single "winning" retrospective account. The learning-analysis method warns against hindsight judgments, premature causal labels, counterfactual claims, and recommendations derived from a single outcome (`lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024/references/facilitated_learning_analysis.md`). |
| **What future costs and benefits actually differ?** Include renewal fees, internal remediation effort, transition and procurement effort, service interruption, avoidable costs, and the benefit forgone by rejecting the next-best option. Exclude already-incurred expenditure. Include qualitative factors such as morale, reliability, customer relationships, quality, and brand effects (`openstax-accounting-vol2/references/relevant_costs.md`). |
| **Which assumptions drive the answer?** Model coherent extension and re-tender scenarios, then vary the most uncertain assumptions to see what changes the decision. Scenario and sensitivity analysis are specifically intended to expose the estimates that have the greatest effect (`openstax-principles-finance/references/financial_forecasting.md`). |
| **Are there genuinely only two alternatives?** A nonprogrammed decision process requires generating multiple alternatives before analyzing and selecting one (`managerial_decision_types.md`). Even if the formal resolution is binary, implementation variants might include an unchanged extension, a conditional extension with remediation milestones, an extension solely as a transition bridge, or immediate termination. Whether those are contractually available is not established by this bundle. |
| **Who gains, loses, supports, resists, and holds power under each path?** Consider the board, CTO, engineering, procurement, customers, vendor, and operational owners. Map their interests and the legitimate, expert, reward, and coercive power they can exercise (`openstax-principles-management/references/stakeholders.md`; `openstax-organizational-behavior/references/organizational_power_bases.md`). |
| **Can engineering disagree without career consequences?** Private frustration paired with public agreement may represent conformity, obedience, groupthink, or normative pressure rather than genuine agreement (`openstax-psychology-2e/references/social_work_stress_and_health_glossary.md`). Legitimate and reward power often produce compliance rather than commitment; coercive power tends toward resistance (`organizational_power_bases.md`). |
| **Am I personalizing a system problem?** Fundamental attribution error overweights personal characteristics and underweights situational forces; self-serving bias attributes success internally and failure externally (`social_work_stress_and_health_glossary.md`). Ask both "What did the vendor or sponsor control?" and "What organizational conditions shaped the result?" |
| **Who recommends, who decides, and who oversees?** The board oversees management and broad policy; management executes strategy and day-to-day operations. Independent oversight and transparent reporting are governance controls (`openstax-principles-finance/references/corporate_governance.md`). Make the decision rights explicit before debate starts. |
| **Who will own implementation?** Teams need specific goals, complementary skills, an agreed way of working, and mutual accountability (`openstax-organizational-behavior/references/team_development_stages.md`). People who participate in developing a choice are more likely to own it; attempting to obtain "buy-in" after others have already decided is a warning that implementers were excluded (`liberating-structures-handbook/references/ownership_and_buy_in.md`). |

## A meeting structure that reduces distortion

1. **Use a facilitator and establish rules:** curiosity, brevity, open-mindedness, permission to disagree, and focus on evidence rather than advocacy. These are the dialogue agreements in `liberating-structures-handbook/references/conversation_cafe_dialogue.md`; facilitated collective sensemaking comes from `facilitated_learning_analysis.md`.

2. **Begin with silent individual writing.** Ask everyone to record: their current recommendation; the strongest evidence against it; the most important unresolved uncertainty. Silent reflection allows people to form an initial view before faster or more powerful speakers shape discussion (`liberating-structures-handbook/references/one_minute_of_silence.md`). Because anchoring means fixating on one aspect of a problem, have the CTO and other senior figures disclose their recommendation after the initial round (`openstax-psychology-2e/references/learning_cognition_and_memory_glossary.md`).

3. **Give each participant an uninterrupted first round.** Small-group or sequenced rounds create more speaking opportunity than immediate whole-room debate (`liberating-structures-handbook/references/one_two_four_whole_group.md`; `conversation_cafe_dialogue.md`). Explicitly make disagreement permissible regardless of rank (`tc-25-20-army-aar/references/after_action_reviews.md`).

4. **Reconstruct before judging.** Put the original objectives, key events, actual results, and standards on one timeline. Mark each statement as observation, interpretation, assumption, or forecast. The AAR method compares actual performance with intended outcomes and standards and organizes observations chronologically (`after_action_reviews.md`).

5. **Generate alternatives before arguing for one.** Then compare them using the same criteria: expected outcomes, differential future costs, uncertainty, qualitative effects, implementation requirements, and stakeholder impacts (`managerial_decision_types.md`; `relevant_costs.md`).

6. **Close with an executable record.** Document the decision, assumptions, unresolved dissent, measures, targets, data sources, responsible owners, deadlines, review point, and conditions that would trigger reconsideration. Named responsibility and deadlines mirror the action-plan discipline in `lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024/references/learning_review.md`; measure definition and ownership come from `balanced_scorecard_perspectives.md`.

## Failure modes to watch during the meeting

- **A referendum on the CTO.** Cognitive dissonance can make evidence threatening when it conflicts with behavior, beliefs, and a positive self-image. Fundamental attribution error can also turn a system discussion into a character judgment (`social_work_stress_and_health_glossary.md`). Redirect from "Who was wrong?" to "Which conditions and assumptions produced the result?"

- **Sunk-cost arguments.** "We have invested too much to stop," "termination would admit failure," and "we need six more months to prove the original decision" are not forward-looking economic reasons. Already-incurred costs are irrelevant to the difference between alternatives (`relevant_costs.md`).

- **Public unanimity masking private dissent.** Groupthink, conformity, obedience, and normative social influence can all create apparent consensus (`social_work_stress_and_health_glossary.md`). Ask for disagreement explicitly and preserve minority reasoning rather than forcing it into a consensus statement (`after_action_reviews.md`; `facilitated_learning_analysis.md`).

- **Polarization after advocates take sides.** Group discussion can strengthen the group's initial attitude (`social_work_stress_and_health_glossary.md`). Reframe the tension as a genuine paradox—for example, "How do we preserve continuity while refusing to normalize underperformance?"—rather than treating one concern as illegitimate (`liberating-structures-handbook/references/wicked_questions.md`).

- **Reactive urgency.** High emotion can make quick, intuitive processing unreliable; novel and consequential choices call for reflective processing (`managerial_decision_types.md`). A deadline is a reason for structure, not a reason to skip it.

- **Metric warfare.** A favorable variance is not automatically good, and an unfavorable one is not automatically harmful; metrics must be interpreted alongside quality, capacity, and operating causes (`openstax-accounting-vol2/references/standard_cost_variances.md`). Excessive scoring can also suppress candor and turn learning into grading (`after_action_reviews.md`).

- **Premature single-cause stories.** Stakeholder belief, interview evidence, quantitative data, and existing analysis each have limitations; association is not automatically causal (`system_map_evidence_types.md`). Retain genuine differences in perspective rather than declaring one narrative correct too early (`facilitated_learning_analysis.md`).

- **Participation theatre.** If engineering is invited only to endorse a decision already developed by executives, that is buy-in rather than ownership (`ownership_and_buy_in.md`).

- **A decision without a change system.** Termination may require technological, structural, cultural, group, and individual changes; planned change can also produce unplanned effects, and higher-level change normally needs supporting lower-level changes (`openstax-organizational-behavior/references/organizational_change_taxonomy.md`).

## What to watch after the decision

Regardless of the outcome:

- **Stable but revisable assumptions.** Track the assumptions behind the decision and explain any subsequent changes. A theory of change should be tested and refined, while scenario analysis should show which assumptions drive results (`theory_of_change_results_chain.md`; `financial_forecasting.md`).

- **Balanced results rather than a single headline.** Monitor financial results, internal processes, customer effects, and learning/capability. Watch for apparent success in one dimension that damages another (`balanced_scorecard_perspectives.md`).

- **Compliance versus commitment.** Polite agreement followed by slow execution, workarounds, or renewed private complaints suggests that formal authority produced compliance but not commitment (`organizational_power_bases.md`). Examine whether implementers actually participated in designing the plan (`ownership_and_buy_in.md`).

- **Targets drifting after bad news.** Changing measures or targets may be legitimate when conditions change, but changes should be explicit and documented; otherwise they can prevent disconfirming evidence from registering. This combines the scorecard requirement to define targets, sources, periods, and owners with the warning about confirmation bias (`balanced_scorecard_perspectives.md`; `foundations_and_research_glossary.md`).

- **Blame replacing learning.** Individual accountability should mean providing an honest account and participating in repair; organizational accountability should examine tools, procedures, incentives, and other system conditions (`lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024/references/just_culture.md`).

- **No follow-through.** Conduct a scheduled review comparing actual performance with the original intent and standards, identify strengths and corrective actions, and change procedures or plans where required. The value of an AAR comes from applying its results (`after_action_reviews.md`).

If you extend, specifically watch whether promised activities generate deliverables and whether those deliverables produce the intended business outcomes—not merely more activity. Recalculate the next renewal from future differential and opportunity costs rather than allowing the additional six months to become a new sunk-cost argument (`theory_of_change_results_chain.md`; `relevant_costs.md`).

If you terminate, watch for a comforting narrative that the vendor—or the CTO—was the sole cause. Preserve the internal lessons, test proposed replacement arrangements with the people closest to the work, and monitor structural, technological, cultural, and unplanned transition effects (`learning_review.md`; `organizational_change_taxonomy.md`; `just_culture.md`).

The bundle does not establish contract-law rights, procurement lead times, market benchmarks, or whether this particular vendor can recover. Those need case-specific commercial, legal, and technical evidence. Also, none of the concept files cited above carries `[V]`, `[AP]`, `[AR]`, `[AE]`, or `[BT]` markers, so no such marker has been omitted.
