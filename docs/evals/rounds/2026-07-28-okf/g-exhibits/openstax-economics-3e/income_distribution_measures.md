# Poverty measures

- **Poverty line:** the income level deemed necessary for a basic standard of living.
- **Poverty rate:** the percentage of the population whose income falls below the poverty line.

$$
Poverty\ rate =
\frac{Population\ below\ poverty\ line}{Total\ population} \times 100
$$

The poverty rate depends on the chosen threshold, so comparisons should state the poverty-line definition, population, and period.

# Quintiles

A quintile divides a ranked population into fifths. Income-distribution tables commonly report the share of total income received by each 20% group, from the lowest-income quintile to the highest-income quintile.

# Lorenz curve

A Lorenz curve plots:

- cumulative share of the population on the horizontal axis, ordered from lowest to highest income; and
- cumulative share of total income received on the vertical axis.

The 45-degree line represents perfect equality, where each cumulative population share receives the same cumulative income share. A Lorenz curve farther below that line represents greater inequality.

# Income and wealth

Income is a flow received over a period, such as a month or year. Wealth is a stock equal to the value of assets—including financial holdings, pensions, and housing—at a point in time. Measures of income inequality and wealth inequality therefore answer different questions and should not be interchanged.
