# Definition

The Daily Scrum, also known as the standup, is the Development Team’s daily event for inspecting progress toward the Sprint Goal, adapting the Sprint Backlog, and planning work for the next 24 hours.

# Time-box

- Hold it every day at the same time and place.
- Keep the event to 15 minutes or less.
- Participants may remain afterward for optional detailed discussion, blocker resolution, or replanning.

# Participants

- **Required:** the entire Development Team.
- **Strongly suggested:** the Product Owner and Scrum Master.
- **Invited:** stakeholders and subject-matter experts may observe or support the team but must not interfere with its process.

# Inputs

- The Sprint Goal.
- The Sprint Backlog.
- Optionally, a forecasting aid such as a burn-down chart.

# Output

- A plan for the next 24 hours and an updated Sprint Backlog when adaptation is needed.

# Sample 15-minute agenda

1. For about 13 minutes, each Development Team member addresses:
   - What did I do yesterday that helped the Development Team meet the Sprint Goal?
   - What will I do today to help the Development Team meet the Sprint Goal?
   - Do I see an impediment that prevents me or the Development Team from meeting the Sprint Goal?
2. For about two minutes, inspect and update the Sprint Backlog and any burn-down chart.
3. After the event, anyone interested may remain for optional “Parking Lot” topics.

# Operating guidance

- Move discussions that threaten the time-box to the optional Parking Lot.
- Use the event for Development Team coordination, not as a status report to the Product Owner, Scrum Master, or stakeholders.
- The exact format may vary provided the event stays within 15 minutes and still supports inspection and planning.
- The Scrum Master ensures the event occurs smoothly and within its time-box but need not always facilitate it.
