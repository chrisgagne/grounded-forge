# Definition

A Sprint is a fixed iteration in which the Scrum Team delivers a usable, potentially releasable product Increment that meets the Definition of “Done” and accomplishes the Sprint Goal.

# Time-box

- Run the Sprint for one to four weeks, with one month as the maximum.
- Start each Sprint immediately after the preceding Sprint ends.
- Do not extend the Sprint duration.

# Participants

- **Required:** the self-organizing Scrum Team: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and cross-functional Development Team.
- **Invited:** business people, stakeholders, and subject-matter experts may participate in Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, and Sprint Review as appropriate.

# Inputs

- Enough prioritized Product Backlog items that are “Ready” to work on.

# Outputs

- A usable, potentially releasable product Increment.
- Increased useful knowledge about the product, customers, competition, and Scrum Team.

# Operating guidance

- Clarify and renegotiate scope as the team learns, while preserving the Sprint Goal whenever possible.
- Maintain a sustainable pace and do not neglect product quality.
- The Product Owner may cancel a Sprint when the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete, usually in consultation with the Scrum Master and Development Team; cancellation should be uncommon because it is disruptive and consumes resources.

# Typical two-week cadence

- Sprint Planning: about three hours.
- Daily Scrum: no more than 15 minutes each day.
- Backlog Refinement: up to 10% of the Sprint.
- Sprint Review: about one hour.
- Sprint Retrospective: about 1½ hours.
- Optional unstructured time after the retrospective to connect and celebrate as a team.
