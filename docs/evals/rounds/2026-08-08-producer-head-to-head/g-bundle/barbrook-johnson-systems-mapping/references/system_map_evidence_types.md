# Evidence-source categories

- **Participatory-process information:** Stakeholder knowledge, beliefs, assumptions, and discussion captured while participants build or use a map together. It can surface mental models and disagreement even where formal data is sparse.
- **Qualitative data:** Textual material from sources such as interviews or focus groups that a researcher translates into factors and causal connections. It can be rich and broad but places substantial interpretive responsibility on the researcher.
- **Existing evidence:** Completed studies or analyses, including peer-reviewed research and suitable grey literature, selected through explicit inclusion criteria and translated into supported nodes and edges.
- **Quantitative data:** Numerical variables and estimated statistical or causal relationships used to define nodes and connections. Connections should be described with only the causal strength supported by the analysis; an association is not automatically a cause.

# Coverage and combination

These categories overlap: participatory processes can generate qualitative data, and existing evidence can be based on qualitative or quantitative data.

Every source can leave systematic gaps. Quantitative data and existing studies may omit difficult-to-measure social or behavioural factors; qualitative and participatory sources require interpretation and may contain disagreement. Combining sources can expose omissions, support triangulation, and refine a map, provided the claims made from the map remain appropriate to the information used.
