# FLO Peer Feedback Criteria

The FLO MicroCourse Peer Feedback Rubric defines six criteria and three performance levels for assessing feedback on a peer's draft or prototype.

| Criterion | Sophisticated | Competent | Marginal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | Focuses on observation. | Includes observation and inference. | Uses broad statements that could apply to any project. |
| Specific | Refers to actual elements in the project. | Refers to the project, but the elements connected to the feedback are unclear. | Does not refer to project elements, suggesting the work was not thoroughly reviewed. |
| Respectful | Acknowledges strengths or potential. | Offers advice without identifying promising elements. | Emphasizes inadequacies. |
| Useful | Explains the rationale for a critique and may offer suggestions. | Identifies areas for improvement but gives limited explanation of why. | Stops at a judgment such as “I like your idea.” |
| Engaging | Uses a genuinely inquisitive tone to invite further dialogue. | Asks questions to prompt further discussion. | Does not elaborate beyond statements about the project. |
| Realistic | Considers both the project's scope and the learner's expected skill level. | Demonstrates an understanding of the work involved. | Suggests changes that may not be feasible. |

Effective peer feedback is grounded in observable details, tied to the actual work, framed with respect, actionable within the project's constraints, and open to continued conversation.
