A software platform brings together third-party producers and consumers and creates value by facilitating their interactions. It is therefore a two-sided market rather than simply a product assembled through a controlled value chain.

The source distinguishes several related roles:

- **Platform owner:** governs the ecosystem, establishes interfaces, and seeks to maximize the total value extracted from the ecosystem.
- **Third-party producer:** creates applications, enhancements, or add-ons that attract consumers and fill niches the platform owner may not serve.
- **Consumer:** uses the products or services made available through the platform.
- **Aggregator:** collects and makes items available but does not necessarily create a producer-consumer interaction market.
- **Middleware:** software intended to reduce the cost of porting applications among operating systems, thereby lowering switching costs.

A platform may subsidize one participant group to attract another. Its community and participant-contributed resources are difficult-to-copy assets, so ecosystem governance, stable interfaces, and third-party confidence are central to building it. Platform owners may operate application stores or approval processes to influence the quality consumers experience.

One practical distinction between an aggregator and a platform is the “Bill Gates line”: a platform exists when the economic value of everyone who uses it exceeds the value of the company that created it.
