# Definition

A Facilitated Learning Analysis (FLA) is a scalable process for making sense of an unintended or surprising outcome and developing lessons for the broader organization. It uses a factual story to convey the context and conditions that shaped participants’ in-the-moment sensemaking, decisions, and actions.

An FLA is learning-focused, does not assign blame, and ordinarily produces lessons rather than formal recommendations. If the Delegating Official requires formal recommendations, the [Learning Review](learning_review.md) process is the appropriate extension.

# When to use an FLA

Consider an FLA when the circumstances or outcome have implications for a broader audience and offer substantial organizational-learning value. The process can be used for accidents, incidents, close calls, escaped prescribed fires, interagency events, property damage, rare events, and positive or otherwise surprising outcomes.

Selection should not depend only on the severity of the realized outcome. A near miss separated from a fatal outcome by chance may provide a richer learning opportunity than its reporting category suggests.

An FLA may be inappropriate when litigation against an employee or the agency is likely, there is evidence of reckless and willful disregard for human safety, the Delegating Official will not disseminate the lessons in a public learning product, or punishment is the intended response.

# Scale

- **Basic FLA:** May be led by a few people, completed within about a week, and focused on the lessons learned by those involved.
- **Complex FLA:** May involve roughly 5–15 people, subject-matter experts, specialists, a Lessons Learned Analysis, and weeks or months of work to develop organizational insight and a learning product.

The team structure is flexible and scalable. See the [FLA team roles reference](fla_team_roles.md) for the functions that may be assigned.

# Required commitments and boundaries

- A signed delegation from the Delegating Official must state that information discovered or revealed through the review will not result in agency administrative or punitive action.
- Participants must understand that the Delegating Official cannot grant immunity from external legal, regulatory, or other authorities.
- The FLA must remain insulated from concurrent administrative, law-enforcement, claims, or regulatory investigations.
- Team deliberations, participant communications, and draft products are kept confidential to the extent possible.
- Physical evidence requested by authorized investigators may be shared through the Delegating Official, but FLA interviews and deliberations are not repurposed for an internal punitive process.
- The team avoids causal labels, counterfactual claims, hindsight judgments, and recommendations based on a single outcome.

# Core activities

1. The Delegating Official selects the learning process, cares for affected employees, assigns liaisons, forms the team, and issues the delegation.
2. The unit and team establish common objectives, expectations, confidentiality boundaries, logistics, and a shared understanding of the process.
3. The team gathers perspectives through site visits, individual interviews, and a facilitated group dialogue.
4. Participants reconstruct the event, compare different perspectives, and explore why decisions and actions made sense in context.
5. For a complex FLA, the team may conduct a confidential Lessons Learned Analysis to examine key decisions, actions, beliefs, perceptions, expectations, and performance-shaping conditions.
6. The team develops a factual, contextual story and validates it through readbacks with the people involved before wider review.
7. The approved learning product is distributed to the intended audience.

# Facilitated group dialogue

The central FLA activity is a dialogue in which one facilitator helps people directly involved in the event think together about what happened and what can be learned. Learning for future events takes priority over assessing past blame; respectful discussion, vulnerability, uncertainty, and multiple perspectives are expected.

The facilitator looks for points where participants’ accounts diverge. These differences are not resolved by choosing a winning account; they are used to reveal perspective, context, and new understanding. The goal is collective sensemaking rather than a group interview.

# Learning product

The product should convey a compelling and factually accurate event story, including context, conditions, sensory detail, multiple perspectives, and the sensemaking behind decisions. It may be a written report, video, story map, podcast, or another format suited to the audience.

Before release, key participants should hear the story read aloud and have the opportunity to correct their own account and clarify important details. Genuine differences in memory or perspective should be represented without judgment.

# Termination

If the team discovers a serious criminal act or reckless and willful disregard for human safety, the Team Leader may terminate the FLA and advise the Delegating Official to consider an administrative or law-enforcement investigation. The team releases physical evidence through the appropriate channel, secures confidential interview and team material, stops further FLA work, and does not disclose what it learned through the review.
