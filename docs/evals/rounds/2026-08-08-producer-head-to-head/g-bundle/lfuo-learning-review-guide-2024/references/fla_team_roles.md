# Team model

A [Facilitated Learning Analysis](facilitated_learning_analysis.md) team is a flexible, scalable response organization. One or two people may cover several functions in a basic review; a complex event may require individual people for specialized roles. The Delegating Official should remain flexible and add, merge, or release roles as the work develops.

# Minimum team attributes

Every team needs:

- a basic understanding of Just Culture;
- a basic understanding of the FLA process, with formal training or experienced coaching for team leaders and facilitators;
- a strong reputation for handling confidential matters; and
- awareness of local or personal bias, particularly when members are drawn from the affected unit.

Selection of the Team Leader and Lead Facilitator should account for their ability to step back from the event and avoid imposing a personal view.

# Core roles

| Role | Function |
|---|---|
| FLA Process Coach | Provides experienced procedural guidance, a sounding board for complex or controversial issues, help locating specialists, and an outside review of whether the learning product is coherent to someone who did not conduct the interviews. The role may operate remotely. |
| Learning Review Process Coach | Joins when the FLA will transition to a Learning Review; supports all phases, helps locate specialists, and co-leads the subsequent review work with the FLA Team Leader. |
| Team Leader | Keeps the team on task and on schedule, mediates between the team and Delegating Official, receives the delegation of authority, and is accountable for the quality and content of the FLA. |
| Lead Facilitator | Required on every FLA; serves as the process expert, elicits the conditions and influences behind decisions and actions, identifies systemic weaknesses through inquiry, and leads interviews, dialogue, reconstruction, and sensemaking as appropriate. |
| Storyteller or Writer-Editor | Converts the event and its perspectives into an accurate, clear, compelling nonfiction story or multimedia product that supports organizational learning. |
| Subject-Matter Expert or Peer | Brings current, firsthand knowledge of work comparable to that performed by the people involved so the team can understand operational context. A peer may also fill another team role. |
| Technical Specialist | Contributes expertise in the activity, equipment, human performance, medicine, mapping, engineering, or another specialty relevant to the event and proposed learning. |
| Union Representative | Advises on labor and representation issues, ensures employees can access representation during interviews, coordinates with local union representatives, and may participate as a full team member in a complex FLA. |
| Interagency Representative | Represents another involved agency and may serve as a peer, subject-matter expert, or other team member under applicable interagency arrangements. |
| Documentation Specialist | Organizes and tracks documentation and supports complex learning products; particularly valuable when substantial records, private-property damage, personal injury, claims, or litigation may make the FLA the government’s principal accident record. |
| Liaison | Supports affected employees, explains the FLA process, provides current information, preserves critical contacts, and assists the team with local logistics, travel, and coordination. More than one liaison may be needed. |

# Specialist triggers

The team should match specialist support to the event rather than assume a fixed composition. The guide highlights:

- equipment experts for equipment performance and evaluation;
- human-performance specialists for complex FLAs;
- mapping, LIDAR, videography, graphics, or animation expertise for site reconstruction and learning products;
- personal protective equipment expertise for burnovers or fire-shelter deployments;
- fire-lab expertise when escape routes or safety zones are central to an entrapment;
- national program expertise for tree-falling or chainsaw accidents; and
- agency medical leadership when medical response or treatment is under review.

# Scaling considerations

Team size and composition should reflect the surprise and learning potential of the event, the depth of analysis desired, interagency participation, conflicting perspectives, technical complexity, the planned learning product, and whether a subsequent Learning Review will require formal recommendations.
