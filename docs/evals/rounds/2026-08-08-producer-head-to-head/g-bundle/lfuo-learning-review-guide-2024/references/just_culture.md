# Definition

Just Culture is an organizational model characterized by trust, fairness, and dignity. It provides an open and safe reporting environment in which people can speak candidly, individuals give honest accounts of what happened, and the organization is accountable for the systems it designs and for learning from an incident.

Its accountability is forward-looking: the purpose of an account is to reveal system strengths and weaknesses, learn from the conditions that influenced an outcome, and improve the probability of future success—not to correct history through blame.

# Core commitments

- Treat information and learning as the lifeblood of safety.
- Begin from the premise that trained and qualified employees are well-intentioned and act according to how they understand the situation at the time.
- Recognize that workers inherit the organization’s tools, training, procedures, incentives, and safety-versus-production values.
- Direct system-level accountability toward the organization rather than treating an individual as the sole unit of analysis.
- Make room for hard truths, mistakes, close calls, risky decisions, and the trade-offs people make between safety and production or between efficiency and thoroughness.
- Preserve trust so employees remain willing to share how work is actually performed and how risk is perceived, negotiated, and managed.

# Accountability

In this model, individual accountability means giving an honest account of the event and participating in learning and repair. Organizational accountability means examining the conditions that influenced the outcome and making improvements to the overall system.

Attempting to hold an individual responsible for system-level weaknesses constrains information flow and undermines learning. A mature Just Culture instead uses each review to invest in trust and transparency and treats employees fairly and with dignity.

# Restorative justice

Restorative justice is the repair-oriented component of Just Culture. After a serious event, people may be emotionally harmed and relationships may be strained or broken. The parties discuss how they were affected, identify needs and obligations, and participate in repairing trust, relationships, and injuries.

# Application to learning reviews

The [Facilitated Learning Analysis](facilitated_learning_analysis.md) puts Just Culture into practice by separating learning from punishment, avoiding blame and shame, and requiring a signed delegation that assures participants that information disclosed to the review team will not result in agency administrative action.

That assurance does not create immunity from actions by outside legal or regulatory authorities. If a review uncovers a serious crime or reckless and willful disregard for human safety, the learning process may be terminated and a different process initiated while the confidentiality of interview material remains protected.
