# Tool selection

The guide describes a flexible and scalable set of responses for intended, unintended, and surprising outcomes. The desired audience, depth of learning, need for a shareable product, and need for formal recommendations distinguish the tools.

| Tool | Primary use | Typical reach | Product and recommendation characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| After-Action Review (AAR) | Capture immediate learning within the involved group. | Individual, crew, team, or local work unit. | Usually closed, personal, and confidential; learning generally remains local and no broader organizational product is required. |
| Rapid Lessons Sharing (RLS) | Share a lesson from a near miss, accident, new tool, technique, process, protocol, success, or more efficient practice. | Anyone who may benefit beyond the originating unit. | Commonly a one- or two-page narrative with concrete, actionable lessons, photographs, and optional supporting media or links. |
| [Facilitated Learning Analysis](facilitated_learning_analysis.md) (FLA) | Make sense of an outcome with broader organizational-learning value. | Broader organization. | Requires a signed assurance of no agency punitive action; uses factual storytelling and normally produces lessons rather than formal recommendations. |
| [Learning Review](learning_review.md) (LR) | Examine the wider system of work and develop formal recommendations. | Field communities, organizational leaders, and the systems they influence. | Extends the FLA with focus groups, additional subject-matter or academic perspectives, a Learning Review Board, vetted recommendations, and a Safety Action Plan. |

# Choosing the depth of response

Consider:

- how deeply the organization needs to examine the event;
- whether learning is local or has broader organizational implications;
- whether the event presents rich learning despite a minor realized outcome;
- the level of trust available to support candid participation;
- the need for specialist or interagency perspectives;
- the time and resources available;
- conflicting accounts or significant differences in perspective;
- the cost of failing to capture the learning opportunity; and
- whether formal recommendations are essential.

The severity of the outcome should not be the sole basis for scale. Chance may separate a minor incident from a catastrophic one, while the conditions and learning potential remain the same.

# Recommendation boundary

An FLA captures lessons and seeks to improve understanding without issuing top-down recommendations from a single event. If the Delegating Official requires formal recommendations, the process must transition to a Learning Review so recommendations can be examined from multiple perspectives, vetted with affected communities of practice, approved by a board, and assigned through a Safety Action Plan.

# Related response frameworks

- **Coordinated Response Protocol (CRP):** A Forest Service management framework for coordinating internal and external responses to a serious accident. A CRP may include an FLA or Learning Review.
- **Serious Accident Investigation (SAI):** The Department of the Interior process for serious accidents or fatalities. The guide distinguishes its root-cause approach from the FLA and Learning Review emphasis on context, conditions, and networked causality.

In interagency events, applicable memoranda of understanding may prescribe investigative requirements or team composition. Selecting an FLA does not replace ordinary accident-reporting requirements.
