# Definition

A Learning Review (LR) is a process that builds on a [Facilitated Learning Analysis](facilitated_learning_analysis.md) to examine the wider system of work, incorporate additional perspectives, and develop formal recommendations. The defining difference is that the Delegating Official has requested recommendations as an output and is willing to take ownership of recommendations approved by a Learning Review Board.

The review asks not only how a particular event happened, but how it fits within the work system: whether the event was anomalous, whether its conditions have broader implications, whether policy is keeping pace with work, and whether organizational messages or goals conflict.

# Analytical approach

The Learning Review combines:

- **Analysis** for objective evidence about mechanical, ergonomic, or structural failures. This engineering-oriented work can yield specific answers and barriers intended to prevent an event or mitigate its consequences.
- **Synthesis** for understanding emergent properties and the relationships among parts of a complex social system.
- **Sensemaking** for deciding when to examine components and when to examine relationships, and for making visible the interactions, conditions, and circumstances that shaped assessments and actions.

Barriers can be useful in simple and complicated systems but can also add complexity. Proposed barriers and recommendations should therefore be vetted with the people closest to the affected work.

# Initiation

A Learning Review may be planned when the initial FLA team is formed or initiated after a complex FLA concludes. If an FLA is completed first, the team briefs the Delegating Official, who decides whether to publish the FLA as a standalone product or invest in the deeper review needed to produce recommendations.

For the Forest Service process described by the guide, the Delegating Official requests initiation through the Risk Management Council. A Learning Review Process Coach works with the FLA team and supports the subsequent phases.

# Team and perspectives

At minimum, the Learning Review team includes members of the FLA team and a process coach. Its size and specialist composition depend on the event.

The process adds perspectives through:

- subject-matter experts from relevant communities of practice;
- one or more focus groups that examine the FLA story and the system of work;
- academic or professional specialists who can contribute an external lens; and
- FLA team members who retain the event context needed for sensemaking.

Focus groups may identify additional areas of inquiry, connect decision and action points, test proposed recommendations, and determine whether recommendations are feasible and likely to improve work-system design.

# Core artifacts and flow

1. Complete or review the complex FLA narrative.
2. Map the conditions and influences surrounding the event, including their relationships.
3. Use communities of practice, focus groups, and outside specialists to add perspectives and make sense of the work system.
4. Develop and vet proposed recommendations with the people and communities affected by them.
5. Prepare the complex narrative, network of influences map, sensemaking material, learning products, and proposed recommendations for a Learning Review Board.
6. Convene the board for review, feedback, and approval.
7. Convert approved recommendations into a Safety Action Plan that assigns each action to a responsible person or body and establishes a completion deadline.

# Learning Review Board

A Learning Review Board is required whenever formal recommendations are an intended outcome. The Delegating Official or designee chairs the board and may include trusted staff, peers, and relevant outside experts.

The board reviews the narrative, influence map, learning products, and proposed recommendations. Once approved, learning products are released through the appropriate channel and the Delegating Official owns follow-through on the Safety Action Plan.

# Required products

| Product | Audience and purpose |
|---|---|
| Organizational Learning Product | Helps leaders and policy makers understand organizational, structural, systemic, and cultural contributors, including gaps between work as imagined and work as performed. |
| Field Learning Product | Helps field personnel experience and make sense of the event through narrative, dialogue, scenarios, simulations, tabletop exercises, multimedia, or other formats suited to operational learning. |
| Safety Action Plan | Records approved recommendations, responsible owners, and completion deadlines. |

Organizational and field learning may themselves be legitimate corrective actions. A field product may be the only recommendation needed when learning and dialogue best support prevention, resilience, and safer work.

# Review and regulatory coordination

Before the Learning Review Board meets, the final draft should receive sufficient Freedom of Information Act review so the approved product can be released without redaction where possible.

If an oversight regulator requires a causal statement, the statement is prepared at the end of the Learning Review through a separate collaboration among risk, review, safety, community-of-practice, team, and technical representatives. This requirement does not change the review’s broader emphasis on context, systems, and networked influences.
