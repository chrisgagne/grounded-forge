# Accountability and justice

- **Accountability:** Giving an account from which people can learn, looking toward the future, and identifying what needs to be done and who should do it. In a restorative sense, it includes asking who was harmed, what they need, whose obligation it is to meet those needs, and how people involved can participate in repair.
- **Accountabilism:** A slang term for a review that claims to learn from participants’ accounts but later punishes them through judgmental language, required training, or other organizational actions.
- **Culpability:** Responsibility for wrongdoing or failure, especially moral, legal, or criminal responsibility.
- **[Just Culture](just_culture.md):** An organization with an open and safe reporting system where everyone can speak and the organization is accountable for the systems it designs and its analysis of an incident rather than placing accountability solely on an individual.
- **Restorative justice:** An element of Just Culture that seeks to restore the people involved and heal damaged relationships and injuries in the wider community after an ethical breach.
- **Retributive justice:** A post-event approach focused on determining how to treat individuals who breached a standard of conduct and on their errors or violations.
- **Punitive actions:** Actions intended to punish, including demotion, suspension, transfer, or required training.

# Review processes and roles

- **After-Action Review (AAR):** A predominantly closed and confidential process for capturing immediate learning within the involved group.
- **Coordinated Response Protocol (CRP):** The Forest Service process for coordinating the internal and external entities that require access to employees after a serious accident or fatality.
- **Delegating Official:** A person with organizational authority to request a review or investigation and delegate its intent.
- **[Facilitated Learning Analysis](facilitated_learning_analysis.md) (FLA):** A scalable Forest Service learning process for unintended outcomes that uses factual storytelling and a signed delegation assuring participants that agency punitive actions will not result from what they disclose.
- **Facilitated Group Dialogue:** The FLA activity in which a facilitator helps a group think together about an event, talk through what happened, and identify what can be learned.
- **Focus group:** A group drawn from relevant communities of practice, and sometimes outside disciplines, that reviews the FLA story, identifies learning, and may create or vet recommendations.
- **Investigation:** A formal or systematic examination or inquiry; within LFUO, it is framed as a way to process information and learn.
- **[Learning Review](learning_review.md) (LR):** A process that builds on the FLA, adds subject-matter and external perspectives, examines the wider work system, and produces vetted recommendations when requested by the Delegating Official.
- **Learning Review Board (LRB):** The board convened to consider Learning Review products and proposed recommendations; it is chaired by the Delegating Official or designee.
- **Readback:** The FLA validation step in which a story is read to a participant for feedback on the accuracy and validity of that participant’s own account.
- **Rapid Lessons Sharing (RLS):** An extension of an AAR that produces a short, shareable learning product about a near miss, accident, new tool, technique, process, protocol, or other useful lesson.
- **Recommendation:** A suggestion or proposal for a course of action.
- **Serious Accident Investigation (SAI):** The Department of the Interior process for investigating serious accidents or fatalities.

# Learning and communication

- **Dialogue:** Communication based on listening without imposing intent, bias, or a preconceived position; an open exchange of perspectives intended to produce new shared understanding.
- **Discussion:** Talking about a subject to exchange ideas or reach a decision.
- **Listening:** In LFUO, deep attention to another person’s words, body language, inflection, tone, and meaning without distraction.
- **Organizational learning:** Learning that occurs as people share experiences and as leaders examine patterns across events and adapt equipment, policy, training, or the work system.
- **Sensemaking:** Creating situational awareness and understanding in conditions of complexity or uncertainty so decisions can be made.
- **Storytelling:** Sharing the knowledge and wisdom participants gained from an unintended outcome through a factual account with enough context and sensory detail for an audience to comprehend the event.

# Systems and human performance

- **Bias:** A prejudice for or against a thing, person, or group, often operating unfairly and sometimes outside conscious awareness.
- **Conditions:** Environmental, social, cultural, political, and other factors that affected relevant decisions and actions during an event.
- **Doctrine:** Principles developed from law, culture, history, and precedent that express an organization’s fundamental framework and guide planning and operations.
- **Efficiency-Thoroughness Trade-Off (ETTO):** The recurring choice between efficiency and thoroughness when both cannot be maximized: productivity pressure tends to reduce thoroughness, while safety pressure tends to reduce efficiency.
- **Gap:** The difference between what administrators believe is happening and how work is actually being performed in the field.
- **High Reliability Organization (HRO):** An organization that seeks to minimize catastrophic events through preoccupation with failure, reluctance to simplify, sensitivity to operations, commitment to resilience, and deference to expertise.
- **Networked causality:** The view that outcomes in complex systems emerge from a dynamic network of influencing conditions rather than a one-to-one cause-and-effect chain or a single root cause.
- **Recognition-primed decision making:** A model in which experience enables a person to generate a plausible action as the first option considered.
- **Root cause analysis:** A family of approaches for identifying the highest-level issue that initiates a cause-and-effect chain; the guide treats it as better suited to simple or complicated systems than to complex social events.
- **Safety-versus-production:** The dynamic tension between accomplishing work and keeping people free from harm or injury; it is closely related to the efficiency-thoroughness trade-off.
- **Systems Thinking:** Making sense of complexity by examining wholes and relationships rather than reducing the world to isolated parts, enabling effective action and system change in complex contexts.
