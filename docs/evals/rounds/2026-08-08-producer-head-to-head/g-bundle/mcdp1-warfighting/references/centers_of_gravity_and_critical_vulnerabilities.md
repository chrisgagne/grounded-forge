A **center of gravity** is an important source of strength. It may be an intangible characteristic such as resolve or morale, a capability such as armored or aviation forces, a locality such as decisive terrain, or a relationship such as cooperation between arms, allied partners, or converging forces.

A **critical vulnerability** is a weakness that, if exploited, will do the most significant damage to an enemy's ability to resist. It is not merely any weakness; it is one whose exploitation materially contributes to the enemy's downfall.

The concepts are complementary:

- Center-of-gravity analysis asks which sources of enemy capability are indispensable and which friendly sources of strength must be protected.
- Critical-vulnerability analysis asks where and when the enemy can be attacked with greatest effect.
- A critical vulnerability provides a pathway for attacking a center of gravity without confronting strength directly.

An enemy system may not have a single decisive center of gravity, and an existing center may be well protected. It may therefore be necessary to attack several lesser centers or vulnerabilities simultaneously or in sequence.
