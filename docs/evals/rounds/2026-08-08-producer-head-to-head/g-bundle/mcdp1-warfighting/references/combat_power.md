Combat power is the total destructive force that can be brought to bear on an enemy at a given time. It is a unique, temporary, and situation-dependent product of physical, moral, and mental factors rather than a fixed quantity.

Its components may include:

- Tangible factors such as numbers and materiel.
- Context-dependent effects such as maneuver, tempo, surprise, geography, climate, the relative strengths of offense and defense, and the direction of attack.
- Intangible factors such as morale, fighting spirit, perseverance, and leadership.

Speed and focus have universal significance in generating combat power. Speed permits a force to seize the initiative, create surprise, maneuver, and concentrate strength at the decisive time and place. Focus converges effects in time and space on an objective, creating local superiority while requiring economy and the acceptance of risk elsewhere.
