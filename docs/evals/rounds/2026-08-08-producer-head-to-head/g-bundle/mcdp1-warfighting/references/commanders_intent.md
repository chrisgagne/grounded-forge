Commander's intent is the purpose behind an assigned task. It gives subordinates the larger context needed to exercise judgment and initiative, including when unforeseen events make the original task or plan obsolete.

Every mission has two parts:

- The **task** states what is to be done and may also state when and where.
- The **intent** explains why the action is required.

Intent is predominant because it is more enduring than the task. A commander normally includes intent in the mission assigned to a subordinate, and each subordinate mission's intent should contribute to the intent received from above. This top-down flow establishes the context for bottom-up initiative and preserves unity of effort.

Intent should be brief, clear, and compelling. It can often be expressed as a task followed by an “in order to” purpose, such as: “Control the bridge in order to prevent the enemy from escaping across the river.” Subordinates should understand the intent of their own commander and of the commander at least two levels higher.
