Friction is the force that resists all action and saps energy in war. It makes the simple difficult and the difficult seem impossible.

Friction can be:

- **Mental**, such as indecision over a course of action.
- **Physical**, such as effective enemy fire or a terrain obstacle.
- **External**, imposed by the enemy, terrain, weather, or chance.
- **Self-induced**, caused by unclear goals, poor coordination, complicated plans, complex command relationships, or complicated technology.

Because war is a human enterprise, friction always has psychological as well as physical effects. Forces should minimize self-induced friction, but the greater requirement is to fight effectively despite friction through strength of will, realistic experience, and persistent action. At the same time, they should increase enemy friction until it weakens the enemy's ability to fight.
