Activities in war occur at three interrelated levels:

- **Strategic:** Activities focus directly on policy objectives. National strategy coordinates all elements of national power, while military strategy applies military force to secure policy objectives. Military strategy is subordinate to national strategy and can be understood as the art of winning wars and securing peace.
- **Operational:** Activities link tactical results to strategic objectives. This level determines when, where, and under what conditions to engage or refuse battle in support of higher aims. It is the art and science of winning campaigns.
- **Tactical:** Concepts and methods are used to accomplish a particular mission. In combat, tactics applies combat power to defeat an enemy force at a particular time and place. It is the art and science of winning engagements and battles.

The levels form a hierarchy but are not rigidly separated. Their boundaries may overlap, and in nuclear war or military operations other than war, a single commander or even a small-unit leader may operate at two or three levels simultaneously.
