Maneuver warfare is a warfighting philosophy that seeks to shatter the enemy's cohesion through rapid, focused, and unexpected actions that create a turbulent and rapidly deteriorating situation with which the enemy cannot cope.

Maneuver is not limited to physical movement for positional advantage. It is action that creates and exploits an advantage over the enemy, whether spatial, temporal, psychological, or technological. Maneuver in time is especially important: a force gains advantage by operating at a tempo the enemy cannot match.

Rather than incrementally destroying every part of the enemy force, maneuver warfare seeks to penetrate the enemy system and disrupt its moral, mental, and physical cohesion. Selective attrition and concentrated firepower remain essential when they help produce systemic disruption or destroy enemy elements at decisive points.

Its practical demands include:

- Seizing and maintaining the initiative through speed.
- Focusing combat power against critical vulnerabilities.
- Using surprise, ambiguity, stealth, and deception.
- Exploiting advantage relentlessly before the enemy can recover.
- Developing leaders able to act with initiative, boldness, judgment, flexibility, and responsibility amid uncertainty and disorder.

The aim is decisive effect against the enemy at the least possible cost to the friendly force.
