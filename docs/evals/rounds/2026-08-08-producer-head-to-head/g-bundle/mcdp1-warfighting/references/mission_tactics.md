Mission tactics is the practice of assigning a subordinate a mission without specifying how it must be accomplished. The subordinate receives the freedom—and the duty—to take whatever steps the situation requires, exercising initiative within the senior commander's guidance and intent.

The senior commander:

- Provides the support required to accomplish the mission.
- Prescribes execution only as much as coordination requires.
- Supplies clear intent and intervenes in execution only by exception.

The subordinate:

- Acts responsibly and loyally within the proper limits of authority.
- Adapts action to changing conditions without waiting for permission.
- Understands how local action contributes to the higher mission.
- Reports what has been done.

Mission tactics enables decentralized command, preserves senior attention for higher-level concerns, and supports a high tempo of operations. Unity comes from harmonious initiative, lateral coordination, and common guidance rather than detailed imposed control.
