**Surfaces** are enemy strengths or hard spots. **Gaps** are enemy weaknesses or soft spots. Maneuver seeks to avoid surfaces and focus against gaps so that friendly strength is applied against enemy weakness.

A gap may be:

- A physical opening in the enemy's dispositions.
- A moment when the enemy is overexposed or unprepared.
- A seam in an air-defense umbrella.
- A unit vulnerable in unsuitable terrain.
- A boundary between two units.

A surface may be a strongpoint, a moment of replenished readiness, or the technological superiority of a particular weapon or capability. Whether a condition is a surface or gap depends on the friendly force: terrain that obstructs armor may permit infantry infiltration.

Gaps are usually fleeting. Forces must find them through continuous, aggressive reconnaissance and exploit them with flexibility and speed. If a unit other than the current main effort finds a gap, the commander can redesignate the main effort and pull combat power through the opening.
