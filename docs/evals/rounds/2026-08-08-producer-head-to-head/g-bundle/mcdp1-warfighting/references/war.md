War is a violent clash of interests between or among organized groups characterized by the use of military force. The groups may be nation-states, international coalitions, or factions within or outside an existing state that have political interests and can generate organized violence on a politically significant scale.

Its essence is a struggle between hostile, independent, and irreconcilable wills. Each side tries to impose its will while resisting and adapting to the other, making war an interactive social process rather than action against an inanimate object.

The object of war is to impose one's will on the enemy. Its means are the organized application or credible threat of violence by military force. The violence may be limited to hostile combatants or extend more broadly, and conflict may range from subtle unconventional hostilities to intense clashes between large forces.

War is also an extension of policy and politics with the addition of military force. Military action must serve policy, while leaders must recognize that war is an animate force whose course and unintended consequences can alter the political situation.
