# Scope

Use the guide to support a conversation about whether a staff member involved in a patient safety incident needs specific individual support or intervention to work safely. Apply it when an incident investigation suggests concern about an individual action; do not automatically apply it to every staff action associated with an incident.

The guide:

- does not replace a full patient safety incident investigation;
- does not replace HR advice and must be used with organisational policy;
- may be used at any point in an investigation and revisited when new information becomes available; and
- evaluates only one action or failure to act at a time, so separate actions must be considered separately.

# Sequential tests

Apply the tests in order and stop when a branch reaches a recommendation.

1. **Deliberate harm test:** Was there any intention to cause harm?
   - **Yes:** outcome A.
   - **No:** continue to the health test.
2. **Health test:** Are there indications of substance abuse, physical ill health, or mental ill health?
   - **Substance abuse:** outcome B.
   - **Physical or mental ill health:** outcome C.
   - **No indication of any of these:** continue to the foresight test.
3. **Foresight test:** Were applicable protocols or accepted practices in place, workable and routinely used, and did the individual knowingly depart from them?
   - **Yes to all three conditions:** continue to the substitution test.
   - **No to any condition:** outcome D.
4. **Substitution test:** Would comparable peers behave similarly in the same circumstances, was the individual omitted from relevant peer-group training, or did senior team members fail to provide normally expected supervision?
   - **Yes to any condition:** outcome E.
   - **No to all three conditions:** continue to mitigating circumstances.
5. **Mitigating circumstances:** Were there any significant mitigating circumstances?
   - **Yes:** outcome F.
   - **No:** outcome G.

# Outcome reference

| Outcome | Trigger | Recommended response |
|---|---|---|
| **A — deliberate-harm response** | An intention to cause harm is identified. | Follow organisational guidance for appropriate management action. This may include contacting regulators, suspending staff, referring the matter to police, and using disciplinary processes. Investigate more widely to understand how and why patients were not protected. |
| **B — substance-abuse response** | There are indications of substance abuse. | Follow organisational substance-abuse-at-work guidance. Investigate more widely to determine whether the issue could have been recognised and addressed earlier. |
| **C — health-issue response** | There are indications of physical or mental ill health. | Follow organisational guidance for health issues affecting work, likely including occupational health referral. Investigate more widely to determine whether the issue could have been recognised and addressed earlier. |
| **D — individual action unlikely to be appropriate (foresight branch)** | An applicable protocol or accepted practice was absent, was not workable or routinely used, or was not knowingly departed from. | Action singling out the individual is unlikely to be appropriate. Use the incident investigation to identify the wider actions needed to improve safety; those actions may include, but are not limited to, the individual. |
| **E — individual action unlikely to be appropriate (substitution branch)** | Comparable peers might act similarly, the individual missed relevant peer-group training, or expected senior supervision was not provided. | Action singling out the individual is unlikely to be appropriate. Use the incident investigation to identify the wider actions needed to improve safety; those actions may include, but are not limited to, the individual. |
| **F — mitigating circumstances apply** | Significant mitigating circumstances are present. | Action directed at the individual may not be appropriate. Follow organisational guidance, likely including senior HR advice about the degree of mitigation, and use the investigation to identify wider safety actions. |
| **G — management action plus wider investigation** | No significant mitigating circumstances are identified after all preceding tests have been passed. | Follow organisational guidance for appropriate management action. This may include training, performance management, competency assessment, role changes, increased supervision, regulatory contact, suspension, or disciplinary processes. Use the investigation to identify wider safety actions. |

# Interpretation

An individual-focused response does not close the patient safety investigation. Every outcome that calls for individual support, intervention, or management action still requires examination of the wider conditions that allowed the incident to occur. Conversely, an outcome that makes individual action unlikely to be appropriate does not prevent the investigation from identifying improvements that involve the individual alongside broader system changes.
