# Business entity concept

The business entity concept separates an organization from its owner or owners: only business transactions, not the personal transactions of owners, employees, or other connected parties, belong in the organization's accounting records.

# Legal structure categories

| Structure | Definition | Typical characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | A business owned by one individual. | Relatively easy to form, generally receives single-level tax treatment, gives the owner substantial control, and exposes the owner to unlimited personal liability. Its life and access to capital are usually limited. |
| Partnership | An association of two or more people who contribute money, property, or services and operate as co-owners. | Generally receives single-level tax treatment and combines access to capital and expertise, but ordinary partners face unlimited personal liability and must formalize matters such as authority and profit sharing. Limited-liability forms can mitigate liability risk. |
| Corporation | A legal entity distinct from its one or more owners. | Owners generally have limited liability, ownership interests can be transferred, the entity can continue beyond an owner's life, and it can raise capital through borrowing or stock. A corporation and its owners may both be taxed when earnings are distributed. |

# Equity terminology

| Accounting event or balance | Sole proprietorship or partnership | Corporation |
|---|---|---|
| Owner investment | Capital | Common stock |
| Distribution to owners | Drawing or withdrawal | Dividend |
| Residual ownership interest | Owner's or partners' capital | Stockholders' equity |
| Undistributed cumulative income | Included in capital | Retained earnings |

The underlying accounting does not change with legal form, but the account names used to describe ownership do.
