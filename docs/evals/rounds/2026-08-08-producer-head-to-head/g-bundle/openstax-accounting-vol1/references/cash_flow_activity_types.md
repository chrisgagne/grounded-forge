The statement of cash flows classifies cash receipts and disbursements into three activity types.

# Activity classifications

| Activity type | Definition | Typical cash inflows | Typical cash outflows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating | Day-to-day activities used to produce net income. | Collections from customers; interest and dividend receipts. | Inventory purchases; salaries, utilities, interest, and income taxes. |
| Investing | Acquisition or disposal of long-term assets and investments. | Proceeds from selling land, buildings, equipment, or other investment assets. | Purchases of land, buildings, equipment, or other investment assets. |
| Financing | Transactions that raise or retire debt or equity financing. | Issuing notes, bonds, or capital stock. | Repaying note or bond principal, paying dividends, or purchasing treasury stock. |

# Classification cues

- Operating cash flows primarily relate to income-producing operations.
- Investing cash flows are often associated with changes in long-term asset accounts.
- Financing cash flows are often associated with changes in long-term liabilities and equity.

Transactions that exchange assets, debt, or equity without cash are not cash flows; material noncash investing and financing activities are reported separately rather than included as cash receipts or disbursements.
