A chart of accounts is the numbered list of accounts a business uses for day-to-day transactions. It organizes the accounts maintained in the general ledger and normally follows financial-statement order: balance-sheet accounts first, then income-statement accounts.

# Numbering convention

| Account category | Leading digit | Small-company range | Large-company range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Assets | 1 | 100–199 | 1000–1999 |
| Liabilities | 2 | 200–299 | 2000–2999 |
| Stockholders' equity | 3 | 300–399 | 3000–3999 |
| Revenues | 4 | 400–499 | 4000–4999 |
| Expenses | 5 | 500–599 | 5000–5999 |

Larger merchandising and manufacturing organizations may add categories beginning with `6` and higher.

# Scope

The exact account list is tailored to the entity's business and expected transactions. Common accounts include Cash, Accounts Receivable, Supplies, Accounts Payable, Unearned Revenue, Common Stock, Dividends, Revenue, and Expense accounts.
