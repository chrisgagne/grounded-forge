Financial statements communicate an organization's financial performance and financial position.

# Statement set

The four statements are prepared in this order:

1. **Income statement** — reports financial performance for a period. Revenues and gains are compared with expenses and losses to determine net income or net loss.
2. **Statement of owner's equity** — reports how equity changed during a period through owner investments, distributions, and net income or loss. For a corporation, the corresponding reports and account names use stockholders' equity and retained earnings.
3. **Balance sheet** — reports, at a specific date, what the organization owns, owes, and is worth: assets, liabilities, and equity.
4. **Statement of cash flows** — reports cash inflows and outflows for a period, often described as sources and uses of cash.

# Interrelationships

The statements form a linked set rather than four independent reports:

- Net income or loss from the income statement changes equity.
- Ending equity from the equity statement is reported on the balance sheet.
- The balance sheet's ending cash is explained by the period's cash inflows and outflows on the statement of cash flows.

The statements are period-based except for the balance sheet, which describes financial position at a point in time.
