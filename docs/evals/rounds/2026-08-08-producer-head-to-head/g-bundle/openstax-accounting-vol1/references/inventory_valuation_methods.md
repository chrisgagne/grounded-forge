Inventory costing allocates goods available for sale between cost of goods sold and ending inventory. Except for specific identification, the assumed flow of costs need not match the physical flow of goods.

# Cost-allocation methods

| Method | Cost-flow rule | Typical implication |
|---|---|---|
| Specific identification | Trace the actual cost of each individual item and assign that cost when the item is sold. | Suited to expensive, customized, or inherently distinctive items; cumbersome for large quantities of similar goods. |
| First-in, first-out (`FIFO`) | Treat the earliest acquired costs as the first costs assigned to goods sold. | The latest acquisition costs remain in ending inventory. |
| Last-in, first-out (`LIFO`) | Treat the latest acquired costs as the first costs assigned to goods sold. | The earliest acquisition costs remain in ending inventory. |
| Weighted average (`AVG`) | Divide the combined cost of units available by their total units and use the resulting average cost. | Under a perpetual system, recalculate the moving average after inventory changes. |

# Reporting conventions

- FIFO, weighted average, and specific identification are permitted under both US GAAP and IFRS when appropriate.
- IFRS does not permit LIFO.
- Once selected, a costing method is normally applied consistently across periods. A justified change requires disclosure of its effect.
- In the United States, an entity using LIFO for tax reporting must also use LIFO for financial-statement costing.
