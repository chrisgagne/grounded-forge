# Contribution Margin

Contribution margin is the portion of sales available to cover fixed costs and then profit.

```text
Unit contribution margin
  = Selling price per unit − Variable cost per unit

Contribution margin ratio
  = Unit contribution margin / Selling price per unit
  = Total contribution margin / Total sales

Total contribution margin
  = Total sales − Total variable costs
  = Unit contribution margin × Units sold
```

# Break-Even and Target Profit

At break-even, total revenue equals total cost and operating income is zero.

```text
Break-even units
  = Fixed costs / Unit contribution margin

Break-even sales dollars
  = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Units for a target profit
  = (Fixed costs + Target profit) / Unit contribution margin

Sales dollars for a target profit
  = (Fixed costs + Target profit) / Contribution margin ratio
```

For multiple products, use a composite unit based on the expected sales mix. A change in sales mix changes the weighted contribution margin and therefore changes the break-even point.

# Margin of Safety

Margin of safety measures how far expected or actual sales can fall before the business reaches break-even.

```text
Margin of safety
  = Actual or budgeted sales − Break-even sales

Margin of safety percentage
  = Margin of safety / Actual or budgeted sales
```

# Operating Leverage

Operating leverage measures the sensitivity of net operating income to a percentage change in sales.

```text
Degree of operating leverage
  = Total contribution margin / Net operating income

Expected percentage change in net operating income
  = Degree of operating leverage × Percentage change in sales
```

A cost structure with a larger fixed-cost share generally has higher operating leverage: profits rise faster after fixed costs are covered, but losses also deepen faster when sales decline.

# Core Assumptions

CVP analysis generally assumes that selling price per unit and variable cost per unit are constant, total fixed costs are constant within the relevant range, units produced equal units sold, and sales mix is stable when more than one product is involved.
