# Master Budget

A budget is a quantitative plan for expected resource receipts and uses. The **master budget** combines an operating budget with a financial budget and culminates in budgeted financial statements.

# Operating Budget

The operating budget plans revenue, production, and day-to-day expenses:

1. **Sales budget:** expected unit sales and selling prices; the starting point for the other operating budgets.
2. **Production budget:** units to produce after considering expected sales and desired beginning and ending inventory.
3. **Direct materials budget:** materials required for production and purchases needed after inventory policy.
4. **Direct labor budget:** labor hours and labor cost required for planned production.
5. **Manufacturing overhead budget:** variable and fixed manufacturing costs other than direct materials and direct labor.
6. **Selling and administrative expense budget:** expected nonmanufacturing variable and fixed expenses.
7. **Budgeted income statement:** expected revenue and expenses assembled from the operating schedules.

```text
Required production
  = Budgeted unit sales
  + Desired ending finished-goods inventory
  − Beginning finished-goods inventory

Required materials purchases
  = Materials needed for production
  + Desired ending materials inventory
  − Beginning materials inventory
```

# Financial Budget

The financial budget plans long-term assets, financing, and cash:

- **Capital asset budget:** planned investment in long-term assets.
- **Cash collections schedule:** timing of cash receipts from customers.
- **Cash payments schedule:** timing of cash payments, including payments for materials.
- **Cash budget:** beginning cash plus receipts, less payments, with financing added when the required minimum cash balance would otherwise not be met.
- **Budgeted balance sheet:** expected period-end assets, liabilities, and equity.

# Preparation Dependencies

The sales budget drives the production budget. Production drives direct materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead. Sales also drives expected collections and selling expenses. The operating schedules feed the budgeted income statement; collections, payments, operating cash flows, and capital spending feed the cash budget; the ending cash and other schedule balances feed the budgeted balance sheet.

# Budget Variants

- **Static budget:** prepared for one activity level.
- **Flexible budget:** restates expected revenue and costs for the actual or another activity level.
- **Rolling budget:** continuously adds a new future period as the current period ends.
- **Zero-based budget:** begins at zero and includes only amounts that can be justified.
- **Top-down budget:** senior management sets targets for lower levels.
- **Bottom-up or participative budget:** operating managers develop estimates that move upward for review.
