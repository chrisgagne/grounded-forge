# Job Order Costing

Job order costing is used when products or services are distinguishable and costs can be traced to individual jobs. A job cost sheet accumulates each job’s:

- direct materials, supported by materials requisition records;
- direct labor, supported by time tickets; and
- applied manufacturing overhead, based on a predetermined overhead rate.

Costs flow from raw materials to work in process, then to finished goods, and finally to cost of goods sold when the job is sold.

# Process Costing

Process costing is used for high-volume, standardized production when costs cannot be traced economically to individual units. Costs accumulate by processing department, and each department has its own work-in-process account.

Equivalent units restate partially completed production as the number of whole units that the work performed would represent. Materials and conversion costs can have different equivalent-unit totals because materials may be added at specific points while conversion normally occurs throughout processing.

```text
Equivalent units
  = Physical units × Percentage complete

Cost per equivalent unit
  = Costs to account for / Equivalent units of production
```

A production cost report reconciles physical units, equivalent units, costs to account for, cost per equivalent unit, and the allocation of costs between units transferred out and ending work in process.

# Inventory-Costing Conventions

Under **absorption costing**, both variable and fixed manufacturing costs are product costs:

```text
Absorption product cost
  = Direct materials
  + Direct labor
  + Variable manufacturing overhead
  + Fixed manufacturing overhead
```

Under **variable costing**, only variable manufacturing costs are product costs; fixed manufacturing overhead is expensed as a period cost:

```text
Variable product cost
  = Direct materials
  + Direct labor
  + Variable manufacturing overhead
```

When production exceeds sales, absorption costing generally reports higher operating income because some fixed manufacturing overhead remains in ending inventory. When sales exceed production, previously deferred fixed overhead is released from inventory and absorption-costing income is generally lower.
