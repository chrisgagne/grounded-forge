# Responsibility Accounting

Responsibility accounting assigns financial accountability for an organizational segment to a manager who has authority to affect that segment’s performance. The framework should support goal congruence between the manager, the segment, and the organization.

# Center Types

| Center | Manager accountable for | Typical evaluation focus |
|---|---|---|
| Cost center | Costs with a direct relationship to the center’s output | Cost containment, quality, and service |
| Discretionary cost center | Controllable costs where output has no well-defined cost relationship | Spending against an approved plan and service effectiveness |
| Revenue center | Revenue generation | Sales or revenue outcomes |
| Profit center | Both revenues and costs | Segment profit |
| Investment center | Profit and the capital invested in the segment | Return on investment, residual income, or economic value added |

# Controllability

- **Controllable costs:** costs the manager or segment can influence.
- **Uncontrollable costs:** costs the manager or segment has little or no short-term ability to influence.
- **Allocated costs:** centrally generated costs assigned to segments through an allocation formula; these are commonly uncontrollable by the receiving manager.

Managers should be evaluated on factors they control. A report can still show uncontrollable or allocated costs for organizational planning, but those amounts should be distinguished when assessing the manager’s performance.
