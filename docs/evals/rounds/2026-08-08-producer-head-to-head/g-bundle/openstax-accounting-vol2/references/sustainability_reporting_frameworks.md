# Materiality

Materiality determines whether an issue is significant enough to include in a report. The reporting frameworks described by the source all use materiality, but they differ in audience and scope.

# Global Reporting Initiative

The Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) is designed for sustainability reporting to a broad stakeholder audience. The source describes three universal standards:

- **Foundation:** reporting principles and requirements for preparing a report in accordance with GRI.
- **General disclosures:** contextual information about the organization and its reporting practices.
- **Management approach:** how the organization manages each material topic.

An organization identifies material economic, environmental, and social impacts, explains why each topic is material and where impacts occur, and selects relevant topic-specific standards.

# Sustainability Accounting Standards Board

The Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB) focuses on sustainability information material to investors and likely to affect financial performance. The source describes industry-specific standards and an investor-centered materiality test: whether omitted information would significantly alter the reasonable investor’s total mix of information.

# Integrated Reporting

Integrated Reporting connects strategy, financial performance, governance, and environmental and social information in a concise account of value creation over time. It organizes resources and relationships into six capitals:

1. financial;
2. manufactured;
3. intellectual;
4. human;
5. social and relationship; and
6. natural.

The materiality process identifies matters capable of affecting value creation, evaluates their magnitude and likelihood, prioritizes them, and determines what to disclose.

# Selection Lens

- Use GRI when the reporting objective emphasizes broad stakeholder impacts.
- Use SASB when the objective emphasizes financially material information for investors.
- Use Integrated Reporting when the objective is to connect multiple forms of capital, strategy, performance, and long-term value creation.

The approaches are not inherently exclusive; the decisive issues are the report’s audience, materiality definition, and intended decision use.
