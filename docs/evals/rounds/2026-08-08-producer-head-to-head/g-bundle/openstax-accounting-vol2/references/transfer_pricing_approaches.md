# Transfer Price

A transfer price records the internal sale of goods or services from one segment of an organization to another. The selling segment records revenue and the buying segment records cost, but the internal amounts eliminate in the organization’s consolidated results.

# Approaches

- **Market price:** uses the price charged to an outside customer. It preserves market-based profit-center signals and is especially relevant when the seller operates at capacity.
- **Variable-cost or full-cost price:** transfers at variable cost, full manufacturing cost, or one of those costs plus a markup. A cost-plus price is:

  ```text
  Transfer price = Cost base × (1 + Markup percentage)
  ```

- **Negotiated price:** the buying and selling segment managers agree on a price, often within the range bounded by the seller’s minimum and the buyer’s external purchase alternative.

# Capacity and Opportunity Cost

The seller’s minimum rational price includes any contribution margin lost by displacing an external sale:

```text
Minimum transfer price
  = Incremental cost per unit + Opportunity cost per unit
```

When the seller has excess capacity, the opportunity cost is normally zero and the minimum approaches incremental variable cost. At full capacity, the opportunity cost includes contribution margin forgone on displaced external sales.

The buyer’s maximum rational price is normally the amount it would pay an outside supplier for a comparable input.

# Decision Considerations

A transfer-pricing policy should promote goal congruence rather than competition between segments. Relevant considerations include product comparability, quality, delivery reliability, unused capacity, segment-manager incentives, and the organization’s total profit. Cross-border transfers also require attention to taxes, duties, regulation, currency, and exchange-rate effects.
