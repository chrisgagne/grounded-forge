# Corporation

A **corporation** is a business entity granted corporate status when a state issues it a charter of incorporation. Its legal structure separates ownership and control from personal liability for the business's debts and other financial obligations.

## Ownership and governance

Shareholders own shares in the corporation and elect its directors. The directors oversee the organization's direction and appoint officers to manage it.

## Limited liability

Corporate status ordinarily limits the losses of shareholders, directors, and managers if the business fails. For shareholders, the loss generally cannot exceed the amount paid for their shares. By reducing investors' personal financial exposure, limited liability can make it easier for a corporation to attract capital.

## Legal status and responsibility

**Corporate personhood** is the doctrine that a corporation, distinct from its owners and managers, has some of the legal rights and responsibilities of a natural person. Corporate law creates the entity and limits its owners' risk, giving rise to an ethical debate about whether a corporation owes duties only to shareholders or also to society and its other stakeholders.

The **business judgment rule** generally protects corporate officers, directors, and managers from liability for losses when their decisions were reasonable and made in good faith.
