# Shareholder

A **shareholder**, also called a **stockholder**, is an individual or institution that owns stock or shares in a corporation. A shareholder is one type of stakeholder, not a synonym for all stakeholders.

Shareholders generally expect a return on their investment. Under the traditional corporate model, a corporation earns revenue, deducts expenses, and may distribute profit to shareholders as dividends.

## Governance and liability

Shareholders elect the corporation's directors, who appoint its officers. Corporate status normally gives shareholders **limited liability**: if the business fails, their loss ordinarily cannot exceed the amount paid for their ownership shares.

## Shareholder interests and other stakeholders

**Shareholder primacy** is the view that a company has a duty to maximize profits for its stockholders. The text contrasts this view with stakeholder-oriented management and corporate social responsibility, which consider the effects of decisions on customers, employees, communities, the environment, government, and other stakeholders as well.

Minority shareholders in a large corporation may have little power to influence corporate choices. Available protections vary by state and may include cumulative voting or, for some closely held corporations, buyout or dissolution provisions.
