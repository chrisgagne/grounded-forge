Administrative law, also called regulatory or public law, governs administrative agencies. Agencies are created by federal or state government to carry out defined statutory purposes and protect the public in specialized areas.

# Authority

- **Organic statute:** The law that creates an agency and defines its purpose, structure, responsibilities, and authority.
- **Delegation:** Congress's assignment of authority to an agency to implement and administer legislation.
- **Rulemaking:** The agency's development of rules within its delegated authority.
- **Adjudication:** The agency's resolution of alleged rule violations and related disputes.

Administrative agencies can examine and investigate regulated entities. They remain constrained by the Constitution, their statutory authority, due process, civil liberties, and applicable procedural requirements.

# Administrative law judges

Administrative law judges oversee procedural matters, determine facts, interpret relevant rules and statutes, and decide whether agency rules were violated. Their jurisdiction is limited to the agency's field, but they may award money or benefits and impose authorized sanctions.

# Administrative Procedure Act

The Administrative Procedure Act establishes the federal system for agency rulemaking and adjudication. It requires agencies to follow specified procedures, act within statutory limits, base rules on facts and reason, respect constitutional rights, and provide an opportunity for public participation.

Examples of federal administrative agencies include the FDA, EPA, FTC, FEC, FBI, and NLRB.
