Alternative dispute resolution (ADR) comprises methods for resolving disputes with the intent to avoid litigation.

# Mechanisms

- **Negotiation:** A process in which parties with nonidentical preferences allocate resources through interpersonal activity and joint decision-making. It is commonly the first and simplest ADR step because it requires no outside party.
- **Mediation:** A solution-focused process in which an impartial mediator trained in conflict resolution helps the parties seek a voluntary agreement. Unlike a judge or arbitrator, the mediator facilitates rather than imposes the outcome.
- **Arbitration:** A private process in which the disputing parties authorize one or more neutral arbitrators to receive evidence, hear arguments, decide the dispute, and issue an award.

# Arbitration classifications

- **Voluntary** arbitration is chosen by the parties; **involuntary** arbitration is required by an outside authority or applicable law.
- In **binding** arbitration, the decision is final except in rare circumstances. A **non-binding** award is a recommendation that becomes final only if both parties accept it.
- Common business applications include labor disputes, business transactions, and property disputes.

# Practical distinction

Mediation preserves party control over the resolution. Arbitration shifts decisional authority to the arbitrator and more closely resembles a private adjudication.
