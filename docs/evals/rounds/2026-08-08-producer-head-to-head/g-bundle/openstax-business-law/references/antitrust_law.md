Antitrust laws regulate economic competition to maintain fair trade practices and prevent restraints such as price-fixing, market control, and anticompetitive mergers.

# Restraint classifications

- **Naked restraint:** A general, open-ended restraint of competition without limiting boundaries.
- **Ancillary restraint:** A restriction limited in time and geography and attached to a broader legitimate arrangement.
- **Rule of reason:** A judicial standard for evaluating the purpose and competitive effect of a restraint rather than treating every restraint as unlawful.

# Principal federal statutes

- **Sherman Antitrust Act:** A criminal statute prohibiting contracts and interactions that unreasonably restrain interstate or foreign trade. Price-fixing, bid-fixing, and customer allocation can produce criminal liability.
- **Clayton Act:** A civil statute addressing practices such as price discrimination, exclusive dealing, corporate mergers, and interlocking directorates when they create monopolies or substantially lessen competition.
- **Federal Trade Commission Act:** Prohibits unfair methods of competition and deceptive acts or practices affecting commerce. It reaches anticompetitive conduct not otherwise covered by federal antitrust law and is enforced by the FTC.

# Enforcement and exemptions

The FTC and U.S. Department of Justice enforce federal antitrust law, with remedies including divestiture or dissolution where authorized. Limited exemptions can apply to labor activity, agricultural and fisheries cooperatives, foreign-trade cooperation, and cooperative research and production, but the source conditions them on not restraining U.S. trade.
