# Core definition

White-collar crimes are crimes committed by business professionals and characterized by deceit, concealment, or violation of trust. They commonly involve fraud—an intentional misrepresentation of material facts for monetary gain—and do not depend on threats or physical violence.

# Common types

- **Pump-and-dump scheme:** Misrepresenting or manipulating a security to raise its price before selling the perpetrator's holdings into the market.
- **Ponzi scheme:** Paying promised returns to earlier investors with money obtained from newer investors rather than from investment performance.
- **Larceny:** Unlawfully taking another person's or business's personal property.
- **Embezzlement:** Converting or refusing to return an item of value that was entrusted to the offender.
- **Environmental crime:** A criminal violation of an environmental statute or regulation, including laws governing air, water, waste, and protected species.
- **Racketeering:** A pattern of activities such as loan-sharking, money laundering, or blackmail; federal RICO law applies to organized-crime syndicates and commercial enterprises.
- **Bribery:** Offering, giving, receiving, or soliciting money, goods, services, information, or another thing of value in exchange for favorable action.
- **Money laundering:** Passing proceeds of crime through apparently legitimate activity so the funds appear lawful and cannot readily be tied to the underlying offense.
- **Spam violations:** Sending unsolicited commercial email using false or misleading sender, origin, or subject information or without required commercial disclosures.

Business crimes may violate state law, federal law, or both, depending on the conduct and its connection to matters such as interstate commerce or the postal system.
