# Comparison

| Attribute | Criminal case | Civil case |
|---|---|---|
| Conduct addressed | Offenses against the public, society, or state | Injury to private rights, including contract, property, reputation, and personal harms |
| Initiating party | Federal or state government, acting as the prosecution | A private person, group, or business, acting as plaintiff or complainant |
| Accused party | Defendant | Defendant |
| Burden of proof | Beyond a reasonable doubt | Preponderance of the evidence, meaning more probable than not |
| Result terminology | Guilty or not guilty | Liable or not liable |
| Typical consequences | Incarceration and/or a fine paid to the government | Damages, restitution, or injunctive relief |
| Right to appointed counsel | Available to a qualifying defendant | Parties ordinarily secure their own representation |

# Components of a crime

Criminal conduct ordinarily requires proof of both:

- **Mens rea:** The criminal, guilty, or wrongful mental state specified by the statute.
- **Actus reus:** The criminal, guilty, or wrongful act specified by the statute.

A single course of conduct can create both criminal exposure and civil liability when the applicable requirements for each are satisfied.
