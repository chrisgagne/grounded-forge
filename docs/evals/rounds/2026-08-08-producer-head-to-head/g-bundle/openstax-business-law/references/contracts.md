A contract is an agreement between two or more parties that is enforceable by law.

# Elements of an enforceable contract

- **Offer and acceptance:** One party makes a definite proposal with intent to contract, and the other agrees to be bound by its terms.
- **Genuine agreement:** The parties reach a meeting of the minds that is not destroyed by fraud, misrepresentation, mistake, duress, or undue influence.
- **Consideration:** Each party promises money, property, a right, another promise, or another thing of value in exchange for value from the other.
- **Capacity:** The parties possess legal capacity to contract. Agreements made by minors, mentally incapacitated people, or sufficiently intoxicated people may be voidable.
- **Legality:** The agreement has a lawful purpose and does not violate public policy.

# Promissory estoppel

Promissory estoppel may provide equitable relief without consideration when a promise induces reasonable action, the relying party suffers a substantial and measurable detriment, and enforcing relief is necessary to avoid gross injustice.

# Breach and remedies

A breach occurs when a party fails without legal excuse to perform a contractual promise. A **material breach** substantially defeats the expected exchange and can excuse the non-breaching party's remaining performance; a **minor breach** leaves most of the promised benefit intact but may support damages.

Available remedies may include money damages, restitution, rescission, reformation, and—when damages are inadequate—specific performance.
