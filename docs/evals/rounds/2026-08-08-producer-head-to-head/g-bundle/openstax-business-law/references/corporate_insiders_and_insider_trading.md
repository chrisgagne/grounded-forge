# Definitions

- **Insider trading:** Buying or selling a security, in breach of a fiduciary duty or another relationship of trust and confidence, on the basis of material nonpublic information about the security.
- **Material nonpublic information:** Information not disclosed to the public that could affect the market price of a security.
- **Fiduciary:** A person charged with acting in another party's best interest; corporate fiduciaries are expected to act in investors' interests.
- **Corporate insider:** An officer, director, or beneficial owner holding more than 10% of a class of securities registered under Section 12 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.

# Compliance

Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 prohibit purchases or sales based on material nonpublic information in breach of the applicable duty. Corporate insiders must file ownership statements and report insider transactions as required by SEC rules.

Not every transaction by an insider is unlawful. SEC Rule 10b5-1 permits qualifying **pre-arranged trading plans** when trades are established in good faith in advance, occur on predetermined future dates, and involve preset amounts. A compliant plan can provide a **safe harbor**, meaning an exemption from insider-trading charges for the covered trades.

Companies need internal controls that identify corporate insiders, preserve required reports, and support timely disclosure of covered transactions.
