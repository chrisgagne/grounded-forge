# Title VII

Title VII of the Civil Rights Act prohibits covered employers from discriminating in hiring, firing, and promotion on the basis of sex, national origin, color, religion, or race.

- **Disparate treatment:** Intentional different treatment based on a protected class. The employee presents a prima facie case, the employer offers a legitimate nondiscriminatory reason, and the employee may show that reason is pretextual.
- **Disparate impact:** A facially neutral practice unintentionally causes disproportionate injury to a protected class. Statistical impact, business necessity, and possible pretext are central to the analysis.
- **Quid pro quo harassment:** A sexual demand is made or perceived as a condition of employment.
- **Hostile work environment:** Unwelcome conduct creates an abusive employment environment. The source notes that this theory can apply to sex-, race-, and religion-based harassment.

# Defenses

- **Bona fide occupational qualification:** A protected characteristic other than race or color is actually necessary to perform the job and the decision rests on qualifications rather than stereotypes.
- **Merit system:** A validated, job-related testing or performance system supports the decision.
- **Seniority system:** Preferential treatment based on tenure is lawful when the system did not originate in discrimination and applies equally.

# Related federal protections

- **Equal Pay Act:** Requires equal pay for work demanding equal skill, effort, and responsibility under similar conditions, subject to specified affirmative defenses.
- **Americans with Disabilities Act:** Prohibits disability discrimination and requires reasonable accommodation unless it imposes an undue burden.
- **Age Discrimination in Employment Act:** Prohibits covered employers from discriminating against people age 40 or older.
