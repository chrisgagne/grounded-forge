A trade union or labor union is an organized group of workers that represents employees and lobbies employers about working conditions. A **local union** is a company- or region-based group operating under a national union's charter.

# Representation

- **Appropriate bargaining unit:** The group of workers a union seeks to represent.
- **Collective bargaining:** Negotiation between a union and an employer over wages, hours, and other terms and conditions of employment.
- **Collective bargaining agreement:** The contract produced by collective bargaining.

# Union-security arrangements

- **Closed shop:** Union membership is a prerequisite to employment.
- **Union shop:** A newly hired employee must join the union within a specified period.
- **Agency shop:** Employees need not join the union but must pay agency fees.
- **Union security agreement:** An agreement defining whether employees must join the union and whether the employer collects dues or fees.

# Protected activity and limits

The National Labor Relations Act protects employees' rights to form, assist, and join labor organizations, bargain collectively, and engage in concerted activity. The National Labor Relations Board administers and enforces the Act, supervises representation elections, remedies unfair labor practices, and issues interpretive rules.

Workers may strike or picket subject to legal and contractual limits. Violent, sit-down, wildcat, and intermittent or partial strikes are identified as unlawful, and some collective bargaining agreements contain no-strike clauses.
