A sales contract obligates a **vendor** or seller to deliver a good and transfer its ownership to a **vendee** or buyer, who must pay money or its equivalent for the good.

# Defining features

Sales contracts are:

1. **Consensual:** Perfected by consent without an additional act.
2. **Bilateral:** Both parties owe reciprocal obligations.
3. **Onerous:** The good and its price are exchanged as consideration for one another.
4. **Commutative:** The good and price are treated as equivalent.
5. **Nominate:** The contract has the special designation of a sale.
6. **Principal:** Its validity does not depend on another contract.

# Governing law

Article 2 of the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), as adopted and potentially modified by each state, governs sales of goods. The UCC defines goods as tangible, movable items and distinguishes merchants because merchant transactions carry additional duties.

The UCC permits price, payment, delivery, and ongoing-contract duration to remain open by supplying reasonableness-based defaults. Quantity ordinarily must be specified, subject to requirements and output contracts governed by good-faith limitations.

For international sales between parties in ratifying countries, the United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (CISG) can govern and preempt the UCC. The CISG does not apply to consumer sales or service contracts.
