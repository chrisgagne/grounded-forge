Reporting companies must make periodic disclosures under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 so investors can evaluate the business, its leadership, its finances, and material events.

# Filing glossary

- **Form 10-K:** An annual report containing audited financial statements and disclosures concerning the business, financial condition, market risks, internal controls, legal proceedings, defaults, and other information material to investors.
- **Form 10-Q:** A quarterly report containing unaudited financial information.
- **Form 8-K:** A current report for specified events such as changes in corporate officers, mergers, or bankruptcy. The source states that it is due within four business days of the event.
- **Proxy statement:** Information sent to shareholders of companies with securities registered under Section 12 so they can vote on director elections and other matters at a shareholder meeting.
- **Schedule 13D:** A beneficial-ownership report intended to give investors notice of possible corporate takeovers. The source states that a person or entity becoming beneficial owner of more than 5% of a company's stock must file within 10 days, subject to exceptions.

# Related concepts

- **Audited financial statements:** Statements reviewed by an independent CPA for conformity with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles.
- **Beneficial owner:** A person or entity with voting and investment power over shares.
- **Reporting company:** A publicly owned company subject to periodic-disclosure requirements under Section 13(a).

The SEC makes company reports available to investors through EDGAR.
