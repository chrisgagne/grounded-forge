Title means ownership of a good. A sales transaction must account separately for ownership, encumbrances, risk of loss, and insurable interest.

# Title states

- **Good title:** Obtained from an owner who holds the goods free and clear.
- **Void title:** Purportedly transferred by someone who does not legitimately own the title. A buyer's good faith ordinarily does not cure a void title.
- **Voidable title:** Arises when a sale would otherwise be valid but circumstances such as deceit about identity, minority, or a bad check make the transaction voidable.

# Delivery arrangements

- **Simple delivery contract:** Title and insurable interest transfer when the contract is executed. Risk ordinarily transfers when the buyer takes possession, subject to the UCC rule applicable when the seller is not a merchant.
- **Shipment contract:** The seller arranges shipment and transfers the goods to a common carrier. Title and risk pass to the buyer at shipment.
- **Destination contract:** The seller must deliver to the contractually specified destination. Title and risk remain with the seller until delivery.
- **Goods-in-bailment contract:** Goods remain with a third-party custodian. Transfer depends on whether a negotiable or non-negotiable ownership document exists and when it is endorsed or the custodian is notified.
- **Conditional sales contract:** Completion depends on approval or another condition, as in a sale-or-return arrangement. The delivery method determines title and risk, while identification of the goods creates an insurable interest.

An insurable interest exists when a party holds title, bears the risk of loss, or otherwise has an economic interest in the goods.
