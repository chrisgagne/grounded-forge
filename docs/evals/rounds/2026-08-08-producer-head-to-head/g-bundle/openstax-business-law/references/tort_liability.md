A tort is an act or omission that causes injury or harm and creates a civil wrong. Tort law determines the defendant's legal responsibility and provides compensation for loss or disadvantage suffered because of another's conduct.

# Principal categories

- **Intentional tort:** Harm arising from a conscious and purposeful act. Examples include assault, malicious prosecution, defamation, and invasion of privacy.
- **Negligence:** Conduct that fails to meet the reasonable standard of care. A negligence claim requires a duty to act or refrain from acting, breach of that duty, a causal connection to the injury, and measurable harm.
- **Strict liability:** Liability imposed for reasons other than fault, including qualifying abnormally dangerous activities even when reasonable care was exercised.
- **Product liability:** Legal responsibility of a manufacturer, wholesaler, distributor, or retailer for harm proximately caused by a product defect.

# Product-defect categories

An unreasonably dangerous product commonly involves:

- A manufacturing flaw caused by a failure to exercise proper care.
- A design defect when a safer, economically feasible alternative exists.
- Inadequate instructions or warnings about proper use and potential danger.

# Common product-liability defenses

- **Assumption of risk:** The user knew of the defect and its risk but used the product anyway.
- **Misuse:** The product was used in a way it was not intended to be used.
- **Contributory negligence:** In jurisdictions applying it, the plaintiff's negligence prevents recovery.
- **Comparative negligence:** Recovery is reduced according to the plaintiff's share of responsibility.
