An unfair trade practice is a deceptive, fraudulent, or unethical method used to gain a business advantage or injure a consumer. Alternative names include deceptive trade practice and unfair business practice.

# Practice types

- False representation of a good or service.
- False advertising, including bait-and-switch sales.
- False endorsements, testimonials, guarantees, prizes, or gifts.
- Targeting vulnerable populations with deceptive or high-pressure tactics.
- Tied selling or failing to disclose a purchase condition.
- False or deceptive pricing, including fictitious limited-time or going-out-of-business sales.
- Withholding facts, such as the full price, that would reasonably influence a purchase decision.
- Noncompliance with applicable manufacturing standards.

# Federal Trade Commission standard

Section 5(a) of the Federal Trade Commission Act prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce. The source characterizes an unfair practice as one that causes or is likely to cause unavoidable consumer injury not outweighed by product or service benefits. A deceptive practice materially misrepresents or misleads the consumer.

# Enforcement

The FTC enforces federal consumer-protection law through investigations, voluntary compliance and consent orders, administrative complaints, rulemaking, and federal litigation. The source states that a plaintiff challenging an unfair trade practice need not prove intent when the practice itself is shown to be unfair or deceptive.
