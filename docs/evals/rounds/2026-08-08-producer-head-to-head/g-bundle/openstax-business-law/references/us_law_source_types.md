# Source types

- **U.S. Constitution:** The supreme law of the United States. It defines the boundaries of federal authority, empowers Congress to enact statutes for enumerated purposes, and includes the Bill of Rights.
- **Common law:** Judge-developed law expressed through decisions. Courts use case law and follow precedent from earlier, jurisdictionally relevant cases with substantially similar facts.
- **Federal statutes:** Laws enacted by Congress under constitutional authority and codified in the United States Code.
- **Administrative regulations:** Rules adopted by administrative agencies under authority delegated by statute. A valid regulation ordinarily has the force of law when it reasonably interprets the governing statute.
- **State law:** State constitutional, statutory, regulatory, and judicial law governing matters not preempted by valid federal law.
- **Local law:** Ordinances and other rules enacted by municipalities, cities, towns, or counties that do not conflict with state or federal law.

# Preemption

Valid federal law preempts conflicting state and local law, but only when Congress acts within powers granted by the Constitution. Federal and state law can also coexist in areas such as antitrust, trademarks, and employment.
