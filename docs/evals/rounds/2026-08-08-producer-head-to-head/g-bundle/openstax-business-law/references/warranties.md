A warranty is a legally binding guarantee, included with a sales contract, that a good or service will satisfy a stated task or minimum standard. If that standard is not met, the warranty supplies a remedy such as repair or replacement.

# Warranty types

- **Express warranty:** The seller explicitly states or demonstrates a fact about a product's quality, nature, character, purpose, performance, condition, use, or capacity, and the buyer reasonably relies on that representation as part of the bargain. Obvious puffery is not ordinarily binding.
- **Implied warranty:** A warranty arising by operation of law from the sale itself, even when the seller made no express guarantee.
- **Warranty against infringement:** A merchant seller's implied assurance that the goods are not subject to third-party trademark, patent, or other intellectual-property claims.
- **Warranty of merchantability:** The implied assurance that goods sold by a merchant are fit for their ordinary purpose.
- **Warranty of fitness for a particular purpose:** Arises when the buyer communicates a special purpose, relies on the seller's skill or judgment, and the seller assists in selecting the product.
- **Warranty of title:** The seller's implied assurance that title is good, transfer is lawful, and the goods are free of undisclosed liens.

# Breach

An express warranty is breached when the warranted representation is false. To establish breach of the implied warranty of merchantability, a buyer must show that the good was defective, the defect made it unfit for its purpose, and the defect caused harm. Defects can involve design, manufacture, instructions, or warnings.
