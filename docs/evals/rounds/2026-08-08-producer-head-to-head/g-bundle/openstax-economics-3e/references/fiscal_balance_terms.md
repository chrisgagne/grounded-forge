# Annual budget status

Let $T$ denote government tax receipts and $G$ denote government spending during a year:

- **Balanced budget:** $T=G$.
- **Budget surplus:** $T-G>0$; receipts exceed spending.
- **Budget deficit:** $G-T>0$; spending exceeds receipts.

Deficits and surpluses are annual flows, not the outstanding stock of government borrowing.

# National debt

The national debt is the total accumulated amount the government has borrowed over time and not yet repaid. Successive deficits add to debt, while surpluses can reduce it:

$$
Debt_t = Debt_{t-1} + Deficit_t - Surplus_t
$$

Debt and annual deficit should therefore not be used interchangeably. Analysts often scale either amount by GDP to compare it across economies or over time.

# Standardized-employment budget

The standardized-employment budget estimates the deficit or surplus that would prevail if the economy were producing at potential GDP. It separates the budget's cyclical response to current economic conditions from the underlying fiscal stance.

# Automatic and discretionary changes

Automatic stabilizers are standing tax and spending rules that cushion changes in aggregate demand without new legislation. Discretionary fiscal policy is a newly enacted change in taxes or spending intended to alter overall economic activity.
