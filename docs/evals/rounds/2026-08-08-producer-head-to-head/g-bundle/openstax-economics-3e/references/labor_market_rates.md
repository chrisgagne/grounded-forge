# Population categories

- **Employed:** currently working for pay.
- **Unemployed:** without a job, currently available to work, and actively looking for work.
- **Out of the labor force:** not working for pay and not actively looking for work.
- **Labor force:** employed people plus unemployed people.
- **Adult population:** the working-age population from which the labor force and those out of the labor force are classified.

# Unemployment rate

The unemployment rate is the percentage of the labor force that is unemployed:

$$
Unemployment\ rate =
\frac{Unemployed}{Employed + Unemployed} \times 100
$$

It is not the percentage of the entire adult population without jobs. Discouraged workers who have stopped actively looking are classified as out of the labor force, while part-time workers and people working below their skill level are classified as employed.

# Labor-force-participation rate

The labor-force-participation rate is the percentage of the adult population that is employed or unemployed and looking for work:

$$
Labor\ force\ participation\ rate =
\frac{Employed + Unemployed}{Adult\ population} \times 100
$$

# Interpretation

The two rates answer different questions. The unemployment rate describes unsuccessful job seeking among labor-force participants; the participation rate describes how much of the adult population is working or actively seeking work. A falling unemployment rate can therefore coexist with weak labor-force participation if people stop looking for work.
