# Market share

A firm's market share is its percentage of total sales in the relevant market:

$$
s_i = \frac{Firm\ i\ sales}{Total\ market\ sales} \times 100
$$

Any concentration measure depends on how the product and geographic market are defined.

# Concentration ratio

A concentration ratio is the share of industry sales accounted for by a specified number of the largest firms. The commonly used four-firm concentration ratio is:

$$
CR_4 = s_1+s_2+s_3+s_4
$$

It is easy to interpret but ignores the distribution of market shares outside the selected largest firms and does not distinguish among different share distributions that have the same sum.

# Herfindahl-Hirschman Index

The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) squares every firm's market share and sums the results:

$$
HHI = \sum_{i=1}^{N} s_i^2
$$

When shares are expressed as percentages, HHI ranges from near zero for a market with many very small firms to 10,000 for a single-firm market. Squaring gives relatively more weight to firms with large market shares.

# Use

Concentration measures summarize market structure and can help antitrust analysis, but they do not by themselves establish anticompetitive conduct or its effects.
