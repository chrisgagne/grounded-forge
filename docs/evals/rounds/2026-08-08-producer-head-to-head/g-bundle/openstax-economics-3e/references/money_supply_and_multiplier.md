# Money aggregates

- **M1:** a narrow measure comprising currency in circulation and checkable bank deposits, with traveler's checks as a small additional component.
- **M2:** M1 plus savings deposits, money-market funds, and time deposits such as certificates of deposit.

M2 is broader and therefore includes every component of M1.

# Bank balance-sheet terms

- **Reserves:** funds a bank keeps on hand rather than lending or investing.
- **Reserve ratio:** reserves divided by deposits.
- **Net worth or bank capital:** total assets minus total liabilities.
- **Limited reserves:** banks hold reserves at or only slightly above the required minimum.
- **Ample reserves:** banks hold reserves significantly above the required minimum.

# Simple money multiplier

In the textbook's limited-reserves model, banks lend funds beyond required reserves, borrowers spend the loans, and recipients redeposit the funds. The maximum deposit expansion is summarized by:

$$
Money\ multiplier = \frac{1}{Reserve\ ratio}
$$

For an initial increase in reserves:

$$
Maximum\ change\ in\ deposits =
Initial\ change\ in\ reserves \times Money\ multiplier
$$

# Scope

The simple multiplier describes a limited-reserves environment and a maximum expansion under its assumptions. It is not a mechanical prediction when banks hold ample excess reserves or when funds do not remain in the deposit-and-lending cycle.
