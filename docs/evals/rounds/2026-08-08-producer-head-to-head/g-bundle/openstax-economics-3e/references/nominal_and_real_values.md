# Convention

A **nominal value** is measured at the prices that prevailed when the statistic was recorded. A **real value** is the same statistic adjusted for changes in the price level, allowing comparisons in the purchasing power of a common base period.

# Conversion

When a published price index uses a base-year value of 100:

$$
Real\ value = \frac{Nominal\ value}{Price\ index / 100}
$$

Equivalently:

$$
Price\ index = \frac{Nominal\ value}{Real\ value} \times 100
$$

For GDP, the GDP deflator is the price index for the average prices of all final goods and services included in GDP. Real GDP is nominal GDP divided by the GDP deflator expressed as a decimal.

# Base-year interpretation

The base year supplies the constant prices used to value quantities in every comparison period, and its index is defined as 100. Nominal and real values therefore coincide in the base year. With positive inflation, real GDP is lower than nominal GDP after the base year and higher than nominal GDP before it.

# Growth-rate approximation

For small changes:

$$
Real\ growth\ rate \approx Nominal\ growth\ rate - Inflation\ rate
$$

Direct percentage-change calculations on real values are more accurate when changes are large.
