# Price level and index numbers

A price level combines the prices of many goods and services into one weighted measure, with weights based on the quantities in a representative basket. An index number expresses that level relative to an arbitrary base period whose value is defined as 100:

$$
Price\ index_t =
\frac{Cost\ of\ basket_t}{Cost\ of\ basket_{base}} \times 100
$$

Index numbers are unit-free: they carry neither currency symbols nor percent signs. Changing the base year rescales the index but does not change the measured inflation rate.

# Inflation rate

Inflation is a general and continuing rise in an economy's price level. The inflation rate between two periods is the percentage change in the relevant price level or index:

$$
Inflation\ rate_t =
\frac{Price\ index_t - Price\ index_{t-1}}{Price\ index_{t-1}} \times 100
$$

The rate must be calculated as a percentage change rather than by merely subtracting index values. Negative inflation is deflation.

# Consumer Price Index

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) is the commonly cited U.S. inflation measure based on the prices of a fixed basket of goods and services representing an average consumer's purchases. A **core inflation index** commonly starts from CPI and excludes volatile categories such as food and energy to better expose the persistent price trend.

# Other price indexes

- **GDP deflator:** based on the prices of all components of GDP.
- **Producer Price Index (PPI):** based on the prices producers pay for supplies and inputs.
- **Employment Cost Index:** based on wages paid in labor markets.
- **International Price Index:** based on prices of imported and exported merchandise.

# Measurement cautions

- **Substitution bias:** a fixed basket can overstate the rise in the cost of living because consumers substitute away from goods whose relative prices rise.
- **Quality/new-goods bias:** a fixed basket can overstate the rise in the cost of living when it does not fully account for improved quality or newly available goods.

Updating the basket can reduce these biases but cannot eliminate them completely.
