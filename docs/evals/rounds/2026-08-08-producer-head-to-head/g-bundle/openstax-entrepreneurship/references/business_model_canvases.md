# Business model canvas

A business model explains how a venture creates, delivers, and captures value. The business model canvas maps that logic into nine named blocks.

| Block | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| Customer segments | The groups of customers the venture intends to serve. |
| Value propositions | The benefits customers can expect from the venture's products or services. |
| Channels | How the venture communicates with customers and delivers its offerings. |
| Customer relationships | How the venture establishes and maintains its interactions with each customer segment. |
| Revenue streams | How the venture receives income from the value it provides. |
| Key resources | The assets and capabilities needed to execute the model. |
| Key activities | The work the venture must perform to create and deliver its value proposition. |
| Key partners | The outside parties that help the venture execute the model. |
| Cost structure | The costs incurred while operating the model. |

The source groups the blocks by three broad tests:

- **Desirability:** value propositions, customer relationships, customer segments, and channels test whether the model creates customer value.
- **Feasibility:** key partners, key activities, and key resources describe how the model will be executed.
- **Viability:** cost structure and revenue streams test whether the venture can sustain itself financially.

# Lean model canvas

The lean model canvas is Ash Maurya's startup-oriented adaptation. It retains five business model canvas blocks:

- Customer segments
- Value propositions
- Channels
- Revenue streams
- Cost structure

It replaces customer relationships, key activities, key partners, and key resources with:

- **Problem:** the customer problem the startup needs to understand.
- **Solution:** the proposed response, often connected to the features of a minimum viable product.
- **Key metrics:** the measures used to judge whether the model is working.
- **Unfair advantage:** a defensible advantage or barrier to entry.

OpenStax characterizes the lean canvas as simpler and often better suited to startups, while the standard business model canvas gives a more complete picture of a mature business.

# Social business model canvas

The social business model canvas adapts the core canvas for social-enterprise ventures. In addition to familiar business-model fields, its distinct concerns include:

- **Impact measures:** quantifiable evidence of the social or environmental effect, such as trees planted, people housed, jobs created, or investments made.
- **Surplus:** what happens to profit and where it will be reinvested.
- **Beneficiary segments:** the people or groups intended to receive the social benefit.
- **Social and customer value propositions:** the value created for beneficiaries as well as paying customers.

# Related tools

- A **value proposition canvas** expands the customer segment and value proposition blocks by examining customer pains, gains, and jobs to be done alongside products and services, pain relievers, and gain creators.
- A **customer empathy map** portrays a target customer from that person's perspective, including what the person needs to do, sees, says, does, hears, and thinks.
- The canvases are iterative tools: their fields should be revised as customer discovery tests the venture's assumptions.
