# Financial statement set

Financial statements summarize the transactions recorded by an accounting system so that owners, managers, investors, and other stakeholders can evaluate a venture.

| Statement | Purpose | Core fields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Balance sheet | Summarizes the company's financial condition at a point in time and shows what it owns, what it owes, and the owners' residual interest. | Assets, liabilities, and equity. |
| Income statement | Reports the results of operations by showing how much the company earned and the costs incurred to generate that revenue. It is also called a profit-and-loss statement. | Revenue, cost of goods sold, gross profit, operating expenses, and net income. |
| Statement of cash flows | Explains the sources and uses of cash and reconciles activity that may appear on the income statement or balance sheet without producing an immediate cash receipt or payment. | Operating activities, investing activities, and financing activities. |

# Balance-sheet fields

- **Assets:** Resources the company owns or expects to benefit from, such as cash, inventory, receivables, equipment, vehicles, land, or buildings.
- **Liabilities:** Debts or other obligations that require a future payment, such as accounts payable, credit-card balances, or loans payable.
- **Equity:** The owners' claim on the assets after liabilities are repaid.
- **Accounting equation:** `assets = liabilities + equity`. The balance sheet must keep both sides equal.
- **Accounts receivable:** Amounts customers owe the business.
- **Accounts payable:** Amounts the business owes vendors.
- **Fixed assets:** Long-lived major purchases such as equipment, land, or buildings.

# Income-statement fields

- **Revenue:** Amount earned by selling products or providing services.
- **Expenses:** Costs incurred in the normal course of operations.
- **Cost of goods sold:** Direct cost required to produce the product or service sold.
- **Gross profit:** Selling price or revenue less direct costs.
- **Net income:** Revenue remaining after expenses; a positive result is profit.

# Cash-flow sections

- **Operating activities:** Day-to-day cash activity, including receipts from customers and payments for supplies and rent.
- **Investing activities:** Major purchases or sales of equipment, facilities, and investments.
- **Financing activities:** Cash contributed by owners, lenders, or other investors and related repayments or distributions.

The statement of cash flows differs from the income statement because it records cash actually received or paid. For example, an income statement can recognize a sale before the customer pays, while the cash-flow statement will not show a receipt until cash is collected.
