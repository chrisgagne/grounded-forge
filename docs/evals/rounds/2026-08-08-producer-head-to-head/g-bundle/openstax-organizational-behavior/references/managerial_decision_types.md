# Definition

**Decision-making** is the process of considering possible options and selecting one. Managerial decisions affect organizational effectiveness and the stakeholders influenced by the organization.

# Decision types

| Type | Characteristics | Appropriate approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Programmed decision** | Repeated over time; rules and criteria are known or can be estimated with reasonable accuracy | Apply established rules, structure, or a tested heuristic; often routine and low-involvement |
| **Nonprogrammed decision** | Novel and unstructured; criteria may be ambiguous and information incomplete | Gather relevant information, exercise judgment and creativity, and use a systematic process; often nonroutine and high-involvement |

# Information-processing systems

- **Reflective system:** logical, analytical, deliberate, and methodical; particularly useful for novel, complex, or consequential decisions.
- **Reactive system:** quick, intuitive, and guided by emotion, habit, or learned pattern recognition; useful when immediate action or well-developed expertise applies.

The systems are not simply “good” and “bad.” Experience can make a rapid reactive judgment effective, while strong emotion can make it unreliable. Significant unfamiliar decisions generally call for reflective processing.

# Nonprogrammed decision process

1. Recognize that a decision is needed.
2. Generate multiple alternatives.
3. Analyze the alternatives.
4. Select an alternative.
5. Implement the selected alternative.
6. Evaluate its effectiveness.
