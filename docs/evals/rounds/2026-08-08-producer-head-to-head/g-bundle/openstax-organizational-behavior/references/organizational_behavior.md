# Definition

**Organizational behavior** is the study of the actions and attitudes of people and groups toward one another and toward the organization as a whole. It applies findings from the social and behavioral sciences to understand and predict human behavior at work and to improve both organizational performance and the quality of working life.

# Levels of analysis

- **Individual:** abilities, personality, values, perception, attitudes, learning, motivation, and performance.
- **Group:** roles, norms, teams, communication, leadership, power, conflict, and negotiation.
- **Organization:** structure, culture, environmental relationships, change, and human-resource systems.

The individual and group levels are often called **micro-organizational behavior**. Organization-wide design, structure, and environmental relationships are **macro-organizational behavior**, also called **organization theory**. The levels should be considered together because organization-wide conditions can shape individual and group behavior.

# Relationship to management

Management coordinates people and other resources through planning, organizing, directing, and controlling to accomplish organizational objectives. Organizational behavior supplies managers with concepts for understanding behavior, anticipating responses to managerial action, and designing more effective organizations.
