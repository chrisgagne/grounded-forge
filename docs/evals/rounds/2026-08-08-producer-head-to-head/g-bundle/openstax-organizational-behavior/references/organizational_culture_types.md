# Definition

**Organizational culture** is the shared pattern of values, beliefs, assumptions, and behaviors within an organization. Culture helps the organization adapt to its external environment and creates internal unity around common goals. Founders and top leaders strongly influence it through their vision, values, mission, and modeled behavior.

# Competing Values Framework

The Competing Values Framework classifies culture along two axes:

- **Focus:** internal versus external.
- **Orientation:** flexibility versus stability and control.

| Culture type | Focus | Orientation | Primary emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Adhocracy** | External | Flexibility | Innovation, experimentation, entrepreneurship, risk-taking, and adaptation |
| **Clan** | Internal | Flexibility | Relationships, participation, commitment, mentoring, coaching, and human development |
| **Hierarchy** | Internal | Stability and control | Efficiency, process, precision, cost control, technical expertise, and careful decisions |
| **Market** | External | Stability and control | Competition, customer and shareholder value, goals, results, speed, and execution |

An organization may combine types or contain different subcultures, even when one culture remains dominant.
