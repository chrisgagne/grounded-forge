# Core distinctions

- **Power:** the capacity of one person or group to cause another person or group to take an action that would not otherwise be taken.
- **Authority:** the accepted right to seek compliance; unlike power in general, its exercise is backed by legitimacy.
- **Leadership:** the ability to elicit responses that go beyond required or mechanical compliance; its distinguishing feature is voluntary influence.

# Bases of power

| Base | Source of influence |
| --- | --- |
| **Referent power** | Admiration, identification, reputation, or interpersonal attraction |
| **Expert power** | Knowledge or expertise relevant to the other party |
| **Legitimate power** | An accepted right to exercise influence in a defined domain, commonly attached to an official role |
| **Reward power** | Control over outcomes the other party values, such as pay, promotion, assignments, or responsibility |
| **Coercive power** | The capacity to punish or withhold valued outcomes, making compliance rest primarily on fear |

# Typical responses

Referent and expert power are most likely to produce **commitment**. Legitimate and reward power commonly produce **compliance** without the same enthusiasm. Coercive power is most likely to produce **resistance**.
