# Definition

**Organizational structure** is the system of task and reporting relationships used to coordinate work and motivate people toward organizational goals. Structure should fit the organization’s environment, strategy, scale, and need for control or adaptation.

# Structural continuum

- **Mechanistic structure:** hierarchical, centralized, formalized, specialized, and governed through narrow spans of control and clear rules; suited to relatively simple and stable environments.
- **Organic structure:** flatter, decentralized, flexible, participatory, and governed through broader spans of control and shared tasks; suited to complex, changing, and uncertain environments.

# Major structure types

| Type | Organizing principle | Typical benefit | Typical risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Functional** | Departments based on expertise such as production, finance, or human resources | Specialization, economies of scale, and simple reporting within functions | Departmental silos and slow cross-functional coordination |
| **Divisional** | Semi-contained units based on a market, product, service, or other business segment | Clear segment accountability and faster service within a division | Duplication, isolation, and incompatible systems across divisions |
| **Geographic** | Units based on customer locations or regions | Local knowledge and responsiveness | Decentralized decisions and difficult headquarters coordination |
| **Matrix** | Simultaneous functional and product, project, or geographic authority | Horizontal coordination and flexible use of expertise | Dual reporting, divided loyalties, and conflict between managers |
| **Networked/team** | Flexible relationships and teams formed around tasks, problems, or opportunities | Fast access to distributed expertise and adaptable collaboration | Unclear communication, technology dependence, and coordination strain |
| **Virtual** | Information technology links geographically dispersed people and outsourced capabilities | Flexibility, lower costs, broad labor access, and fewer geographic constraints | Social isolation, reduced trust, and weaker collaboration |

Large organizations commonly mix structure types rather than use a single pure form.
