# Team

A **team** is a group of people organized to function cooperatively. Effective teams combine:

- a common commitment and purpose;
- specific performance goals;
- complementary skills;
- agreement about how the work will be done; and
- mutual accountability.

A team performs work and assesses collective work products together. A conventional working group more often has a single focused leader, individual accountabilities and work products, and a purpose inherited from the broader organization.

# Development stages

1. **Forming:** members become acquainted, seek structure and direction, test acceptable behavior, and remain dependent on others for cues.
2. **Storming:** members compete for influence, test group processes, surface conflict, and work to clarify goals and roles.
3. **Norming:** shared norms and boundaries develop, members accept one another, responsibility is shared, and commitment to group goals grows.
4. **Performing:** roles and task relationships support goal attainment; the team becomes increasingly self-directed and focused on results.
5. **Adjourning:** the team disbands when its project or shared work ends.

Development is not strictly linear. A new member, changed scope, or disruptive task can return a team to an earlier stage before it resumes performing.
