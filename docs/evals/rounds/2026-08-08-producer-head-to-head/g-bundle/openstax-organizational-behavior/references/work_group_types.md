# Definition

A **work group** is a collection of individuals who share norms, generally have differentiated roles, and interact in pursuit of common goals. Work groups have both a structure—the roles, norms, status relationships, and other stable features of the group—and processes through which members interact.

# Classification

| Organizational status | Relatively permanent | Relatively temporary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Formal** | **Command group** (or functional group) | **Task group** |
| **Informal** | **Friendship group** | **Interest group** |

# Type definitions

- **Formal group:** a work unit deliberately prescribed by the organization to perform assigned work.
- **Command group:** a continuing formal unit, such as a department.
- **Task group:** a temporary formal unit, such as a task force established for a specific objective.
- **Informal group:** a group that develops naturally from members’ interests, social needs, or friendships rather than deliberate organizational design.
- **Friendship group:** a generally long-lasting informal group based on members’ social relationships.
- **Interest group:** an informal group organized around a shared interest that may dissolve when the interest changes.

An employee can belong to several formal and informal groups at once, creating overlapping norms, roles, goals, and loyalties.
