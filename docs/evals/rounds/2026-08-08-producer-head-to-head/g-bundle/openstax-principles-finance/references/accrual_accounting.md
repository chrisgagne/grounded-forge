# Accrual Accounting

Accrual-basis accounting records economic activity when revenue is earned or an expense is incurred, even when the related cash receipt or payment occurs in another period. Cash-basis accounting instead records activity when cash changes hands.

## Core conventions

- **Revenue recognition**: recognize revenue after the promised goods or services have been delivered or rendered and the revenue has been earned.
- **Expense recognition**: record an expense in the period in which it is incurred and associate it with the revenue or activity it helps produce.
- **Double-entry accounting**: every transaction affects at least two accounts, and total debits must equal total credits.
- **Accounting equation**: `Assets = Liabilities + Equity`.

## Capitalization and depreciation

An expenditure that creates a significant resource with a useful life longer than one year is generally capitalized as an asset rather than expensed immediately. Its cost is then allocated over its useful life through depreciation. Common allocation methods include straight-line, double-declining-balance, and units-of-production depreciation.

Because recognition and cash movement can occur at different times, accounting profit is not the same as cash flow.
