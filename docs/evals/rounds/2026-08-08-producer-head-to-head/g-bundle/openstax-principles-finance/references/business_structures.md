# Business Structures

The legal form of a business determines how it is owned, governed, taxed, financed, and exposed to liability.

| Structure | Ownership and control | Liability | Continuity and financing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | One owner directly controls the business | The owner generally has unlimited personal liability | Easy to form, but its life and access to capital are tied closely to the owner |
| Partnership | Two or more owners share management and profits under a partnership agreement | General partners typically have unlimited personal liability; limited partners may have liability limited to their investment | Pools owners’ resources but can be disrupted by changes in membership |
| Corporation | A separate legal entity owned by shareholders and governed through a board of directors | Shareholders generally have limited liability | Has continuing existence and can raise capital by issuing debt or equity |
| Limited-liability hybrid | Combines pass-through or partnership-like features with limited liability, depending on the jurisdiction and form | Owners generally receive limited-liability protection | Offers flexible ownership and governance arrangements |

Corporations are normally formed by filing articles of incorporation. Their separation of ownership from management makes corporate governance and agency conflicts especially important.
