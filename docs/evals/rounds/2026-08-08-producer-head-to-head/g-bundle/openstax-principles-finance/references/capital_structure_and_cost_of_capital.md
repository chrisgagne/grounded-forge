# Capital Structure and Cost of Capital

Capital structure is the mix of debt and equity used to finance a company’s assets. The cost of each source is the return required by investors providing that capital.

## Component costs

- The yield to maturity on outstanding debt indicates the return required by debt investors.
- Because interest expense is tax-deductible, the after-tax cost of debt is `rd × (1 - T)`.
- The cost of equity is not directly observed. Common estimates use CAPM or a constant-growth dividend model.
- Preferred stock and newly issued equity may be included as separate components when material; issuing new shares can also impose flotation costs.

Using market-value weights:

```text
WACC = wd × rd × (1 - T) + we × re
```

Additional capital components can be added with their respective market weights and required returns.

## Leverage trade-off

An unlevered firm uses no debt; a levered firm uses debt and equity. Debt can add value through its interest tax shield, but greater leverage also increases the probability and cost of financial distress and raises the returns demanded by capital providers. Trade-off theory frames levered value as unlevered value plus the interest tax shield minus expected financial-distress costs.

The target structure is the financing mix expected to maximize firm value, not simply the mix with the lowest stated borrowing rate.
