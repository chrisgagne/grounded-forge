# Corporate Governance

Corporate governance is the system through which shareholders, the board of directors, and management direct and oversee a corporation.

## Participants

- **Shareholders** own the corporation through shares and elect directors.
- **Board of directors** acts as a fiduciary for shareholders, oversees management, sets broad goals and policies, appoints or removes the chief executive, and helps ensure that the company has adequate resources.
- **Management** runs day-to-day operations and executes corporate strategy.
- **Stakeholders** are people or groups with an interest in the effects of the organization’s actions, including employees, customers, creditors, regulators, and communities.

## Agency issues

An agency problem arises when managers or directors pursue interests that conflict with shareholders’ interests. Common controls include independent oversight, transparent reporting, ethical standards, performance-linked compensation, and equity incentives designed to encourage long-term decision-making.

Board independence, relevant experience, and diversity can strengthen oversight. Investor relations supports governance by providing market participants with clear, timely, and understandable information.
