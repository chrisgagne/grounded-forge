# Financial Forecasting

Financial forecasting estimates future performance, financial position, and cash needs so that managers and investors can assess growth plans and financing requirements.

## Forecast sequence

1. **Forecast sales first.** Use historical sales together with information about products, customers, competitors, and the economy.
2. **Build the pro forma income statement.** Estimate costs and earnings from the sales forecast, historical relationships, and known operating changes.
3. **Build the pro forma balance sheet.** Forecast assets, liabilities, and equity while preserving their links to sales, earnings, investment, financing, and company policy.
4. **Complete the cash forecast.** Combine the projected statements with collection, payment, investment, and financing assumptions to locate cash surpluses or gaps.
5. **Review value and feasibility.** Test whether proposed growth creates value, maintains liquidity, and can be financed.

Pro forma statements are forward-looking, internally linked statements rather than historical reports. Scenario analysis changes a coherent set of assumptions, while sensitivity analysis changes selected inputs to identify the estimates that have the greatest effect on the outcome.
