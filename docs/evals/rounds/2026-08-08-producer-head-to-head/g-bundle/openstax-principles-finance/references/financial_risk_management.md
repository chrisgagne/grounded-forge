# Financial Risk Management

Financial risk management identifies uncertain cash inflows and outflows, measures their potential effect, and selects which exposures to retain, reduce, transfer, or hedge.

## Exposure types

- **Commodity-price risk**: changes in input or output prices affect cost of goods sold, revenue, and profit. Responses include vertical integration, long-term supply agreements, and futures contracts.
- **Transaction currency risk**: exchange-rate movements alter the home-currency value of contracted foreign-currency receipts or payments.
- **Translation currency risk**: exchange-rate movements change the reported home-currency value of foreign operations.
- **Economic currency risk**: currency movements alter long-run competitive position and future operating cash flows.
- **Interest-rate risk**: changing rates alter borrowing costs and the present value of fixed future cash flows. Duration summarizes a fixed-income instrument’s sensitivity to rate changes.

## Instruments

- A **forward contract** fixes an exchange rate or price for an exchange on a future date.
- A **futures contract** is a standardized exchange-traded commitment used to lock in a future price.
- A **call option** gives its holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy the underlying asset.
- A **put option** gives its holder the right, but not the obligation, to sell the underlying asset.
- A **swap** exchanges specified payment streams, such as fixed-rate payments for floating-rate payments.

Hedging reduces a selected exposure; it does not automatically eliminate operational, counterparty, basis, liquidity, or forecasting risk.
