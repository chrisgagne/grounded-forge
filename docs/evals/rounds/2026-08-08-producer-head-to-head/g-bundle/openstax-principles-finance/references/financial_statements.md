# Financial Statements

The primary financial statements describe performance over a period, financial position at a point in time, changes in owners’ claims, and the sources and uses of cash.

| Statement | What it reports | Core relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Income statement | Revenue, expenses, and profit over a period | `Net income = Revenues − Expenses` |
| Balance sheet | Assets, liabilities, and equity at a date | `Assets = Liabilities + Equity` |
| Statement of owners’ equity | Beginning equity, owner transactions, earnings, distributions, and ending equity | Ending retained earnings incorporates net income and dividends |
| Statement of cash flows | Cash generated or used by operating, investing, and financing activities | Reconciles accrual-based results with changes in cash |

The income statement is prepared before the ending balance of retained earnings is determined. That ending balance flows into equity on the balance sheet.

## Cash-flow measures

Operating cash flow reflects cash generated or used by the core business. Free cash flow to the firm is:

```text
FCFF = Operating cash flow - Capital expenditures
```

FCFF represents cash available for debt service, distributions to capital providers, and growth needs.

## Common-size statements

In a common-size income statement, each line is expressed as a percentage of net sales. In a common-size balance sheet, each line is expressed as a percentage of total assets. This normalization supports comparisons across firms of different sizes.
