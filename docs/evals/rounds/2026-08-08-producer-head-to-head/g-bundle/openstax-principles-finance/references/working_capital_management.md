# Working Capital Management

Working capital supports day-to-day operations and expands or contracts with the business cycle.

```text
Net working capital = Current assets - Current liabilities
```

## Operating components

- **Cash management** maintains enough cash for expected obligations and contingencies while placing excess funds in low-risk, liquid marketable securities.
- **Trade credit** allows business customers to buy before paying. Creditors assess creditworthiness, set terms, monitor payment, and manage delinquency; debtors manage accounts payable and available discounts.
- **Receivables management** uses accounts-receivable turnover, average collection period, and aging schedules to monitor collection quality.
- **Inventory management** balances ordering and carrying costs against stockout risk. Just-in-time practices reduce inventory investment but increase sensitivity to supply interruption.

The cash conversion cycle measures the time between committing cash to operations and collecting cash from customers. A cash budget projects inflows, outflows, minimum balances, and short-term financing needs; although it is prepared after other operating budgets, it is central to short-term planning.
