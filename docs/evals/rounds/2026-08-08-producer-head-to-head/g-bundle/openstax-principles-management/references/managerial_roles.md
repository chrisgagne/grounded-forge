# Role Families

Managers perform three broad families of roles:

- **Interpersonal roles** arise from formal authority and relationships.
  - **Figurehead:** performs ceremonial duties as the head of an organizational unit.
  - **Leader:** takes responsibility for the work and development of people in the unit.
  - **Liaison:** establishes and maintains contacts outside the vertical chain of command.
- **Informational roles** make the manager a collection and distribution point for organizational information.
  - **Monitor:** scans the environment and the manager's network for information.
  - **Disseminator:** passes privileged or useful information to people inside the organization.
  - **Spokesperson:** communicates information to people outside the organization.
- **Decisional roles** apply information and authority to choices on behalf of the organization and its stakeholders.
  - **Entrepreneur:** initiates improvements and adapts the business to opportunities or changing conditions.
  - **Disturbance or crisis handler:** responds to conditions that require corrective action.
  - **Resource allocator:** decides who receives limited funding, equipment, labor, space, and managerial attention.
  - **Negotiator:** represents the organization in allocations, agreements, and dispute resolution.

# Practical Context

The balance among these roles depends on a manager's hierarchical level and function. Managerial work is typically fragmented, conversational, and highly interactive, so the roles often overlap during a single activity.
