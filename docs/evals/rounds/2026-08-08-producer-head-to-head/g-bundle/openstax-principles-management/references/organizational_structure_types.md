# Structural Orientation

- **Mechanistic structures** use centralized, rule-based, top-down control, narrow spans of control, specialized tasks, and rigid departmentalization. They best fit relatively simple, stable, low-uncertainty environments.
- **Organic structures** are flatter and more fluid, with participatory communication, decision-making in multiple directions, wider spans of control, shared tasks, and fewer rigid rules. They best fit unstable, complex, changing environments.

# Common Structure Types

| Type | Organizing principle | Characteristic trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional | Departments based on expertise, such as research and development, production, accounting, and human resources | Supports specialization, straightforward reporting, and economies of scale, but can isolate departments and slow cross-functional coordination. |
| Divisional | Functional departments grouped under a division head, often by market, product, service, or another business segment | Focuses accountability and service within a segment, but can create insular divisions and duplicate or fragment enterprise systems. |
| Geographic | Units organized around the locations of the customers the company serves | Improves local knowledge and responsiveness, but can decentralize decisions and make headquarters coordination harder. |
| Matrix | Vertical functional authority combined with horizontal product or project teams | Integrates expertise and supports flexible coordination, but dual reporting can create confusion, conflict, and divided loyalties. |
| Networked-team | Informal, flexible networks and teams organized around tasks, problems, or opportunities | Enables rapid access to people and resources, but requires clear communication, dependable technology, and active relationship management. |
| Virtual | Technology-dependent, dispersed work organized with few geographic constraints | Can reduce costs and response time and broaden access to talent, but may increase social isolation, distrust, and collaboration difficulties. |

Organizations may combine structure types. The appropriate design depends on the external environment and the organization's vision, mission, and goals.
