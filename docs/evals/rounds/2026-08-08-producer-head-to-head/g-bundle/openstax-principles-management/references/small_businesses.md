# Definition

A **small business** is independently managed, owned by an individual or a small group of investors, based locally, and not dominant in its industry. The textbook's general definition uses fewer than 500 employees, but the exact boundary varies by purpose and industry.

# Size Standards

The U.S. Small Business Administration uses size standards to determine whether an entity is eligible for programs and preferences reserved for small businesses. These standards depend on economic activity or industry and are generally aligned with the North American Industry Classification System.

Statistics may use different criteria—including employee count, revenue, age, employer status, and geography—so a statement about the number or performance of small businesses should identify the definition being applied.

# Entrepreneurial Distinction

Small-business ownership does not by itself make the owner an entrepreneur. An owner may deliberately remain small and manage an established model, while an entrepreneur assumes risk to create a new business or substantially redirect an existing one.
