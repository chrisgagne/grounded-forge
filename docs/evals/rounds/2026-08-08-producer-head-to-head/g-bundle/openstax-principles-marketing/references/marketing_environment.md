# Marketing Environment

The marketing environment comprises the internal and external forces that influence an organization's marketing activities. Internal factors are more directly controllable; external factors generally are not.

## Internal environment

The internal environment includes organizational culture and the **5Ms**:

- **Minds (staffing):** the people who deploy the organization's other resources.
- **Minutes (time):** time available for processes, market response, and strategy implementation.
- **Machinery (equipment):** equipment and physical assets used in production.
- **Materials (production):** inputs needed to produce goods and services.
- **Money (finance):** funds used to acquire or hire other resources.

## Microenvironment

The external microenvironment, or task environment, contains actors close to the organization:

- **Suppliers:** partners that provide necessary parts, products, or other inputs.
- **Market intermediaries:** retailers, wholesalers, and other channel members that help distribute an offering.
- **Customers:** the people or organizations the marketer must understand and reach.
- **Competitors:** alternative providers whose strengths, weaknesses, and offerings shape market opportunity.
- **General public:** communities whose perceptions affect the organization's reputation and acceptance.

## Macroenvironment

A **PESTLE** analysis groups broad external forces into:

- **Political:** political stability, public policy, and trade or environmental restrictions.
- **Economic:** inflation, employment, income, interest rates, and general economic conditions.
- **Social:** demographics, culture, values, attitudes, and lifestyle changes.
- **Technological:** innovations and changes in the technologies available to organizations and customers.
- **Legal:** laws and regulations governing organizations and marketing conduct.
- **Environmental:** ecological conditions, natural resources, climate concerns, and sustainability pressures.
