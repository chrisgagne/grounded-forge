# Pricing Strategy Types

## New-product strategies

| Strategy | Definition |
|---|---|
| Price skimming | Set a high initial price, then lower it over time to reach additional customer segments after capturing customers willing to pay more. |
| Market penetration pricing | Set the lowest feasible initial price to gain customers and market share quickly from the beginning of the product life cycle. |
| Break-even pricing | Choose a price intended to cover fixed and variable manufacturing costs; the break-even point produces zero profit. |

Break-even unit volume is:

```text
break-even units = total fixed costs / (unit price - variable cost per unit)
```

## Existing-product strategies and tactics

| Strategy or tactic | Definition |
|---|---|
| Product line pricing | Establish related price levels across products or product lines to address different value perceptions and target markets. |
| Captive product pricing | Price a core product together with a required complementary product to maximize the perceived value and combined return. |
| Bundle pricing | Offer several products together at a combined price that encourages the customer to buy more than one item. |
| Odd-even pricing | Use an odd-ending price to suggest value or an even-ending price to suggest higher quality. |
| Economy pricing | Keep price and promotional expense low, relying on high volume and a no-frills value position. |
