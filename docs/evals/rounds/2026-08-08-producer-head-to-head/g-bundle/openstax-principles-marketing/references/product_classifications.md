# Product Classifications

Marketing first distinguishes products bought for personal use from products bought by organizations to create or support other offerings.

## Consumer products

| Type | Definition | Typical purchase pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Convenience product | An easily and quickly purchased item requiring little deliberation. | Often low priced, habitually purchased, and widely distributed. |
| Shopping product | An item for which customers compare alternatives such as price and quality. | More deliberation, selective distribution, and commonly a higher price than convenience products. |
| Specialty product | An offering with unique qualities for which a customer will make a special effort. | Strong preference, substantial planning, little comparison shopping, and limited distribution. |
| Unsought product | An offering the customer does not anticipate buying or may not know is needed. | Awareness and demand are low until a triggering need or event occurs. |

## Business and industrial products

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Raw materials | Inputs purchased to make another good. |
| Manufactured materials and parts | Fabricated components used to create the final product. |
| Capital items | Tangible assets of lasting value used by the business. |
| Supplies and services | Consumable goods and supporting services that do not become durable business assets. |

## Three levels of a product

- **Core product:** the fundamental benefit or problem solution the customer is actually buying.
- **Actual product:** the tangible or directly delivered offering, including features, brand, quality, packaging, and design.
- **Augmented product:** supporting benefits such as warranties, customer service, and product support.
