# Product Life Cycle Stages

The product life cycle maps the stages an offering passes through by tracking sales and profitability.

| Stage | Sales and profitability | Common marketing implications |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Awareness and sales begin to build; profitability is low because launch and scaling costs are high. | Invest in awareness, trial, sales promotion, and initial distribution. |
| Growth | Sales increase and profitability can improve; competitors may enter after observing demand. | Improve the product, expand distribution, and shift promotion from awareness toward preference. |
| Maturity | Sales growth slows and profitability levels off; this is often the longest stage. | Defend share, consider market or product modification, and maintain reminder promotion. |
| Decline | Sales and profitability decrease significantly as needs, technology, tastes, costs, or competition change. | Reduce activity, harvest remaining returns, sell the brand, or discontinue the product. |

Products do not necessarily move through every stage at a uniform or linear rate. An unsuccessful introduction may lead directly to decline, while a fad may grow and decline rapidly.
