# Retailer Types

Retailers sell goods and services to consumers. They are broadly classified as store retailers or non-store retailers and further distinguished by product line, service model, and selling method.

## Store retailers

| Type | Typical focus |
|---|---|
| Specialty store | A single product type or product line, often supported by knowledgeable employees and high service. |
| Department store | A wide variety of product lines arranged into separate departments. |
| Supermarket | Primarily groceries, with household and personal items and limited supporting services. |
| Superstore | A very large combination of supermarket and department-store characteristics. |
| Convenience store | A small, conveniently located store carrying a limited range of everyday items. |
| Category killer | A very large, low-price retailer with an extensive assortment within one category. |
| Discount store | A broad product range at lower prices, usually with fewer services. |
| Off-price retailer | Brand-name overstock, irregular, closeout, or off-season goods at deep discounts. |
| Factory outlet | Overstocked or over-manufactured goods sold directly by the manufacturer at discounted prices. |
| Warehouse club | Bulk goods sold at discounted prices to members from a no-frills facility. |

## Non-store retailers

| Type | Selling method |
|---|---|
| Automatic vending | An electronic device dispenses the product without direct human contact. |
| Direct mail and catalogs | Products are advertised and ordered through mailed material. |
| Television home shopping | Products or services are demonstrated and sold through television, with orders placed remotely. |
| Online retailing | Customers search for and purchase products remotely over the Internet. |
| Telemarketing | Goods or services are marketed or sold to prospective customers by telephone. |
| Direct selling | A salesperson sells directly to a customer outside a retail store, often at a residence or workplace. |
