# Service Characteristics

Services are nonphysical, intangible economic activities. Four characteristics create distinctive marketing and operational constraints.

| Characteristic | Meaning | Marketing implication |
|---|---|---|
| Intangibility | A service cannot be seen, tasted, felt, smelled, or heard before purchase. | Customers face uncertainty, so providers use evidence, demonstrations, credentials, and brand promises to make quality easier to assess. |
| Inseparability | Production and consumption occur together, and delivery cannot be separated from the provider or the participating customer. | Frontline behavior, customer participation, and the service encounter directly affect perceived quality. |
| Variability | Quality depends on who provides the service and when, where, and how it is delivered. | Providers use process standards, training, audits, surveys, and feedback to improve consistency. |
| Perishability | Unused service capacity cannot be stored in inventory for later sale. | Capacity, scheduling, pricing, and demand management must address expiring hotel rooms, seats, appointments, and similar availability. |
