# Biopsychology, Consciousness, and Sensation Glossary

## Neural Communication

- **Neuron** — a nervous-system cell that acts as an interconnected information processor.
- **Dendrite** — a branch-like extension of the soma that receives signals from other neurons.
- **Soma** — the cell body of a neuron.
- **Axon** — the major extension of the soma.
- **Myelin sheath** — a fatty substance that insulates axons.
- **Action potential** — an electrical signal that travels down a neuron’s axon.
- **All-or-none principle** — the rule that an incoming signal either reaches the threshold of excitation and triggers an action potential or does not.
- **Resting potential** — the ready state of a neuron’s membrane between signals.
- **Synaptic cleft** — the small gap across which two neurons communicate.
- **Neurotransmitter** — a chemical messenger of the nervous system.
- **Receptor** — a protein on a cell surface to which neurotransmitters attach.
- **Reuptake** — pumping a released neurotransmitter back into the neuron that released it.
- **Agonist** — a drug that mimics or strengthens a neurotransmitter’s effects.
- **Antagonist** — a drug that blocks or impedes a neurotransmitter’s normal activity.
- **Neuroplasticity** — the nervous system’s ability to change.

## Nervous and Endocrine Systems

- **Central nervous system (CNS)** — the brain and spinal cord.
- **Peripheral nervous system (PNS)** — the system connecting the brain and spinal cord to peripheral muscles, organs, and senses.
- **Somatic nervous system** — the division that relays sensory and motor information to and from the CNS.
- **Autonomic nervous system** — the division that controls internal organs and glands.
- **Sympathetic nervous system** — the autonomic division involved in stress-related activities and functions.
- **Parasympathetic nervous system** — the autonomic division associated with routine, day-to-day bodily operations.
- **Endocrine system** — the series of glands that produce hormones.
- **Hormone** — a chemical messenger released by an endocrine gland.
- **Homeostasis** — maintenance of biological conditions at optimal levels.

## Major Brain Structures

- **Cerebral cortex** — the brain surface associated with its highest mental capabilities.
- **Frontal lobe** — the cortical region involved in reasoning, motor control, emotion, and language.
- **Parietal lobe** — the cortical region that processes sensory and perceptual information.
- **Temporal lobe** — the cortical region associated with hearing, memory, emotion, and aspects of language.
- **Occipital lobe** — the cortical region associated with visual processing.
- **Thalamus** — the brain’s sensory relay.
- **Hypothalamus** — a forebrain structure that regulates homeostasis and sexual motivation and links the nervous and endocrine systems.
- **Limbic system** — a collection of structures involved in processing emotion and memory.
- **Amygdala** — a limbic structure involved in emotion and in attaching emotional meaning to memories.
- **Hippocampus** — a temporal-lobe structure associated with learning and memory.
- **Cerebellum** — a hindbrain structure involved in balance, coordination, movement, motor skills, and some forms of memory.
- **Broca’s area** — a region in the left hemisphere essential for language production.
- **Wernicke’s area** — a region important for speech comprehension.

## Consciousness and Sleep

- **Consciousness** — awareness of internal and external stimuli.
- **Circadian rhythm** — a biological rhythm occurring over approximately 24 hours.
- **Suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN)** — the hypothalamic area containing the body’s biological clock.
- **Sleep** — a state of reduced physical activity and sensory awareness that is distinct from waking rest.
- **Non-REM (NREM) sleep** — sleep outside periods of rapid eye movement.
- **Rapid eye movement (REM) sleep** — sleep characterized by wake-like brain waves and darting eye movements beneath closed eyelids.
- **Stage 1 sleep** — the transition from wakefulness to sleep.
- **Stage 2 sleep** — a deeper relaxation stage characterized by sleep spindles.
- **Stage 3 sleep** — deep sleep characterized by low-frequency, high-amplitude delta waves.
- **Sleep spindle** — a rapid burst of high-frequency brain waves during stage 2 sleep that may support learning and memory.
- **Sleep debt** — the accumulated result of chronic insufficient sleep.
- **Sleep apnea** — a disorder involving episodes in which breathing stops during sleep.
- **Insomnia** — consistent difficulty falling or staying asleep at least three nights per week for one month.
- **Narcolepsy** — a disorder in which a person cannot resist falling asleep at inappropriate times.
- **Parasomnia** — a sleep disorder involving unwanted, disruptive motor activity or experiences during sleep.

## Sensation and Perception

- **Sensation** — detection of sensory information by a sensory receptor.
- **Perception** — interpretation and conscious experience of sensory information.
- **Transduction** — conversion of sensory stimulus energy into an action potential.
- **Absolute threshold** — the minimum stimulus energy detected 50 percent of the time.
- **Just noticeable difference** — the minimum difference between stimuli required to notice that they differ.
- **Sensory adaptation** — reduced perception of stimuli that remain relatively constant over time.
- **Signal detection theory** — an account of how stimulus detection changes with a person’s current mental state.
- **Bottom-up processing** — building perceptions from sensory input.
- **Top-down processing** — interpreting sensations using knowledge, experience, and thought.
- **Inattentional blindness** — failure to notice a fully visible stimulus because attention is directed elsewhere.
- **Depth perception** — the ability to perceive depth.
- **Binocular cue** — a depth cue requiring both eyes.
- **Monocular cue** — a depth cue requiring only one eye.
- **Proprioception** — perception of body position.
- **Kinesthesia** — perception of the body’s movement through space.
- **Vestibular sense** — the sense contributing to balance and posture.

## Gestalt Organization

- **Gestalt psychology** — an approach based on the idea that a whole differs from the sum of its parts.
- **Figure-ground relationship** — segmentation of the visual field into a focal figure and its background.
- **Closure** — organizing perceptions into complete objects rather than disconnected parts.
- **Good continuation** — the tendency to perceive smooth, continuous lines rather than jagged or broken ones.
- **Proximity** — the tendency to group things that are near one another.
- **Similarity** — the tendency to group things that are alike.
