# Development, Motivation, and Personality Glossary

## Lifespan Development

- **Continuous development** — the view that development is a cumulative process of gradually improving existing skills.
- **Discontinuous development** — the view that development occurs in distinct stages at particular times or ages.
- **Developmental milestone** — an approximate age at which children reach a specific normative event.
- **Physical development** — the domain examining growth and changes in the body and brain, senses, motor skills, health, and wellness.
- **Cognitive development** — the domain examining learning, attention, memory, language, thinking, reasoning, and creativity.
- **Psychosocial development** — the domain examining emotion, personality, and social relationships.
- **Schema** — a mental model used to categorize and interpret information.
- **Assimilation** — adding new information that resembles what a schema already contains.
- **Accommodation** — changing a schema to incorporate information that differs from what was previously known.
- **Object permanence** — understanding that an object continues to exist when it is out of sight.
- **Sensorimotor stage** — Piaget’s first stage, from birth through about age two, in which children learn through senses and motor behavior.
- **Preoperational stage** — Piaget’s second stage, roughly ages two through seven, in which children use symbols and language but do not yet understand mental operations.
- **Concrete operational stage** — Piaget’s third stage, roughly ages seven through eleven, in which children reason logically about concrete events.
- **Formal operational stage** — Piaget’s final stage, beginning around age eleven, in which people can reason about abstract ideas and hypothetical situations.
- **Temperament** — innate traits that influence how a person thinks, behaves, and responds to the environment.

## Attachment and Parenting

- **Attachment** — a long-standing connection or bond with another person.
- **Secure attachment** — an attachment pattern in which a child uses a caregiver as a secure base for exploration.
- **Avoidant attachment** — an attachment pattern marked by unresponsiveness to a caregiver and little reaction when the caregiver leaves.
- **Resistant attachment** — an attachment pattern marked by clinginess and rejection when a caregiver attempts to interact.
- **Disorganized attachment** — an attachment pattern marked by unusual behavior toward a caregiver, commonly observed among abused children.
- **Authoritative parenting style** — parenting with reasonable demands and consistent limits alongside warmth, affection, and responsiveness to the child’s perspective.
- **Authoritarian parenting style** — rigid parenting that prioritizes conformity and obedience and expresses little warmth.
- **Permissive parenting style** — parenting that makes few demands and rarely uses punishment.
- **Uninvolved parenting style** — parenting that is indifferent to a child’s needs and makes few demands.

## Motivation and Emotion

- **Motivation** — wants or needs that direct behavior toward a goal.
- **Intrinsic motivation** — motivation arising from internal feelings.
- **Extrinsic motivation** — motivation arising from external factors or rewards.
- **Drive theory** — the theory that deviations from homeostasis create needs and drive states that direct behavior toward restoring balance.
- **Hierarchy of needs** — a spectrum of needs ranging from basic biological needs through social needs to self-actualization.
- **Self-efficacy** — a person’s belief in their capability to complete a task.
- **Yerkes-Dodson law** — the principle that simple tasks are performed best at relatively high arousal while complex tasks are performed best at lower arousal.
- **Emotion** — a subjective state commonly described as a feeling.
- **Components of emotion** — physiological arousal, psychological appraisal, and subjective experience.
- **James-Lange theory of emotion** — the theory that emotion arises from physiological arousal.
- **Cannon-Bard theory of emotion** — the theory that physiological arousal and emotional experience occur at the same time.
- **Schachter-Singer two-factor theory** — the theory that emotion consists of physiological and cognitive factors.
- **Cognitive-mediational theory** — the theory that appraisal of a stimulus determines emotional experience.
- **Facial-feedback hypothesis** — the proposal that facial expressions can influence emotion.
- **Cultural display rule** — a culturally specific standard governing which emotions may be expressed and how often.

## Personality

- **Personality** — long-standing traits and patterns that lead people to think, feel, and behave in consistent ways.
- **Trait** — a characteristic way of behaving.
- **Five-factor model** — the theory that personality consists of openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism.
- **Locus of control** — beliefs about personal power over life outcomes; an internal locus attributes control to oneself, while an external locus places outcomes outside personal control.
- **Reciprocal determinism** — the idea that environments influence behavior while people also influence their environments through thoughts and behavior.
- **Social-cognitive theory** — Bandura’s personality theory emphasizing cognition and learning as sources of individual differences.
- **Self-concept** — a person’s thoughts and feelings about themself.
- **Congruence** — close alignment between a person’s real and ideal selves.
- **Incongruence** — a large discrepancy between a person’s real and ideal selves.
- **Id** — the aspect of personality containing primitive drives and urges.
- **Ego** — the aspect of personality representing the self visible to others.
- **Superego** — the aspect of personality functioning as a moral compass or conscience.
- **Defense mechanism** — an unconscious protective behavior that reduces ego anxiety.
- **Repression** — keeping anxiety-related thoughts and memories out of conscious awareness.
- **Projection** — attributing one’s own unacceptable urges or behaviors to other people.
- **Displacement** — redirecting an inappropriate urge or behavior toward a less threatening target.
- **Sublimation** — channeling an unacceptable urge into a more socially appropriate activity.
- **Rationalization** — making excuses to justify a behavior that causes anxiety.
- **Regression** — returning to a less mature behavioral state when confronting anxiety.
