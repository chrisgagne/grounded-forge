# Foundations and Research Glossary

## Foundations

- **Psychology** — the scientific study of mind and behavior.
- **Empirical method** — a way of acquiring knowledge through observation and experimentation rather than relying only on logical argument or prior authorities.
- **Behaviorism** — a perspective focused on observing and controlling behavior.
- **Biopsychology** — the study of how biology influences behavior.
- **Biopsychosocial model** — the perspective that biological, psychological, and social factors interact to determine health.
- **Cognitive psychology** — the study of thoughts and their relationship to experiences and actions.
- **Developmental psychology** — the scientific study of development across the lifespan.
- **Humanism** — a perspective that emphasizes the potential for good innate in all people.
- **Personality psychology** — the study of patterns of thought and behavior that make individuals unique.
- **Psychoanalytic theory** — a perspective focused on how the unconscious affects conscious behavior.
- **Structuralism** — an approach to understanding conscious experience through introspection.
- **Functionalism** — an approach focused on how mental activities help an organism adapt to its environment.

## Evidence and Reasoning

- **Fact** — an objective, verifiable observation established using evidence from empirical research.
- **Theory** — a well-developed set of ideas that proposes an explanation for observed phenomena.
- **Hypothesis** — a testable prediction about how the world will behave if an idea is correct, often expressed as an if-then statement.
- **Falsifiable** — capable of being disproved by experimental results.
- **Deductive reasoning** — predicting results from a general premise.
- **Inductive reasoning** — drawing conclusions from observations.
- **Confirmation bias** — the tendency to ignore evidence that disproves an idea or belief.
- **Replication** — repeating an experiment with different samples to assess the reliability of its findings.
- **Reliability** — the consistency and reproducibility of a result.
- **Validity** — the accuracy with which a result measures what it is intended to measure.

## Research Designs

- **Archival research** — using past records or datasets to answer research questions or identify patterns and relationships.
- **Clinical or case study** — observational research focused on one person or a small number of people.
- **Cross-sectional research** — comparing multiple segments of a population at a single point in time.
- **Longitudinal research** — repeatedly surveying or measuring the same group over an extended period.
- **Naturalistic observation** — observing behavior in its natural setting.
- **Survey** — collecting data from many people with questions administered on paper, electronically, or verbally.
- **Cause-and-effect relationship** — a relationship in which changes in one variable cause changes in another; it can be established only with an experimental research design.
- **Correlation** — a relationship in which one variable changes as another variable changes.
- **Correlation coefficient** — a number from −1 to +1, usually represented by *r*, that indicates the direction and strength of a relationship between variables.
- **Positive correlation** — a relationship in which two variables change in the same direction.
- **Negative correlation** — a relationship in which two variables change in opposite directions; it is not the same as no correlation.
- **Illusory correlation** — perceiving a relationship between two things when no such relationship exists.

## Experimental Variables and Controls

- **Independent variable** — the variable controlled or influenced by the experimenter.
- **Dependent variable** — the variable measured to determine the effect of the independent variable.
- **Confounding variable** — an unanticipated outside factor that affects both variables of interest and can create a false impression of causation.
- **Operational definition** — the specific actions and operations used to measure dependent variables and manipulate independent variables.
- **Experimental group** — the group that receives the experimental manipulation.
- **Control group** — the comparison group used to hold chance factors constant so the manipulation is the key difference between groups.
- **Random assignment** — assigning participants to groups so each participant has an equal chance of entering any group.
- **Single-blind study** — an experiment in which the researcher knows participants’ group assignments but the participants do not.
- **Double-blind study** — an experiment in which neither researchers nor participants know participants’ group assignments.
- **Placebo effect** — a change in experience produced by a person’s expectations or beliefs.
- **Experimenter bias** — distortion of results by researchers’ expectations.
- **Observer bias** — recording or classification of observations that is skewed toward observers’ expectations.
- **Inter-rater reliability** — the degree of agreement among observers recording and classifying an event.

## Sampling and Ethics

- **Population** — the full group in which researchers are interested.
- **Sample** — a subset selected from a larger population.
- **Random sample** — a subset selected so every member of the population has an equal chance of inclusion.
- **Generalization** — inferring that results from a sample apply to the larger population.
- **Attrition** — loss of research participants as some leave a study over time.
- **Informed consent** — informing a participant about procedures, risks, and implications before obtaining agreement to participate.
- **Deception** — intentionally misleading participants when necessary to preserve the integrity of an experiment.
- **Debriefing** — giving participants complete and truthful information after an experiment involving deception.
- **Institutional Review Board (IRB)** — a committee of administrators, scientists, and community members that reviews proposed research with human participants.
- **Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC)** — a committee of administrators, scientists, veterinarians, and community members that reviews proposed research with nonhuman animals.
