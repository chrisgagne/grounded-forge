# Learning, Cognition, and Memory Glossary

## Learning and Conditioning

- **Learning** — a change in behavior or knowledge resulting from experience.
- **Associative learning** — learning that connects stimuli or events that occur together, as in classical and operant conditioning.
- **Classical conditioning** — learning in which a stimulus or experience occurs before a behavior and becomes associated with it.
- **Neutral stimulus (NS)** — a stimulus that does not initially elicit a response.
- **Unconditioned stimulus (UCS)** — a stimulus that naturally elicits a reflexive response.
- **Unconditioned response (UCR)** — a natural, unlearned response to a stimulus.
- **Conditioned stimulus (CS)** — a stimulus that elicits a response after being paired with an unconditioned stimulus.
- **Conditioned response (CR)** — the response elicited by a conditioned stimulus.
- **Acquisition** — the initial phase in which a neutral stimulus becomes associated with an unconditioned stimulus.
- **Extinction** — reduction of a conditioned response when the unconditioned stimulus is no longer paired with the conditioned stimulus.
- **Spontaneous recovery** — return of a conditioned response after it had been extinguished.
- **Stimulus discrimination** — responding differently to similar stimuli.
- **Stimulus generalization** — producing a conditioned response to stimuli similar to the conditioned stimulus.
- **Operant conditioning** — learning in which consequences occur after a behavior.
- **Reinforcement** — applying a consequence to increase a behavior.
- **Punishment** — applying a consequence to decrease a behavior.
- **Positive reinforcement** — adding a desirable stimulus to increase a behavior.
- **Negative reinforcement** — removing an undesirable stimulus to increase a behavior.
- **Positive punishment** — adding an undesirable stimulus to reduce a behavior.
- **Negative punishment** — removing a pleasant stimulus to reduce a behavior.
- **Shaping** — reinforcing successive approximations toward a target behavior.
- **Observational learning** — learning by watching other people.
- **Vicarious reinforcement** — becoming more likely to imitate a behavior after seeing a model rewarded.
- **Vicarious punishment** — becoming less likely to imitate a behavior after seeing a model punished.

## Cognition and Problem Solving

- **Cognition** — thinking, including perception, learning, problem solving, judgment, and memory.
- **Concept** — a category or grouping of linguistic information, objects, ideas, or experiences.
- **Schema** — a mental construct consisting of a cluster of related concepts.
- **Prototype** — the best representation of a concept.
- **Algorithm** — a problem-solving strategy consisting of a specific set of instructions.
- **Heuristic** — a mental shortcut used to save time while solving a problem.
- **Availability heuristic** — deciding on the basis of information that comes readily to mind.
- **Representativeness bias** — judging by a stereotype without an adequate factual basis.
- **Anchoring bias** — fixating on a single aspect of a problem while seeking a solution.
- **Functional fixedness** — inability to see an object as useful for anything beyond its customary purpose.
- **Mental set** — continuing to apply an old solution even when it is not working.
- **Trial and error** — trying multiple solutions until one succeeds.
- **Convergent thinking** — producing established or conventionally correct answers.
- **Divergent thinking** — generating novel approaches or solutions.
- **Creativity** — the ability to generate or discover new ideas, solutions, and possibilities.

## Intelligence and Language

- **Fluid intelligence** — the ability to see complex relationships and solve problems.
- **Crystallized intelligence** — acquired knowledge and the ability to retrieve it.
- **Analytical intelligence** — intelligence aligned with academic problem solving and computation.
- **Creative intelligence** — the ability to produce new ideas, products, or novel solutions.
- **Practical intelligence** — intelligence applied to everyday, real-world problems.
- **Emotional intelligence** — the ability to understand emotions and motivations in oneself and others.
- **Triarchic theory of intelligence** — Sternberg’s theory that intelligence has practical, creative, and analytical facets.
- **Multiple intelligences theory** — Gardner’s theory that people possess at least eight types of intelligence.
- **Language** — a communication system that uses words to transmit information.
- **Phoneme** — the basic sound unit of a language.
- **Morpheme** — the smallest language unit that conveys meaning.
- **Lexicon** — the words of a language.
- **Grammar** — the rules used to convey meaning through a lexicon.
- **Semantics** — the process of deriving meaning from morphemes and words.
- **Syntax** — the organization of words into sentences.

## Memory Processes and Systems

- **Memory** — the processes used to encode, store, and retrieve information over time.
- **Encoding** — inputting information into the memory system.
- **Storage** — creating a permanent record of information.
- **Retrieval** — bringing information from long-term storage back into conscious awareness.
- **Sensory memory** — brief storage of sensory events such as sights, sounds, and tastes.
- **Short-term memory (STM)** — a limited-capacity store that holds information briefly or while it is in use.
- **Long-term memory (LTM)** — the continuous storage of information.
- **Explicit memory** — memory a person consciously tries to remember and recall.
- **Implicit memory** — memory outside conscious awareness.
- **Declarative memory** — long-term memory for facts and personally experienced events.
- **Episodic memory** — declarative memory for events a person has experienced.
- **Semantic memory** — declarative memory for words, concepts, and language-based facts.
- **Procedural memory** — long-term memory for skilled actions.
- **Effortful processing** — encoding that requires effort and attention.
- **Automatic processing** — encoding details such as time, space, frequency, and word meaning without deliberate effort.
- **Elaborative rehearsal** — connecting new information’s meaning to knowledge already stored in memory.
- **Chunking** — organizing information into manageable units.
- **Recall** — accessing information without cues.
- **Recognition** — identifying learned information when encountering it again.
- **Relearning** — learning again something learned previously.

## Memory Errors

- **Anterograde amnesia** — loss of memory for events occurring after brain trauma.
- **Retrograde amnesia** — loss of memory for events occurring before brain trauma.
- **Proactive interference** — old information obstructing recall of newer information.
- **Retroactive interference** — newer information obstructing recall of older information.
- **Misinformation effect** — misremembering an original event after exposure to additional, potentially inaccurate information.
- **Misattribution** — confusing the source of remembered information.
- **Suggestibility** — creation of false memories under the influence of misinformation from outside sources.
- **Transience** — fading of unused memories over time.
- **Persistence** — involuntary recall of unwanted, often unpleasant memories.
