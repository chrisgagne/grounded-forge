# Psychological Disorders and Treatment Glossary

## Classification and Clinical Concepts

- **Psychological disorder** — a condition characterized by atypical thoughts, feelings, and behaviors.
- **Psychopathology** — the study of psychological disorders, including their symptoms, causes, and treatment, or the manifestation of a disorder.
- **Diagnosis** — identifying which disorder a set of symptoms represents.
- **Etiology** — the cause or causes of a psychological disorder.
- **Comorbidity** — co-occurrence of two disorders in the same person.
- **Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5)** — the American Psychiatric Association’s index of mental disorders and diagnostic criteria.
- **International Classification of Diseases (ICD)** — the World Health Organization’s index of mental and physical diseases and diagnostic criteria.
- **Diathesis-stress model** — the model proposing that a predisposition makes a disorder more likely to develop when a person encounters stress.
- **Harmful dysfunction** — a model defining psychological disorder through failure of an internal mechanism to perform its natural function.

## Anxiety, Trauma, and Obsessive-Compulsive Disorders

- **Anxiety disorder** — a disorder characterized by excessive and persistent fear and anxiety with related disturbances in behavior.
- **Generalized anxiety disorder** — a continuous state of excessive, uncontrollable, and purposeless worry and apprehension.
- **Panic attack** — an abrupt period of extreme fear or discomfort with physiological and psychological symptoms.
- **Panic disorder** — unexpected panic attacks accompanied by at least one month of worry or behavior related to those attacks.
- **Agoraphobia** — intense fear, anxiety, and avoidance of situations from which escape may be difficult during a panic attack.
- **Specific phobia** — excessive, distressing, and persistent fear or anxiety about a specific object or situation.
- **Social anxiety disorder** — persistent fear, anxiety, and avoidance of social situations involving possible negative evaluation.
- **Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD)** — persistent intrusive memories, avoidance, negative emotion, detachment, irritability, hypervigilance, and heightened startle after profound trauma.
- **Flashback** — a state in which a person relives a traumatic event and behaves as though it were occurring.
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder** — intrusive, unwanted thoughts or urges and repetitive behavior or mental acts performed in response.
- **Hoarding disorder** — persistent difficulty discarding possessions regardless of their actual value or usefulness.

## Mood, Psychotic, Dissociative, and Personality Disorders

- **Major depressive disorder** — a disorder characterized by sadness or loss of pleasure in usual activities alongside other symptoms.
- **Persistent depressive disorder** — a depressive disorder marked by a chronically sad and melancholy mood.
- **Bipolar disorder** — a mood disorder in which mood states fluctuate between depression and mania.
- **Mania** — a state of extreme elation and agitation.
- **Manic episode** — a period of mania involving an unusually euphoric or irritable mood, increased activity, excessive talkativeness, and related symptoms.
- **Schizophrenia** — a severe disorder involving disturbances of thought, perception, emotion, and behavior, including hallucinations, delusions, disorganization, and negative symptoms.
- **Hallucination** — a perceptual experience occurring without external stimulation.
- **Delusion** — a belief contrary to reality that remains firmly held despite contradictory evidence.
- **Negative symptom** — a reduction or absence of typical behavior, emotion, drive, speech, social engagement, or capacity for pleasure.
- **Dissociative disorder** — a disorder involving separation from one’s core sense of self with disturbances of identity and memory.
- **Dissociative amnesia** — inability to recall important personal information, usually after an extremely stressful or traumatic experience.
- **Dissociative identity disorder** — the presence of two or more distinct identities with memory gaps when another identity is active.
- **Personality disorder** — an inflexible, pervasive personality style that differs markedly from cultural expectations and causes distress and impairment.
- **Antisocial personality disorder** — disregard for others’ rights accompanied by impulsivity, deceitfulness, irresponsibility, and lack of remorse.
- **Borderline personality disorder** — instability in relationships, self-image, and mood, with impulsivity, fear of abandonment, unpredictable behavior, and inappropriate anger.

## Treatment Orientations

- **Psychotherapy** — psychological treatment using varied methods to help a person overcome problems or achieve personal growth.
- **Biomedical therapy** — treatment using medication or medical procedures for psychological disorders.
- **Behavior therapy** — treatment using learning principles to change undesirable behavior.
- **Cognitive therapy** — treatment focused on changing irrational thoughts that produce distress.
- **Cognitive-behavioral therapy** — treatment aimed at changing cognitive distortions and self-defeating behavior.
- **Humanistic therapy** — treatment aimed at increasing self-awareness and self-acceptance.
- **Psychoanalysis** — Freud’s therapeutic approach using free association, dream analysis, and transference to uncover repressed feelings.
- **Rogerian client-centered therapy** — a nondirective humanistic therapy emphasizing unconditional positive regard and self-acceptance.
- **Exposure therapy** — treatment of fear or anxiety through presentation of the feared object or situation until the response diminishes.
- **Systematic desensitization** — exposure therapy that presents a feared object or situation through a stimulus hierarchy.
- **Counterconditioning** — using classical conditioning to establish a new response to a stimulus that previously elicited an undesirable response.
- **Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)** — a biomedical treatment that induces seizures with electrical current to reduce severe depression.

## Treatment Modalities and Practice

- **Individual therapy** — treatment in which one client meets one-on-one with a clinician.
- **Group therapy** — treatment in which a trained clinician meets with a small group sharing an issue or concern.
- **Couples therapy** — treatment for two people in an intimate relationship seeking to resolve difficulties.
- **Family therapy** — group therapy involving one or more families.
- **Play therapy** — therapy, often used with children, that uses toys to help resolve psychological problems.
- **Voluntary treatment** — treatment a person chooses in order to obtain relief from symptoms.
- **Involuntary treatment** — treatment mandated by a court or another system.
- **Intake** — the first therapist-client meeting, during which information is gathered to address immediate needs.
- **Confidentiality** — the obligation not to disclose therapeutic communications to third parties except when law permits or requires it.
- **Cultural competence** — a therapist’s understanding of and attention to race, culture, and ethnicity in treatment.
- **Unconditional positive regard** — fundamental acceptance of a person regardless of what they say or do.
- **Relapse** — renewed drug or alcohol use after a period of improvement.
