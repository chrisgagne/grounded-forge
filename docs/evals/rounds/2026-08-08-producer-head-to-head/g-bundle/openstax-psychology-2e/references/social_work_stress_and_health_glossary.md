# Social, Work, Stress, and Health Glossary

## Social Cognition and Influence

- **Social psychology** — the field examining how people affect one another, especially the power of situations.
- **Attribution** — an explanation for another person’s behavior.
- **Fundamental attribution error** — overemphasizing internal causes of another person’s behavior while underestimating situational forces.
- **Actor-observer bias** — explaining other people’s behavior with internal factors while explaining one’s own behavior with situational factors.
- **Self-serving bias** — taking internal credit for positive outcomes while attributing negative outcomes to external situations.
- **Attitude** — a typically positive or negative evaluation of or feeling toward a person, idea, or object.
- **Cognitive dissonance** — discomfort arising from conflict among behavior, attitudes, or beliefs and a positive self-image.
- **Persuasion** — changing an attitude through communication.
- **Conformity** — changing behavior to align with a group despite not agreeing with it.
- **Obedience** — changing behavior to satisfy an authority figure or avoid adverse consequences.
- **Informational social influence** — conforming because the group is believed to be competent and correct.
- **Normative social influence** — conforming to fit in, feel accepted, or gain approval.
- **Groupthink** — modifying individual opinions to match a perceived group consensus.
- **Group polarization** — strengthening a group’s initial attitude after members discuss their views.
- **Social loafing** — exerting less effort when individual performance cannot be evaluated separately from group performance.
- **Bystander effect** — failure of a witness to volunteer help to a person in distress.
- **Diffusion of responsibility** — reduced likelihood of helping when responsibility is spread among a group.

## Groups and Relationships

- **Social norm** — a group’s expectation for acceptable thought and behavior.
- **Social role** — a socially defined behavior pattern expected from a person in a particular setting or group.
- **Stereotype** — a belief or assumption about people based only on group membership.
- **Prejudice** — a negative attitude or feeling toward people based only on group membership.
- **Discrimination** — negative action toward people based on group membership.
- **In-group bias** — preference for one’s own group over other groups.
- **Empathy** — the capacity to understand another person’s perspective and feel what they feel.
- **Prosocial behavior** — voluntary behavior intended to help other people.
- **Altruism** — helping others even when the costs of doing so exceed the benefits.
- **Reciprocity** — give-and-take within relationships.
- **Homophily** — the tendency to form social relationships with people who are similar.

## Work and Organizations

- **Industrial and organizational (I-O) psychology** — the application of psychological science to work and the workplace.
- **Industrial psychology** — the study of job and applicant characteristics, employee selection, training, and performance appraisal.
- **Organizational psychology** — the study of interactions among people at work and their effects on productivity.
- **Human factors psychology** — the study of how workers interact with tools and how tools can be designed for productivity, safety, and health.
- **Job analysis** — identifying and listing the tasks associated with a job.
- **Performance appraisal** — evaluation of an employee’s success in performing job duties.
- **Job satisfaction** — the degree of pleasure an employee derives from work.
- **Organizational culture** — an organization’s values, visions, hierarchies, norms, interactions, operations, and decision practices.
- **Procedural justice** — fairness in the methods used to produce organizational outcomes.
- **Hawthorne effect** — increased performance when people know they are being noticed, observed, and attended to.
- **Transactional leadership** — leadership focused on supervision and organizational goals through rewards and punishments.
- **Transformational leadership** — leadership that uses role modeling, inspiration, intellectual stimulation, and individual consideration to change an organization.
- **Work–family balance** — management of the demands of work life alongside those of family life.

## Stress and Coping

- **Stress** — the process of perceiving and responding to events appraised as overwhelming or threatening to well-being.
- **Stressor** — an environmental event judged as threatening or demanding that initiates the stress process.
- **Eustress** — low- to moderate-intensity stress associated with positive feelings and optimal health and performance.
- **Distress** — high-intensity stress associated with exhaustion, burnout, poorer performance, and poorer health.
- **Primary appraisal** — judgment of how much harm or threat a stressor may pose.
- **Secondary appraisal** — judgment of available coping options and their likely effectiveness.
- **Coping** — mental or behavioral efforts to manage a stressor, its cause, and the feelings it produces.
- **Perceived control** — a person’s belief in their capacity to influence outcomes.
- **Social support** — assistance from others through advice, guidance, encouragement, acceptance, comfort, or tangible help.
- **Biofeedback** — using electronic measurement of involuntary activity and feedback to develop voluntary control over that activity.
- **Relaxation-response technique** — a stress-reduction method combining relaxation and meditation.

## Stress Physiology and Health

- **Fight-or-flight response** — physiological reactions to perceived threat produced by activation of the sympathetic nervous and endocrine systems.
- **General adaptation syndrome** — Selye’s three-stage model of stress response: alarm reaction, resistance, and exhaustion.
- **Alarm reaction** — the immediate physiological reaction to threat or emergency.
- **Stage of resistance** — the period in which the body adapts to a stressor.
- **Stage of exhaustion** — depletion of the body’s capacity to resist stress, increasing the risk of illness or death.
- **Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis** — hypothalamic, pituitary, and adrenal structures that regulate physiological stress reactions through hormones.
- **Cortisol** — a hormone released by the adrenal glands during stress that provides energy for action.
- **Psychoneuroimmunology** — the study of how psychological factors such as stress influence immune functioning.
- **Psychophysiological disorder** — a physical disorder whose symptoms are caused or worsened by stress and emotional factors.
- **Immunosuppression** — decreased effectiveness of the immune system.
- **Job burnout** — work-related emotional exhaustion, cynicism, depersonalization, and diminished accomplishment.
- **Job strain** — the combination of excessive work demands with little decision latitude or control.
- **Health psychology** — the study of psychological influences on health, illness, and responses to illness.
- **Positive psychology** — the scientific study of qualities that contribute to happy, fulfilled, and contented lives.
