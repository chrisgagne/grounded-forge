# Pillars

Scrum implements empiricism through three interdependent pillars:

- **Transparency:** the emergent process and work must be visible to both those performing the work and those receiving it. Decisions depend on the perceived state of Scrum's three formal artifacts; low transparency can diminish value and increase risk.
- **Inspection:** Scrum artifacts and progress toward agreed goals are inspected frequently and diligently to detect undesirable variances or problems. Scrum's events provide a regular cadence for inspection.
- **Adaptation:** when a process deviates beyond acceptable limits or a product is unacceptable, the process or materials must be adjusted as soon as possible to minimize further deviation.

# Relationships

Transparency enables meaningful inspection; inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful. Inspection enables adaptation; inspection without adaptation is pointless.

Adaptation is more difficult when people are not empowered or self-managing. A Scrum Team is expected to adapt when it learns something new through inspection.
