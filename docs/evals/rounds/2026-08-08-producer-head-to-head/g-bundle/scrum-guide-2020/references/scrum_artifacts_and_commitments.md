# Artifact-to-commitment mapping

Scrum artifacts represent work or value and maximize the transparency of key information. Each artifact has a commitment that reinforces empiricism and the Scrum values by providing information that enhances transparency and focus:

| Artifact | Commitment | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product Backlog | Product Goal | Progress toward a long-term product objective |
| Sprint Backlog | Sprint Goal | Progress toward the single objective for the Sprint |
| Increment | Definition of Done | The quality state required for usable, completed work |

# Product Backlog

The Product Backlog is an emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product and is the single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team.

Product Backlog items become ready for selection in Sprint Planning when the Scrum Team can complete them within one Sprint. Ongoing refinement breaks down and further defines items, adding details such as description, order, and size. Developers who will perform the work are responsible for sizing; the Product Owner may help them understand and select trade-offs.

## Product Goal

The Product Goal describes a future state of the product that serves as a target for the Scrum Team's planning. It resides in the Product Backlog, whose remaining content emerges to define what will fulfill it.

The Product Goal is the Scrum Team's long-term objective. The team must fulfill or abandon one Product Goal before taking on the next.

# Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Backlog is composed of:

- the Sprint Goal, explaining **why**;
- the Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint, defining **what**; and
- an actionable plan for delivering the Increment, describing **how**.

It is a plan by and for the Developers and a visible, real-time picture of the work they intend to accomplish. It is updated throughout the Sprint as more is learned and must contain enough detail to support inspection in the Daily Scrum.

## Sprint Goal

The Sprint Goal is the single objective for the Sprint. It is a commitment by the Developers that creates coherence and focus while preserving flexibility in the exact work needed to achieve it.

The Scrum Team creates the Sprint Goal during Sprint Planning and adds it to the Sprint Backlog. If the required work differs from expectations, Developers collaborate with the Product Owner to renegotiate Sprint Backlog scope without affecting the Sprint Goal.

# Increment

An Increment is a concrete, usable stepping stone toward the Product Goal. Each Increment is additive to prior Increments and thoroughly verified so that all Increments work together.

Multiple Increments may be created during a Sprint, and an Increment may be delivered before the Sprint ends. The Sprint Review is not a gate to releasing value.

## Definition of Done

The Definition of Done is a formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the product's required quality measures. A Product Backlog item becomes part of an Increment when it meets the Definition of Done.

An item that does not meet the Definition of Done cannot be released or presented at the Sprint Review and returns to the Product Backlog for future consideration.

When the Definition of Done is an organizational standard, every Scrum Team must follow it as a minimum. Otherwise, the Scrum Team creates a Definition of Done appropriate for the product. Developers must conform to it, and multiple Scrum Teams working on the same product must mutually define and follow the same Definition of Done.
