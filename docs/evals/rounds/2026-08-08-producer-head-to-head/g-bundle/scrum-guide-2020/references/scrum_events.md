# Event system

The Sprint contains all other Scrum events. Each event is a formal opportunity to inspect and adapt Scrum artifacts, enable transparency, create regularity, and reduce the need for meetings not defined in Scrum. Events are optimally held at the same time and place to reduce complexity.

| Event | Purpose | Participants | Maximum timebox for a one-month Sprint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sprint | Turn ideas into value and contain all work necessary to achieve the Product Goal | Scrum Team | One month or less |
| Sprint Planning | Initiate the Sprint by collaboratively establishing why it is valuable, what can be Done, and how the work will be done | Entire Scrum Team; invited advisers may attend | 8 hours |
| Daily Scrum | Inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog and upcoming planned work | Developers; Product Owner or Scrum Master participate when actively working on Sprint Backlog items | 15 minutes every working day |
| Sprint Review | Inspect the Sprint outcome, discuss progress toward the Product Goal, and determine future adaptations with stakeholders | Scrum Team and key stakeholders | 4 hours |
| Sprint Retrospective | Plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness | Scrum Team | 3 hours |

For shorter Sprints, Sprint Planning, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective are usually shorter.

# Sprint

Sprints are fixed-length events of one month or less. A new Sprint starts immediately after the previous Sprint concludes. Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective, and all work necessary to achieve the Product Goal occur within the Sprint.

During a Sprint:

- no changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal;
- quality does not decrease;
- the Product Backlog is refined as needed; and
- scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned.

Only the Product Owner may cancel a Sprint, and only when the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.

# Sprint Planning

Sprint Planning creates the plan for the Sprint through the collaborative work of the entire Scrum Team. It addresses:

1. **Why is this Sprint valuable?** The Scrum Team collaborates to define a Sprint Goal, which must be finalized before Sprint Planning ends.
2. **What can be Done this Sprint?** Developers select Product Backlog items through discussion with the Product Owner and may refine them to improve understanding and confidence.
3. **How will the chosen work get done?** Developers plan the work needed to create an Increment that meets the Definition of Done, often by decomposing selected items into work of one day or less.

The Sprint Goal, selected Product Backlog items, and delivery plan together form the Sprint Backlog.

# Daily Scrum

The Daily Scrum is held at the same time and place every working day of the Sprint. Developers choose its structure and techniques, provided it focuses on progress toward the Sprint Goal and produces an actionable plan for the next day.

It improves communication, identifies impediments, promotes quick decisions, and supports self-management. Developers may also adjust their plan at other times during the day.

# Sprint Review

The Sprint Review is a working session in which the Scrum Team presents results to key stakeholders, and attendees inspect what was accomplished, discuss progress toward the Product Goal and environmental changes, and collaborate on what to do next. The Product Backlog may be adapted to new opportunities; the event should not be limited to a presentation.

# Sprint Retrospective

The Scrum Team inspects the previous Sprint with respect to individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and the Definition of Done. It identifies helpful changes to improve effectiveness and addresses the most impactful improvements as soon as possible, potentially by adding them to the next Sprint Backlog. The Sprint Retrospective concludes the Sprint.
