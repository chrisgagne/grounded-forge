An accumulation is something that builds up or drains over time. In system dynamics, an accumulation is also called a **stock**, and an action that increases or decreases it is called a **flow**.

The bathtub analogy captures the core behavior: the amount of water is the stock, the faucet is an inflow, and the drain is an outflow. Opening the drain does not make the water level fall if water is still entering faster than it leaves; it only slows the increase. Turning off the faucet also does not remove the water already present. The stock decreases only when outflow exceeds inflow.

Accumulations may be tangible, such as money in an account, dirty clothing, or people on a waiting list, or intangible, such as traumatic experiences or trust. A stock's current level embodies the full history of its inflows and outflows, which is why a present-day input alone may reveal little about system behavior.

Stock-and-flow diagrams identify accumulations and the actions that change them. They can reveal interventions aimed at inflows, outflows, or both. Their formal appearance does not guarantee accuracy: people often reason incorrectly about even simple accumulations, intangible stocks can be difficult to quantify, and every model remains dependent on the mental models and evidence used to construct it.
