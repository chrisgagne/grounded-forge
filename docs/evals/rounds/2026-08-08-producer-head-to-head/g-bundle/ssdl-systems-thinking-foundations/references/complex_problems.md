Complex problems change over time and arise from interacting system components rather than a single, stable cause.

Key characteristics include:

- **Dynamic behavior:** The problem and its surrounding conditions evolve over time.
- **Multiple stakeholders:** People experience the same system differently, pursue different goals, and affect one another through their decisions and actions.
- **Time delays:** The effects of an intervention may appear long after the action, sometimes after funding or support has disappeared.
- **Unintended consequences:** An action can improve a condition in the short term while creating feedback that eventually worsens the original problem.
- **Accumulation and history dependence:** Current behavior reflects the buildup of past experiences and conditions, not only what is happening now.

Linear planning tools can clarify resources, activities, outputs, outcomes, and impacts, but they may miss these features. System dynamics addresses that limitation with causal maps and simulation models that represent feedback, accumulations, and system behavior over time. These tools are most useful when complexity is central; an urgent decision or a straightforward calculation may call for a simpler method.
