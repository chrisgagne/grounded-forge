Feedback thinking examines how causal relationships can operate in both directions and form loops, rather than treating cause and effect as a one-way sequence.

Linear thinking asks how `X` affects `Y` and often addresses contributing factors separately. A feedback perspective looks for interdependencies: a change in one part of a system can alter another part, whose response can eventually reinforce or counteract the original change. This structure helps explain why problems persist and why technically sound, well-intended interventions can produce unexpected long-term results.

From a feedback perspective, intervention design focuses on changing system structure by adding or removing feedback loops, strengthening or weakening loops, or adding or removing causal links.

A causal loop diagram is a tool for describing these relationships. It should be treated as a hypothesis informed by the modelers' mental models, not as the single correct representation of a system. Sharing and revising the diagram with people who hold different perspectives helps expose questionable variables, mistaken causal links, and missing feedback.
