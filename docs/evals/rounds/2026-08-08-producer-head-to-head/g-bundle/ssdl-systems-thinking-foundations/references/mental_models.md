Mental models are internalized pictures or cognitive maps that people use to make sense of complex systems. They influence how a person defines a problem, the language used to describe it, and the actions chosen in response.

Every mental model is incomplete because it reflects a particular vantage point shaped by lived experience, values, assumptions, power, and bias. Stakeholders can therefore hold different but individually valid pictures of the same system. Acting from one partial picture can produce ineffective, counterproductive, or harmful interventions.

System dynamics makes mental models explicit through maps and models so they can be challenged, negotiated, tested against evidence, and refined. Group model building extends this process by bringing people together to develop a model, establish shared language, compare perspectives, and build common insight for change.

Mental models are also resistant to change. Simply disclosing assumptions does not necessarily broaden a person's beliefs, so eliciting mental models is best treated as an iterative systems-thinking practice rather than a one-time exercise.
