Larchwood incident notes (synthetic case; every organisation, person and number is invented)

What the engineering manager wrote at the start:

"We had a bad one last Tuesday (INC-2291) and I need to run the AAR this week. Around 16:00 UTC, payroll file submissions started failing for a big chunk of our employer customers. It went on for about two and a half hours and a lot of payroll runs missed the 18:00 bank cut-off, so thousands of people got paid a day late. Support spotted it from tickets before we did. The cause was a deploy of the payments API that afternoon. Our on-call SRE had silenced the alert that should have caught it, so we lost over an hour. Honestly, I think he just wasn't paying attention; he's fairly new. The dev who merged the library upgrade also clearly didn't read the changelog. My VP wants the AAR by Friday and wants to know who is responsible, so we can tell the bigger customers it won't happen again. Can you help me run it? I'm the engineering manager for the payments team."

What the review established (Larchwood, a B2B payroll SaaS company, about 140 engineers; the engineering manager of the Payments team has talked to the on-call SRE, the DBA, the support lead and the payments engineer who diagnosed it):

- Timeline (UTC), Tuesday 29 September. 16:05 deploy of payments-api v4.18, which includes a bump of the internal library platform-commons 3.4 → 3.5. 16:14 first failed submissions ("connection pool exhausted"). 16:22 alert `db-pool-saturation` (severity warning) posts to the #payments-alerts Slack channel; no page. 16:31 on-call paged for "payments p99 latency high"; acks in 2 min and silences it for 2 h. 17:10 first support tickets. 17:48 support lead escalates in #incidents with 41 tickets and a customer list. 17:55 Sev1 declared. 18:00 bank cut-off passes. 18:01 payments engineer finds pool exhaustion in logs. 18:05 decision to roll back. 18:33 DBA approves. 18:46 rollback done. 19:20 queued submissions drained.
- Impact: 1,180 employer accounts saw failures; 37 payroll runs missed the 18:00 cut-off. Finance ops paid the bank's fee for its late-processing window and got 22 of the 37 runs through that evening. The other 15 runs, about 3,800 employees, were paid a day late.
- What changed: platform-commons 3.5 lowered the default DB connection pool from 50 to 20 ("safer defaults for small services"). The note is line 40 of a 52-line changelog. payments-api never set its pool size, so it took the default.
- How the change got in: the bump was an automated PR from the internal dependency bot. Since February, minor and patch bumps of internal libraries auto-merge when CI is green; the policy came in to cut review load (the bot opens about 60 PRs a week). Nobody merged it by hand. The developer's name shows because he owns the repo's bot config.
- Why testing missed it: staging sets the pool size to 50 explicitly in its environment config (added in 2024 during a staging performance problem). Production relied on the default, so staging could not show the change. Load tests run only on major version bumps.
- What the on-call saw: `db-pool-saturation` was never silenced. It was created two years ago as a "warning", which posts to #payments-alerts instead of paging. That channel gets about 220 alerts a week and about 90% auto-resolve; nobody watches it live. The page he got was p99 latency, which had flapped 14 times that week, always around deploys. The runbook says "silence known flapping alerts for the deploy window (2 h)". He followed it.
- The on-call's situation: six weeks into the rotation. On-call shadowing was cut from four weeks to one in Q2 (hiring freeze, rota coverage). At the time he was also working an urgent data-export request for an enterprise customer, flagged by account management.
- Slow recovery: one-click rollback has been disabled since March, after a rollback corrupted a migration. A manual rollback needs DBA approval. Two DBAs serve nine product teams; the one on shift was in a vendor meeting until 18:30.
- History: the June AAR (INC-2104, a smaller latency incident) had the action "tune noisy alerts", owner "SRE team", no ticket; not done. In February the SRE lead proposed an alert-clean-up initiative; it was deprioritised for the Q3 enterprise-tier launch.
- Measures: the SRE team's quarterly goal is mean time to acknowledge under 5 minutes. A leadership dashboard counts incidents per team, and teams wait to declare until they're sure. Nobody declared on the 16:22 warning.
- What went well: the support lead's escalation was crisp (ticket count, customer list, first-seen time). The payments engineer found the cause 6 minutes after declaration. Finance ops got 22 runs through the bank's late window.
- Leadership: the VP Engineering wants "the person responsible" named in the customer write-up. Two large customers asked for "root cause and named remediation". The engineering manager is uneasy but hasn't pushed back.
- People: the on-call SRE has asked his manager whether he'll be taken off the rota. He is mortified. The developer is annoyed his name is on the PR.

And from the engineering manager, after hearing all that: "Can we just add more alerts on the DB pool metrics and call it done?"
