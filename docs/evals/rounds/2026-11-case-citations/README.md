# Case citations: do Gemini Notebook's markers point where its claims come from, on the questions a practitioner asks?

**TL;DR.** On 31 October and 1 November 2026, three case questions in a practitioner's own words (a CTO's statement about an overloaded engineering group; doctrine from war, spaceflight, nuclear and wildfire read against the management textbooks; the contemplative traditions read against the leadership literature) were put to Gemini Notebook over a 35-source public notebook and a private one in the low hundreds of sources, and to grounded-forge's `answer-from-corpus` in project mode over the same two corpora, two runs per case per arm. Every citation was graded on whether the chunk or source it points at carries the claim it sits on.

- **Asked for a memo, Gemini Notebook often doesn't cite.** On 7 of the 10 as-asked runs (all four on the public notebook, and C1 twice and C3 once on the private one) Gemini wrote the memo as a Studio report with no citation markers and a one-paragraph chat summary. The reports name sources, and most of what they say is in the corpus, but no claim can be traced to a passage. That is the deliverable a practitioner asks for, and on it the product's per-claim auditability is absent. The other three as-asked runs (private C2 twice, C3 once) stayed in the chat with markers; which way a prompt goes isn't predictable from the prompt.
- **Where Gemini cites, the markers mostly land near the claim, not on it.** Of 1,509 citations graded (404 on the public notebook, 1,105 on the private one), 2–14% per case pointed at a chunk that doesn't carry the claim; 41–80% at a chunk on the topic that carries only part of it or a weaker form; the rest carry it. The October pattern, markers on chunks from unrelated books, did not recur on either notebook. Its nearest relative is the private C3 chat pair, where a seventh of the markers open a reference list, a table of contents or a chunk about something else.
- **grounded-forge's credits hold up at 0–1% unsupported on both corpora,** with 2–12% partial, no wrong-book credits, and 142 of 146 quotations found word for word. Its partials are stretches, not inventions: a qualified position stated as absolute, a conference talk called research, a line an author passes on from someone else quoted as his own.
- **Both arms covered the answer keys about equally on the public notebook** (Gemini 17–19 of 22 expected ideas on C1 and 18 of 26 on C2 per pair of runs, grounded-forge 18–20 and 19–21). On the private notebook grounded-forge led by three to five ideas (18 and 19 against Gemini's 15–16 and 14–15) in the Claude scorers' grading; Sol's re-grade has the lead at about one idea per run. Both missed the same ones: the Definition of Done on C1, friction and deference to expertise on C2.
- **Scale did not reproduce October, and it did move the grades.** The share of markers that miss is no higher in the low hundreds of sources than at 35 (C1 chat 7% against 12%, C2 chat 2% against 2%). What rose is the partial share (73–80% against 41–64%) and wrong-book credits (11–35 per case against 3–4), nearly all of one kind: the sentence names a theorist and the marker opens a chunk from a book in the corpus that discusses them. A bigger corpus has more secondary literature to land on.

Three cases, two runs, one public and one private corpus, and Claude as case writer, scorer and one of the arms: read this as a direction, not a decimal. A Codex (Sol) re-grade of all 30 answers agreed with the Claude scorers on 96% of miss calls, moved grounded-forge's partial share up and Gemini's miss share down by a few points, read the key-idea coverage as about level, and left the citation gap: 19% of Gemini's markers carry their claim as stated, 83% of grounded-forge's credits do.

## Why this round

The [keyword-match round](../2026-11-keyword-match/) asked single-concept questions and found Gemini Notebook locates a plainly described concept whatever the words used. It did not recreate the October failure ([Q2 of the private round](../2026-10-22-gemini-notebook-private-corpus/)): a long case raising several issues at once, over a library in the low hundreds of sources, where Gemini's prose was right but every citation marker pointed at a chunk from another book. Two explanations remained, the shape of the question and the scale of the notebook, and this round tests both with the kind of question the operator puts to the corpus.

## What was tested

The design was committed before any run ([`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md), commit c7218594).

**Cases** ([`cases.json`](cases.json)), each the bare prompt a user would type. C1, *Harbourline*: a CTO's statement (overflowing sprints, marketing against engineering on quality, a stretch target with a bonus on it, developers on several squads, a postmortem that put one developer on a performance plan, a CEO who wants AI tools to "do both") asking for a two-page memo for the CEO on what's going on, what to change first and where the thinking disagrees with itself. C2, *Doctrine against the textbook*: what doctrine written for war, spaceflight, nuclear plants and wildfire says against the management textbooks' playbook, for a sceptical CEO of a 300-person company, with the sources' disagreements with each other shown. C3, *Contemplative traditions and leadership*: the esoteric contemplative traditions, the non-dual teachings, the Taoist material and the deeper end of the parts-work and coaching courses read against the leadership literature, as a memo for a CTO who reads widely; private corpus only. **Answer keys** ([`keys.json`](keys.json)): 11 expected ideas for C1, 13 for C2, none for C3.

**Arms.**

- **Gemini Notebook,** Pro plan, default chat settings. The public notebook is the keyword-match round's: 35 files, the originals behind all 31 demo sources. The private notebook is October's, low hundreds of sources, all ticked. Before every question the chat history was deleted and the Studio panel emptied. Claude drove both notebooks in Chris's browser, one notebook at a time ([`capture.js`](capture.js), extended in the page during the round to read and delete Studio reports). For each answer it saved the chat text with its markers, every marker's source name and full chunk text, and the text of any Studio report the run produced. Public captures are in [`runs/gemini/`](runs/gemini/); private captures stay outside the repo.
- **grounded-forge,** project mode over each corpus's reference tier (references, converted sources, concept and reference indexes, lens index), no distillations, through [`run_gf.py`](run_gf.py): isolated `claude -p` on Opus 5.5 at effort high, project and local settings only, auto-memory off, an empty MCP config, no Bash and no web. The ten runs cost $21.78, about four minutes each, 1,330–1,632 words. Public answers are in [`runs/gf/`](runs/gf/).

**Changes from the design, all made before any answer was scored:**

- **A second Gemini condition, `chat`.** The first as-asked runs of C1 and C2 came back as uncited Studio reports (below). The design assumed a cited chat answer, so each as-asked run was paired with a run of the same prompt plus the line "Answer here in the chat." The as-asked runs are the pre-registered condition; the chat runs are the only ones with citations to grade.
- **Three Gemini runs were repeated** (public C1-2, private C1-1 and C2-1) because the first capture lost the Studio report when the page script failed to open it. The repeats replaced them; nothing from the lost runs was scored.
- **Two notebooks can't run at once.** Chrome throttles a tab that isn't in front, so the public notebook's queue ran to the end before the private one started.

## Scoring

The unit is one citation: a Gemini marker occurrence (a marker that repeats on another sentence counts again), or a grounded-forge sentence that credits a source by name or quotes it. [`locate_chunks.py`](locate_chunks.py) maps each Gemini chunk to a corpus slug and a line in the converted text; [`build_packets.py`](build_packets.py) assembles one packet per answer with the question, the answer, every chunk in full and the quotation locations from the keyword-match round's [`quotes.py`](../2026-11-keyword-match/quotes.py). Fourteen Opus 5.5 scorers in fresh contexts (four with five packets each, then ten with one private packet each), every one with the brief in [`scores/scoring-brief.md`](scores/scoring-brief.md), graded every citation **supports**, **partial** or **none**, flagged **wrong book** (the sentence names an author or work and the chunk is from another), checked each quotation of five words or more, and marked each key idea present and credited to a source that carries it. Public score files are in [`scores/`](scores/); private ones hold claim text from private answers and stay outside the repo, so their rows below are counts only. [`summary.py`](summary.py) rebuilds every number from the score files.

**Where the scorers' rules differed.** The brief left two calls open and the scorers made them differently. On a sentence carrying several stacked markers, one scorer graded a chunk that carries only its part of the sentence `partial` (public C1 chat runs) and another graded it `supports` (public C2 chat runs); the gap between those two cases' partial shares is partly that rule. On wrong book, one scorer applied the brief literally and counted a chunk from a secondary source cited on a sentence naming the author it discusses (a textbook passage about Kotter on a sentence naming Kotter), which is most of the private C3 count. The ten single-packet scorers were given both calls settled the strict way (a chunk carrying one part of a stacked claim is `partial`; a chunk from a secondary source is that source), so the private rows other than C1-2 and C3-1 as asked are graded under the stricter rules, and their partial and wrong-book counts sit above what the public C2 chat scorer would have given. Neither call moves the `none` share, the pre-registered number.

**Second-family check.** Codex's `gpt-6.1-sol`, at effort high, one packet per fresh context, re-graded all 30 answers from the same brief with the strict rules. Over 1,519 citations both families graded, they agree on whether a citation misses for 96% and on the three-way grade for 79%. Sol's per-cell grades:

**Against the pre-registered readings.** The readings were set on U, the share of citations graded none. *October's pattern recurs* needed U at or above 25%: the highest cell is 14% (private C3 chat). *Scale* needed the private notebook's U to exceed the 35-source U by 15 points on both C1 and C2: it is lower on C1 (7% against 12%) and level on C2 (2%). *Shape* needed U at or above 25% on both notebooks: no cell reaches it. *Neither* was set at U below 15% everywhere, and that is where every cell sits. On these cases, at this scale, Gemini's markers open chunks on the topic. What the readings didn't anticipate is the as-asked condition giving nothing to grade on 7 of 10 runs.

**Kinds of failure.**

- **The uncited memo.** Asked for "a two-page memo for the CEO", Gemini Notebook opened a Studio report on 7 of the 10 as-asked runs: 14,000–24,000 characters, source names in the text, no markers, and a chat message summarising it. The reports are competent and mostly right. They also carry things the corpus doesn't: the public C1 reports quote Goodhart's law in wording that isn't in any source, credit the Substitution Test to Reason where the DOE handbook credits Neil Johnston, say velocity as a forecasting tool is "mandated by the Scrum Guide" (the Scrum Guide never mentions velocity), and give figures (20–30% of capacity recovered, a 70/20/10 matrix) that nothing cited carries. The private C1 reports credit a work that isn't in the corpus, credit the pro-target side of the targets disagreement to sources that don't carry it, and of their seven quoted phrases put one in a source's words. Without markers none of this can be checked from the answer.
- **Gemini's chat citations are mostly near misses.** The scorers' notes agree on the shape: chunks on the topic, cited for a sentence that goes further. The memo's specifics (a 20–40% context-switching loss, 80% of errors latent, queues going to infinity at full utilisation) sit on chunks that don't give those numbers; a Little's Law chunk is cited for a claim about utilisation it doesn't make; the Columbia board's independent technical authority is cited to chunks that don't carry it; recommendations about AI tools are cited to chunks about water-scrum-fall or TQM. Marker stacking is heavy, up to five on a sentence, each chunk carrying one piece of a compound claim. The outright misses (U) are few on C2 and a tenth on C1, where the case's specifics invite claims the sources don't make. On the private notebook the stacking runs to six markers a sentence and the partial share climbs to three quarters: exact figures absent from or misread off their chunks, a chunk cited for the opposite of the claim, coaching-course chunks about a person's inner parts cited for claims about organisations and leaders, and, on C3, reference lists, tables of contents and further-reading sections cited as support.
- **Wrong-book credits are secondary citation, not October's pattern.** On the public notebook, 7 of 404 chat citations: the Substitution Test credited to Reason on a DOE chunk, and Kotter named on a sentence whose chunks are textbook passages about him. On the private notebook, 128 of 1,105 under the literal rule: a theorist named in the sentence, and a chunk from a corpus book that discusses the theorist, a field guide's summary of the idea, or a bibliography entry. The chunk is about the named author; it is not by them. No marker on either notebook pointed at a book unrelated to its sentence; the nearest approach is one private C3 chat run with 29 of 154 markers on chunks that don't bear on their claim.
- **grounded-forge's partials are stretches.** Org Topologies' Resource topology cited for developers split across squads; the textbook's descriptive "likely to prevail" turned into "belongs"; a label ("outcome bias") the source doesn't use where it says hindsight bias; on the private corpus, Deming on numerical goals stated as absolute, and a passage Dekker quotes from a student credited to Dekker. Its one none: a claim that cascading targets "goes further than the textbook does", which the textbook's own cascade and management-by-objectives passages contradict. Of its 146 quotations, 142 were found word for word; the four misses are a marked `[s]` alteration, a translation set in quote marks, a singular made plural, and a quotation pieced from two fragments.
- **Both arms miss the same keys.** Neither arm, on either corpus, brought the Definition of Done to C1 or friction and deference to expertise to C2. Both frame C1's disagreement as goal-setting theory against Deming or Goodhart rather than against the overload the case describes.
- **Quotation.** Gemini quotes little and loosely: about 60 quoted strings across its 20 answers, 9 of them in a source word for word, and several of the rest paraphrases set in quotation marks. grounded-forge quotes constantly: 146 across 10 answers, 142 exact.

## What it says

**October's failure didn't reproduce, and the round shows what it was standing in for.** Over 1,105 private-notebook citations on three cases, two of them October's shape, no run put its markers on unrelated books. The markers land on the topic. What the grades show instead is a product whose per-claim auditability is thinner than it looks. Asked for a memo, it usually gives one with no markers at all. Told to cite, it puts three to six markers on a sentence, each opening a chunk that carries a piece of the claim or a weaker form of it, so the reader who clicks through finds the subject and rarely the statement. The share of markers that carry the claim as stated fell from 23–57% on the public notebook to 10–24% on the private one, and the wrong-book credits that rose with scale are the same thing seen from the other side: a bigger corpus offers more passages *about* a theorist to land on instead of the theorist.

**For the matrix.** grounded-forge's credits held at 0–2% none on both corpora under both scorer families, 142 of 146 quotations exact, and on the private corpus it covered more key ideas per case than Gemini (three to five more under the Claude scorers, about one under Sol). It costs about four minutes and $2 an answer against Gemini's two to twelve minutes on a flat subscription. The argument for pre-projected, source-only distillations is not that Gemini cites the wrong book. It is that a marker opening a chunk on the topic is not a claim traced to a passage, and the difference is what an auditor needs.

**Open.** Whether the Studio-report default on memo-shaped prompts can be turned off (nothing in the chat settings did), and whether a shorter question draws markers that carry their claims; each is a round of its own.

## Caveats

- **The scorers are Claude, and one arm is Claude.** Chunk and quotation location are mechanical; `supports` against `partial` is a judgement, and four scorers made two of the brief's open calls differently (above). The `none` share, which the readings turn on, is the grade they agree on, and the one a second model family agreed with on 96% of the citations it re-graded.
- **Per-citation support favours the arm that cites less.** Gemini puts a marker on most sentences and stacks them; grounded-forge credits by name and quotes. 404 Gemini citations on the public notebook against 175 of grounded-forge's, 1,105 against 254 on the private one, for answers of about the same length.
- **The as-asked condition can't be scored on citations.** The pre-registered reading needs Gemini's U; on the deliverable the cases ask for, Gemini gives no citations to grade. The chat condition is a workaround added after the first run.
- **Small n, cases written with the corpora in view.** Three cases, two runs, by the operator and Claude.
- **The private corpus is reported as counts.** No answer text, chunk or quotation from it is in this folder.
