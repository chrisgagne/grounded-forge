# K01B-2

Our pipeline throws the same warning on every deploy. Years ago it scared us; now nobody blinks, since nothing ever broke. How does a team drift into treating a red flag as fine?

Teams drift into treating a persistent red flag as acceptable through a well-documented socio-technical process known as the normalization of deviance[1:IN:caib-report-vol1]. When an anomaly or warning occurs repeatedly without causing an immediate catastrophe, the team's perception of risk shifts, gradually converting a warning signal into routine background noise[2:OUT:caib-report-vol1][3:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
This drift typically unfolds through several distinct mechanisms:
1. Reclassifying Anomalies as "In-Family"
When a build or deploy warning first appears, it is recognized as a deviation from expected design standards[4:OUT:caib-report-vol1]. However, if the deployment succeeds without incident, the team begins to reinterpret the signal[4:OUT:caib-report-vol1]. Rather than treating the warning as a sign of underlying danger, the anomaly is categorized as "in-family" (within the experience base) or an "accepted risk"[2:OUT:caib-report-vol1][5:OUT:caib-report-vol1]. Over time, the absence of failure is misconstrued as proof of safety[4:OUT:caib-report-vol1][6:OUT:caib-report-vol1].
2. The "Bald Tire" Effect and Habituation
Repeated success in the presence of a warning fosters a "bald tire" mentality—the flawed belief that because an action has not caused harm in the past, it will not cause harm in the future[3:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. Frequent, non-lethal warnings function like false positives or nuisance alarms[7:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][8:OTHER:jones-evidence-based-sweng]. As familiarity with the warning grows, the team's attention wanes, leading to inaccurate risk perception and complete desensitization to the hazard[3:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][9:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
3. Expanding the "Elastic Waistband" of Acceptable Deviations
As a warning becomes routine, the team's baseline for acceptable behavior expands like an "elastic waistband"[4:OUT:caib-report-vol1]. Once the initial deviation is tolerated, the newly expanded risk boundary becomes the new normal[1:IN:caib-report-vol1][4:OUT:caib-report-vol1]. Subsequent team members inherit this context and assume that the red flag is simply a standard property of the system rather than a flaw waiting to be fixed[3:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][4:OUT:caib-report-vol1].
4. Incremental Decisions and Production Pressure
Organizational drift toward failure occurs in tiny, incremental steps rather than all at once[10:OUT:caib-report-vol1][11:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. When trade-offs between thoroughness and delivery speed arise, bypassing a familiar warning appears entirely rational for any single deployment[11:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][12:OUT:caib-report-vol1][13:OTHER:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024]. Evaluated individually, each decision to ignore the warning seems harmless; cumulatively, however, these small choices erode safety margins until an unexpected combination of conditions causes a failure[10:OUT:caib-report-vol1][12:OUT:caib-report-vol1][14:OUT:caib-report-vol1].
