What is friction in war, and why does it make the simple difficult?

Friction in war is the collective force that resists all action, saps energy, and turns an apparently simple enterprise into an extremely difficult one[1:IN:mcdp1-warfighting]. Originally coined by Carl von Clausewitz, friction is described as "the force that makes the apparently easy so difficult"[1:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][2:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
Why Friction Makes the Simple Difficult
Abstractly, war can be portrayed as a simple clash between opposing wills, but in practice, countless unexpected and impinging factors accumulate during execution[1:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][3:IN:mcdp1-warfighting]. Friction makes simple tasks difficult because war is a dynamic human enterprise subject to continuous resistance and friction across multiple dimensions[3:IN:mcdp1-warfighting]:
Mental Friction: Stems from hesitation, confusion, or indecision over a course of action under high stress[3:IN:mcdp1-warfighting].
Physical Friction: Results from tangible obstacles, such as effective enemy fire, harsh terrain, or physical exhaustion[3:IN:mcdp1-warfighting].
External Friction: Imposed by factors beyond immediate control, including enemy counteractions, bad weather, difficult environments, and the ungovernable element of chance[3:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][4:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
Self-Induced Friction: Caused by internal organizational failures, such as a lack of clearly defined goals, poor coordination, overly complicated plans, complex task organizations, or complex technologies[3:IN:mcdp1-warfighting].
Because these physical, mental, and environmental difficulties continuously accumulate, battle plans routinely go awry, communications fail, and simple instructions are misinterpreted[3:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][5:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
Overcoming and Exploiting Friction
Will and Mindset: While militaries must minimize self-induced friction, they cannot eliminate friction entirely[6:IN:mcdp1-warfighting]. The primary means to overcome friction is human will—prevailing through persistent strength of mind, courage, and spirit[6:IN:mcdp1-warfighting].
Experience: Real combat experience and realistic training allow leaders to appreciate the true force of friction and develop accurate judgment regarding what is achievable on the battlefield[6:IN:mcdp1-warfighting].
Imposing Friction on the Opponent: Beyond managing friendly friction, a key operational goal is to actively raise the enemy's friction[6:IN:mcdp1-warfighting]. By using speed, maneuver, and initiative, a force can keep the enemy off-balance so their reactions are increasingly late until they are overcome by events[7:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
