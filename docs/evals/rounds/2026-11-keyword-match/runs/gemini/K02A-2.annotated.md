What is friction in war, and why does it make the simple difficult?

In military doctrine, friction is defined as the collective force that resists all action, saps energy, and makes the apparently easy so difficult[1:IN:mcdp1-warfighting]. As Carl von Clausewitz famously observed, while everything in war may seem simple conceptually, the simplest task becomes difficult in practice because minor complications continuously accumulate[1:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][2:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
Why Friction Makes the Simple Difficult
War is a Clash of Opposing Wills: War is an interactive social process between independent, hostile human wills, rather than an action performed against an inanimate object[3:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting][4:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting]. Because an active opponent resists, adapts, and attempts to impose their own will, even basic plans encounter immediate counterforces[1:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][4:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
Accumulation of Countless Factors: An operation that appears straightforward on paper inevitably encounters near-countless physical, mental, external, and internal complications during execution[1:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][5:IN:mcdp1-warfighting].
External Impediments: Friction is continuously generated by external forces beyond a unit's control, including enemy fire, terrain obstacles, bad weather, and the element of chance[5:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][6:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
Self-Induced Friction: Friction can also be self-inflicted through organizational deficiencies, such as a lack of clearly defined goals, poor coordination, unclear or complicated plans, complex task organizations, and complicated technologies[5:IN:mcdp1-warfighting].
The Human Dimension: Because war is fundamentally a human enterprise, physical obstacles inevitably produce psychological friction—manifesting as indecision, fear, exhaustion, and moral strain—which further hinders execution[5:IN:mcdp1-warfighting][7:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
Because military organizations function as complex systems, individual units must contend with friction at their own level while often inadvertently generating friction for other friendly elements as well as the enemy[8:OUT:mcdp1-warfighting].
