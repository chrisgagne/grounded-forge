# K03B-1

Writing up last month's crane incident, my reviewers keep saying "had Sam checked the brake, we'd be fine." Is that a helpful way to frame the write-up?

No, framing the write-up around "had Sam checked the brake, we'd be fine" is not a helpful or productive approach for an incident report[1:IN:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024][2:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024].
Human Performance Improvement (HPI) and modern safety learning frameworks caution against this type of framing for several key reasons:
Why Counterfactual Framing Hinders Learning
It relies on counterfactual reasoning and hindsight bias: Statements like "if only X had done Y" are counterfactual ("contrary to the facts") and presume an unknowable outcome[2:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024][3:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024]. They reflect hindsight bias, using post-event knowledge to judge past actions rather than understanding the event as it unfolded in real time[4:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][5:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2][6:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024].
It explains what didn't happen, not what did: Pointing out what someone could or should have done explains nothing about why they took the actions they did, nor why their choices made sense to them at the time based on their focus, cues, and working conditions[4:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][5:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2][7:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2].
It triggers a "blame cycle" and hides systemic risks: Treating an individual omission as the cause creates a false belief that the problem is solved (e.g., by disciplining or retraining Sam)[8:OTHER:caib-report-vol1][9:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. This stops the investigation prematurely, leaving latent organizational weaknesses—such as flawed processes, ambiguous procedures, or poor equipment design—dormant in the system to cause future mishaps[9:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][10:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][11:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2].
It damages trust and reporting culture: Focusing on individual fault destroys psychological safety and forward-looking accountability, making workers far less willing to report errors or near-misses in the future[12:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024][13:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024][14:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024][15:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024].
How to Reframe the Write-Up
To turn the write-up into a meaningful learning opportunity, shift the focus from individual fault to systemic context:
Explore Local Rationality: Instead of asking why Sam failed to check the brake, investigate why Sam's actions made sense at the time[4:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][5:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2]. Were there competing priorities, time pressures, confusing displays, environmental distractions, or interruptions[16:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][17:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2][18:OUT:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024]?
Analyze the "Gap": Contrast work as imagined by procedures or management with work as actually performed on the job site to uncover systemic vulnerabilities[19:IN:lfuo-learning-review-guide-2024].
Examine Controls and Defenses: Evaluate whether the maintenance schedules, pre-job briefings, inspection checklists, or physical barriers were adequate, workable, and clear[20:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][21:OTHER:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2].
