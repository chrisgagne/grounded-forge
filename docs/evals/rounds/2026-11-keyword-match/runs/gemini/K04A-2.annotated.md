# K04A-2

What is the Definition of Done, and what happens to a Product Backlog item that doesn't meet it?

The Definition of Done (DoD) is a formal description of the state of the product Increment when it satisfies the required quality measures for the product[1:IN:scrum-guide-2020]. It creates transparency by establishing a shared, agreed-upon understanding among the Scrum Team and stakeholders regarding what functional and non-functional work was completed for a work item[1:IN:scrum-guide-2020][2:ALT:open-practice-library]. The exact moment a Product Backlog item meets the Definition of Done, a usable product Increment is born[1:IN:scrum-guide-2020][3:OUT:scrum-guide-2020].
If organizational quality standards exist, the team must adopt them as a minimum baseline; otherwise, the Scrum Team creates a Definition of Done tailored to the product, which Developers are required to conform to[1:IN:scrum-guide-2020][4:IN:scrum-guide-2020].
What Happens to an Item That Doesn't Meet the Definition of Done?
When a Product Backlog item does not meet the Definition of Done by the end of a Sprint:
It cannot be released or presented: The item cannot be released to production, nor can it be presented/demonstrated at the Sprint Review[1:IN:scrum-guide-2020][5:OTHER:approach-perfect-field-guide-scrum-events]. This maintains transparency and prevents creating a false impression of progress[5:OTHER:approach-perfect-field-guide-scrum-events].
It returns to the Product Backlog: The item is placed back into the Product Backlog for future re-prioritization, refinement, and consideration in upcoming Sprint Planning sessions[1:IN:scrum-guide-2020].
No partial credit is awarded: No partial story points or fractional progress are credited to the completed Sprint for unfinished work[6:OTHER:approach-perfect-field-guide-scrum-events]. The Product Owner and team re-evaluate the item's remaining scope and priority empirically[6:OTHER:approach-perfect-field-guide-scrum-events].
