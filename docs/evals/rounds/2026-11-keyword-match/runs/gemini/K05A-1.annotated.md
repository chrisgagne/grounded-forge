# K05A-1

What is the scope of work mandate on the vertical axis of the Org Topologies map?

On the Org Topologies map, the vertical axis represents the Scope of Work Mandate assigned to organizational units[1:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025][2:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Core Purpose & Economic Interpretation
Field of Play: It defines how broad a unit's operational domain is, governing how units collaborate, learn, and share work[1:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025]. Moving higher up the axis expands a unit's field of play, bringing it closer to holistically understanding and solving real customer problems[1:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025][2:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Switching Costs: In economic terms, the vertical axis measures the switching costs required to pivot to different work[2:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025]. As a unit moves upward, switching costs decrease, making it easier for the whole organization to adapt direction based on customer feedback, innovation, or strategic shifts[2:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Outputs vs. Outcomes: The vertical axis separates output-oriented archetypes at lower levels (producing artifacts, features, or completing tasks) from outcome-oriented archetypes at higher levels (focusing on broader business goals and customer value)[3:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025][4:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Team-of-Teams Alignment: At the highest levels, the work mandates of various units overlap, encouraging them to co-own initiatives and function as a synchronized "Team-of-Teams"[1:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
The Five Vertical Levels (Bottom to Top)
The vertical axis is structured across five distinct tiers [148, 369–371]:
Tasks Focus: Units receive and execute narrow, fine-grained tasks matching specific functional expertise (e.g., a sous-chef chopping onions or a visual designer sketching wireframes)[5:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Capabilities Focus: Units own an entire capability or feature set within a product or service (e.g., a software Search Team or an Insurance Claims-Processing Group)[5:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Partial-Business Focus: Units operate within a distinct business vertical or profit center (e.g., Uber Ride versus Uber Eats)[6:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025]. Cross-team collaboration increases at this level because teams directly share accountability for business success[6:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Whole-Business Focus: Units collaborate across the whole business without permanent internal silos or sub-product boundaries (e.g., an early-stage startup team)[7:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Unbounded: Units have a mandate to explore unknown markets, satisfy undiscovered customer needs, and freely pivot their direction (e.g., a failing games company pivoting until developing Slack)[7:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
