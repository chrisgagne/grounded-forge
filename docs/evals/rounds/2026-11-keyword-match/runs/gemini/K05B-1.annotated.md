# K05B-1

Our squads get handed tiny tickets like "tweak this button". How do I describe how far up the ladder of responsibility a squad's remit reaches, from chores to an entire product?

To describe how far up the ladder of responsibility a squad's remit reaches, the Org Topologies framework provides a visual model called the Scope of Work Mandate (the vertical axis of organizational design)[1:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
This vertical axis tracks the boundary of problem-solving handed to a team, ranging from fine-grained chores at the bottom to full business/product ownership at the top[1:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025][2:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025]:
The Ladder of Work Mandate (Vertical Axis)
Tasks Focus (Chores & Tickets)
Scope: The squad receives narrow, fine-grained tasks matching specific expertise (e.g., "tweak this button", "design a wireframe", "write CSS")[2:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Dynamics: The squad operates as a Doing Archetype[3:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025]. Members lack visibility into the whole picture, cannot create end-to-end customer value independently, and rely entirely on external planners and coordinators to define what needs to be built[2:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025][3:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Capabilities Focus (Features & Sub-functions)
Scope: The squad owns a complete functional feature or capability within a product or service (e.g., search feature, checkout flow, or claims processing)[2:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Dynamics: While teams at this level have more autonomy over a component, they often operate in Delivering Archetypes (sometimes called a "Feature Factory")[3:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025][4:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025]. They optimize for predictable output within a narrow functional silo but still rely on external management for overall product direction[3:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025][5:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Partial-Business Focus (Business Verticals / Sub-products)
Scope: The squad (or team-of-teams) owns a distinct vertical business line or service area (e.g., Uber Ride vs. Uber Eats)[6:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Dynamics: The squad takes direct responsibility for business outcomes and customer value within that vertical, collaborating across boundaries to drive commercial success or failure[6:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Whole-Business Focus (Entire Product / End-to-End)
Scope: Squads collaborate directly on whole-product problems and broad customer value without permanent internal sub-product barriers[7:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Dynamics: The squad functions as a Driving Archetype, where directing, doing, and delivering merge into a single cohesive unit that owns the overarching product roadmap and customer outcome[7:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025][8:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025][9:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Unbounded Focus
Scope: The highest level of responsibility, where teams have the mandate to explore undiscovered customer needs or pivot to entirely new markets (e.g., pivoting a product strategy based on user feedback)[7:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025].
The Complementary Dimension: Scope of Skills Mandate
To successfully move up the vertical ladder, a squad also needs a broader Scope of Skills Mandate (the horizontal axis of Org Topologies)[1:IN:org-topologies-primer-2025][10:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025]:
Functional Units: Single-specialty groups relying on constant hand-offs[11:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Multi-Skill Teams: Cross-functional but incomplete, retaining dependencies on outside specialists[12:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025].
End-to-End / Expanding Teams: Possess all required skills—and the mandate to learn new ones—to deliver complete value without external hand-offs[13:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Key Concepts to Frame the Remit Shift
Outputs vs. Outcomes: Squads handed tiny tickets are trapped in an output mindset (measuring how many tickets or features are completed)[14:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025]. Elevating their remit shifts them to an outcome mindset (measuring customer impact, revenue, or problem resolution)[14:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025].
Directing vs. Driving: When management writes detailed task specs and delegates chores, they act as Directing Archetypes while the squad acts as Doing Archetypes[3:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025][15:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025]. In an Adaptive Topology, those boundaries dissolve into Driving Archetypes, empowering squads to self-manage towards a long-term Product Goal rather than task lists[9:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025][16:OUT:org-topologies-primer-2025][17:OTHER:scrum-guide-2020].
Cascading Strategic Context: Frameworks like NCTs (Narratives, Commitments, Tasks) or Lean Value Trees (Vision → Strategic Goals → Bets → Initiatives) bridge this gap by giving squads high-level strategic bets or outcome commitments, allowing the squad itself to decide which micro-tasks are necessary to achieve the goal[18:OTHER:open-practice-library][19:OTHER:open-practice-library][20:OTHER:open-practice-library].
