# K06A-2

What is the paper plant, and how can flaws in it lead to error in the physical plant?

The physical plant consists of the physical systems, structures, and components—such as equipment, hardware, valves, and piping—that make up a facility and are used in its operational processes[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][2:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
In contrast, the paper plant is the aggregate of all documentation, directives, procedures, instructions, engineering drawings, specifications, work packages, and design basis documentation that control, direct, and maintain the configuration of the physical plant[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][4:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
While front-line workers directly touch and manipulate the physical plant, knowledge workers—such as engineers, procedure writers, planners, and technical staff—create and alter the paper plant[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][5:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][6:ALT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2].
How Flaws in the Paper Plant Lead to Physical Errors
Flaws in the paper plant originate during planning, design, or procedure writing and propagate into physical plant errors in several key ways:
Creation of Dormant (Latent) Errors: When engineers or technical staff perform incorrect calculations or write ambiguous procedures, they embed latent errors into the paper directives[4:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][7:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. Unlike active physical errors, which produce immediate and visible feedback, flaws in the paper plant can lie dormant and unnoticed for long periods[4:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][7:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
Provoking Active Front-Line Errors: Front-line workers rely directly on paper plant documentation to perform physical tasks[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. If work packages or procedures contain inaccurate or incomplete guidance, workers following those instructions can unwittingly alter the integrity of physical equipment—such as installing an incorrect gasket, mispositioning a valve, or hanging a danger tag on the wrong component[4:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
Invalidating Safety Margins and Design Bases: Inaccuracies in technical drawings or engineering calculations can invalidate the design basis for safety-critical systems[7:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. Because these paper flaws hide beneath the surface, the physical safeguards remain degraded without anyone realizing it until the equipment is called upon to perform its function or an event occurs[7:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][8:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
