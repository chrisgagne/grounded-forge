# K06B-1

An engineer edited a drawing and a manual years ago; the mistake sat unnoticed until a technician followed the drawing and got hurt. Is there a name for the set of records that mistakes like that live in?

In safety engineering and Human Performance Improvement (HPI), the aggregate body of records and documentation that controls a facility or system is known as the "paper plant"[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
The specific concepts that describe this type of failure include:
The Paper Plant: This refers to the full collection of engineering drawings, technical manuals, operating procedures, and design-basis documentation that defines how the physical facility or equipment is built and operated[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][2:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. When an engineer makes an uncorrected error in a drawing or manual, a flaw becomes embedded in the paper plant[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][2:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
Latent Errors / Latent Conditions: The unnoticed mistakes residing in those records are called latent errors or latent conditions[2:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][4:ALT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2]. Unlike active errors (which are committed by front-line workers directly on physical equipment and trigger immediate, observable consequences), latent errors lie dormant—sometimes for years—without causing immediate harm[2:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][5:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][6:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][7:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
Physical vs. Paper Breakdown: The dormant flaw in the paper plant remains hidden until a front-line technician acts on the instruction or drawing. At that point, the latent error in the paper plant is activated, resulting in an active failure or injury in the physical plant[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][2:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][6:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][7:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
