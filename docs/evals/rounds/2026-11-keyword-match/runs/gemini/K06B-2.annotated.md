# K06B-2

An engineer edited a drawing and a manual years ago; the mistake sat unnoticed until a technician followed the drawing and got hurt. Is there a name for the set of records that mistakes like that live in?

In safety and human performance frameworks, the aggregate set of documentation—including engineering drawings, procedures, technical manuals, and design specifications—that controls a facility or system's configuration is referred to as the "paper plant"[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][2:ALT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2].
The specific terminology used to describe these records and the dormant mistakes within them includes:
The "Paper Plant": While the physical plant consists of the tangible structures, equipment, and hardware, the paper plant encompasses all documentation used to direct, maintain, and control the configuration of those physical assets[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. Flaws created by engineers or knowledge workers become embedded directly in the paper plant[2:ALT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2][3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
Latent Errors / Latent Conditions: The mistakes residing inside these documents are called latent errors or latent conditions[3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][4:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. Unlike active errors (which produce immediate visible consequences), latent errors go unnoticed at the time they are made and can lie dormant in drawings or manuals for years without causing obvious harm[3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][5:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
Initiating Action & Physical Consequences: A dormant flaw in the paper plant remains hidden until a frontline worker or technician follows the erroneous drawing or procedure[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1]. The technician's action acts as the initiating action, transferring the hidden flaw from the paper plant into a physical failure or injury in the physical plant[1:IN:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][5:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][6:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1].
Configuration Control & Design Basis Documentation: In engineering management, this set of records is formally maintained under configuration control and forms the design basis documentation, which ensures that physical equipment aligns strictly with approved technical requirements[3:OUT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol1][7:ALT:doe-hpi-handbook-vol2].
