# K07A-1

What does the diathesis-stress model say about how a psychological disorder develops?

The diathesis-stress model asserts that a psychological disorder develops from the interaction between an underlying predisposition or vulnerability (diathesis) and external environmental or psychological triggers (stress)[1:IN:openstax-psychology-2e][2:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e]. Under this model, neither vulnerability nor stress alone is sufficient; both elements are required for a disorder to manifest[3:IN:openstax-psychology-2e][4:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e].
Key Components of the Model
Diathesis (Vulnerability)
Biological factors: Genetic predispositions, brain abnormalities, or neurotransmitter variations (such as carrying short versions of the 5-HTTLPR serotonin transporter gene)[1:IN:openstax-psychology-2e][5:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e][6:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e].
Psychological factors: Cognitive vulnerabilities, such as a pessimistic thinking style, depressive schemas, or a tendency to focus on negative life events[1:IN:openstax-psychology-2e][7:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e].
Stress (Environmental Triggers)
Adverse life events or environmental conditions, including childhood trauma or maltreatment, major life changes, family conflict, loss, or chronic daily stressors[1:IN:openstax-psychology-2e][4:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e][8:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e].
The Inverse Relationship
The level of stress needed to produce a psychological disorder is inversely proportional to the level of diathesis[3:IN:openstax-psychology-2e]. An individual with a high genetic or psychological vulnerability requires relatively little stress to trigger a disorder, whereas someone with low vulnerability would require a significantly higher degree of environmental stress[3:IN:openstax-psychology-2e].
Examples in Psychopathology
Schizophrenia: In adoption studies, adoptees with high genetic risk (whose biological mothers had schizophrenia) were much more likely to develop schizophrenia if raised in a disturbed family environment (36.8%) than if raised in a healthy family environment (5.8%)[4:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e][9:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e]. Adoptees with low genetic risk had low rates of schizophrenia regardless of home environment (~5%)[4:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e][9:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e].
Major Depressive Disorder: Individuals carrying short alleles of the 5-HTTLPR gene show significantly higher rates of depression when exposed to childhood maltreatment or multiple stressful life events, whereas those with long alleles remain relatively resilient under similar stress[6:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e][10:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e].
Posttraumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD): Research indicates that the interaction of genetic factors (such as serotonin gene variations) combined with severe trauma or childhood adversity strongly influences whether an individual develops PTSD[11:OUT:openstax-psychology-2e].
