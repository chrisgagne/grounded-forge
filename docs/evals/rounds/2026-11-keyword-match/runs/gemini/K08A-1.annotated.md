What does equity theory say about how our outcomes and inputs, compared with other people's, affect motivation?

According to equity theory, human motivation is strongly driven by how fairly individuals perceive they are treated relative to others in a social exchange[1:EMPTY:][2:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior]. Rather than judging rewards strictly in an absolute sense, employees evaluate the relationship between what they contribute and what they receive, comparing their balance to that of a referent other (such as a coworker performing similar work)[2:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][3:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].

3. Motivational Actions to Reduce Inequity
When perceived underreward creates tension, equity theory predicts that employees will be motivated to eliminate the inequity through several behavioral or cognitive strategies[12:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][13:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][16:ALT:openstax-principles-management]:
Altering one's own inputs: Reducing work effort, slowing down production, or decreasing work quality[17:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][18:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Altering one's own outcomes: Demanding a salary increase, asking for better perks, or seeking additional rewards[17:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][18:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Altering the referent other's inputs or outcomes: Pressuring the referent coworker to exert more effort or attempting to lower the coworker's rewards[19:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][20:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Distorting perceptions: Cognitively reinterpreting the situation—such as convincing oneself that the referent other works harder or has a more difficult job than initially thought[17:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][20:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Changing the referent other: Selecting a different person or baseline for comparison to evaluate fairness[21:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][22:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
Leaving the situation: Quitting the job, transferring to another department, or escaping the work environment entirely[13:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][17:ALT:openstax-introduction-business].
