What does equity theory say about how our outcomes and inputs, compared with other people's, affect motivation?

Equity theory posits that human motivation is heavily driven by our perceptions of fairness regarding the rewards we receive in exchange for our contributions, compared to what others receive for theirs[1:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][2:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
1. Inputs, Outcomes, and the Comparison Ratio
According to the theory, individuals continuously monitor the fairness of their work environment by evaluating two key sets of factors[3:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior]:
Inputs: The contributions an individual brings to the organization that they perceive as valuable (e.g., time, effort, performance level, education, skill, and bypassed opportunities)[3:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Outcomes: What the individual perceives receiving back from the organization in exchange for those inputs (e.g., salary, benefits, working conditions, job status, recognition, and feelings of achievement)[1:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][4:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].

3. How Inequity Affects Behavioral Motivation
When a perceived state of underreward inequity exists, the resulting psychological tension motivates the individual to restore equity through one or more of the following strategies[8:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][10:ALT:openstax-introduction-business]:
Altering inputs: Exerting less effort, decreasing work quality, or slowing down productivity on the job[10:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][11:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Altering outcomes: Requesting a raise, seeking better benefits/promotions, or in extreme cases, attempting to enhance outcomes through theft[10:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][11:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Altering inputs of the referent other: Pressuring the referent other to work harder or "pull their weight"[12:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Altering outcomes of the referent other: Attempting to decrease the referent other's pay or rewards[13:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Distorting perceptions: Psychologically re-evaluating the situation (e.g., telling oneself that the coworker has more responsibility or less job flexibility)[10:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][13:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Changing the referent other: Comparing oneself to a different person whose inputs and outcomes present a more favorable comparison[14:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Leaving the situation: Quitting the job or transferring to escape the unfair environment[8:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][10:ALT:openstax-introduction-business].
