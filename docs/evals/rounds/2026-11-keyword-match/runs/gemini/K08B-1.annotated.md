# K08B-1

My colleague learned that a recently recruited guy at her desk earns a bigger salary than she does, and she's lost all drive. What explains that?

Your colleague's loss of drive is explained by fundamental principles of organizational behavior and motivation theory, primarily Equity Theory and perceived underreward inequity[1:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][2:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][3:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][4:ALT:openstax-principles-management][5:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
1. Perceived Underreward Inequity
Equity Theory states that employee motivation is heavily influenced by how fairly individuals feel they are treated in comparison to their peers[1:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][3:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][5:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. Employees continuously evaluate their workplace experience by calculating an outcome-to-input ratio[6:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][7:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][8:ALT:openstax-principles-management][9:ALT:openstax-principles-management]:
Inputs: What the employee contributes to the organization, such as effort, time, performance, skills, and length of service[6:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][8:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
Outcomes: What the employee receives in return, primarily compensation, status, and benefits[10:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][11:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
Employees evaluate fairness by comparing their own ratio against a referent other—in this case, a desk mate performing similar work[2:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][4:ALT:openstax-principles-management][7:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][9:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. When an experienced employee discovers that a newly hired colleague receives a higher salary (a greater outcome) for comparable or lower inputs, she experiences underreward inequity[2:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][4:ALT:openstax-principles-management][12:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][13:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][14:ALT:openstax-principles-management][15:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. Even though her own job duties and pay have not changed, discovering the disparity causes job satisfaction to evaporate[2:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][4:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
2. Reducing Effort to Restore Fairness
According to Adams' Equity Theory, underreward inequity creates internal tension, dissatisfaction, and anger, prompting the employee to take action to restore perceived equity[13:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][15:ALT:openstax-principles-management][16:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][17:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. One of the most common ways an employee attempts to rebalance the ratio is by altering her inputs—specifically by decreasing the quantity or quality of her performance and exerting less effort on the job[18:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][19:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][20:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. Losing her drive is a direct behavioral response to lower her inputs so they better match the relative outcomes she feels she receives[18:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][19:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][20:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
3. Distributive Injustice and Broken Expectancies
This situation also represents a failure of distributive justice, which holds that rewards should be allocated in proportion to an employee's contributions and tenure[21:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][22:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][23:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][24:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. Under Expectancy Theory, when an employee observes that greater experience and past performance do not yield superior outcomes compared to a new hire, her belief in a strong link between performance and rewards is damaged, eliminating the incentive to remain a high performer[25:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][26:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][27:ALT:openstax-principles-management][28:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
