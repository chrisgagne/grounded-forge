# K08B-2

My colleague learned that a recently recruited guy at her desk earns a bigger salary than she does, and she's lost all drive. What explains that?

Your colleague's loss of drive can be explained through several foundational organizational behavior and psychological framework principles regarding compensation, workplace fairness, and motivation:
1. Perceived Underreward Inequity (Adams' Equity Theory)
According to Equity Theory, employees continuously monitor fairness by calculating a ratio of their outcomes (such as pay, benefits, and recognition) to their inputs (such as effort, time, experience, and tenure) and comparing that ratio to a referent other—in this case, her male desk mate[1:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][2:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][3:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][4:ALT:openstax-principles-management][5:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
When she discovered that a newly recruited colleague earns more despite having less organizational tenure, she experienced a state of underreward inequity[6:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][7:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][8:ALT:openstax-principles-management][9:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. Equity theory predicts that when individuals experience the psychological tension and anger of underreward, they actively attempt to restore balance[7:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][9:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. The most common way employees do this is by altering their inputs—withdrawing effort, reducing performance quality, or losing their overall drive and engagement[10:ALT:openstax-introduction-business][11:IN:openstax-organizational-behavior][12:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
2. Violations of Distributive and Procedural Justice
Her experience directly touches two critical dimensions of organizational justice[13:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][14:ALT:openstax-principles-management]:
Distributive Justice: This refers to the perceived fairness of how rewards are allocated based on an employee's contributions[13:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][14:ALT:openstax-principles-management][15:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior]. Paying a new hire more for performing equivalent work signals that rewards are not tied to contribution or loyalty, creating a deep sense of unfairness[13:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][14:ALT:openstax-principles-management][15:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior].
Procedural Justice: This involves the fairness of the processes used to determine outcomes[13:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][14:ALT:openstax-principles-management][16:OTHER:openstax-psychology-2e]. When pay decisions feel arbitrary, opaque, or unfair, it acts as a primary trigger for workplace demotivation and dissatisfaction[13:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][14:ALT:openstax-principles-management][16:OTHER:openstax-psychology-2e].
3. Pay as a Hygiene Factor and Expectancy Breakdown
Under Herzberg’s motivator-hygiene theory, compensation is classified as a hygiene factor[17:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][18:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. While adequate pay prevents dissatisfaction, unfair or insufficient pay actively causes job dissatisfaction and erodes employee morale[17:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][18:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
Furthermore, Expectancy Theory indicates that motivation depends on the belief that high performance and effort lead to valued outcomes[19:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][20:ALT:openstax-principles-management][21:ALT:openstax-principles-management]. When an employee sees a newer peer receiving higher compensation for equal or lesser effort, her perception of the connection between effort and reward (performance-to-outcome expectancy) breaks down, leaving little incentive to go the extra mile[19:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][21:ALT:openstax-principles-management][22:OUT:openstax-organizational-behavior][23:ALT:openstax-principles-management].
4. Gender Pay Disparities
Gender pay discrimination remains a widespread workplace issue, where female employees earn less than male colleagues despite having comparable skills, education, and job responsibilities[24:OTHER:openstax-economics-3e][25:OTHER:openstax-psychology-2e]. Uncovering such an explicit salary gap directly in her immediate work area reinforces perceptions of systemic inequality, exacerbating her feeling of unappreciation[24:OTHER:openstax-economics-3e][25:OTHER:openstax-psychology-2e].
