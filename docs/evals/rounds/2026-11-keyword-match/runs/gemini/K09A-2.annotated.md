# K09A-2

What is the "fixes that fail" system archetype?

The "fixes that fail" system archetype describes a scenario where an individual or organization attempts to address a problem using a short-term fix or quick solution[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations]. While the fix may relieve the problem symptom in the short run, it generates unintended consequences that ultimately cause the original problem to return or worsen over time[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
System Structure
The archetype is built from two interacting feedback loops[2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations]:
Short-Term Balancing Loop (B): A problem symptom creates pressure for action, leading to an immediate symptomatic fix that temporarily reduces or controls the problem[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations][2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Long-Term Reinforcing Loop (R): The short-term fix produces unintended side effects or delayed consequences that feed back into the system, intensifying the initial problem[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations][2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Examples from the Sources
Exclusionary Discipline in Schools: When a student displays disruptive behavior in class, sending them to time-out or suspension serves as a short-term fix to remove the immediate disruption[2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations]. Over time, however, the unintended consequence is that the student becomes further disengaged from school, loses trust in adult authority figures, and identifies as a "bad" student—ultimately leading to increased disruptive behavior in the long run[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations][2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Partial Agile Adoption ("Water-Scrum-Fall"): Software teams attempting to solve speed-to-market issues might adopt Agile practices internally as a quick fix[3:OTHER:open-practice-library]. However, if upstream planning and downstream deployment remain bound to traditional waterfall methods, the broader organizational bottleneck persists and the speed-to-market problem remains unsolved[3:OTHER:open-practice-library].
