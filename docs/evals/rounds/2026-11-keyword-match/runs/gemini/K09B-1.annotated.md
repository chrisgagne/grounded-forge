# K09B-1

We hired temps to clear a backlog of customer complaints; the temps gave sloppy answers, which caused even more complaints. What pattern is this?

This scenario is a classic example of the "Fixes That Fail" (also referred to as "Fixes That Backfire") system archetype[1:ALT:barbrook-johnson-systems-mapping][2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
In system dynamics, this archetype describes a situation where an urgent problem symptom is met with a short-term patch; while the patch may seem effective initially, it generates unintended consequences that ultimately make the original problem worse[2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations][3:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Structure of the Pattern
Problem Symptom: A heavy backlog of unresolved customer complaints[2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations][3:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Short-Term Fix: Hiring temporary staff (temps) to clear the queue quickly[2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations][3:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Unintended Consequence: Temps lack full training or familiarity with the system, leading to sloppy or inaccurate responses[2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations][3:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Reinforcing Feedback Loop: The sloppy answers anger customers further, generating a wave of new complaints and enlarging the backlog[2:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations][3:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Related Pattern: "Shifting the Burden"
This scenario also connects to the "Shifting the Burden" archetype[1:ALT:barbrook-johnson-systems-mapping][4:OUT:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations]. Relying on temporary fixes (bringing in short-term labor) treats the symptom rather than addressing fundamental solutions—such as improving root-cause product/service issues, refining training programs, or expanding permanent capacity[4:OUT:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations]. Over time, relying on the quick fix can impede the organization's ability or incentive to invest in those lasting, upstream solutions[4:OUT:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
