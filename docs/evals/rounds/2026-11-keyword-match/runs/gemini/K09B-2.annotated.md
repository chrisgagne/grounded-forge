# K09B-2

We hired temps to clear a backlog of customer complaints; the temps gave sloppy answers, which caused even more complaints. What pattern is this?

This scenario is a classic example of the Fixes that Fail (or Fixes that Backfire) system archetype[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
In system dynamics, the Fixes that Fail archetype describes a situation where an urgent problem symptom is tackled with a quick, short-term solution[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations]. While the short-term fix may temporarily alleviate the symptom, it triggers unintended consequences that ultimately feed back into the system and make the original problem worse[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Here is how the feedback structure maps to your scenario:
Problem Symptom: A growing backlog of customer complaints[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Short-Term Fix: Hiring temporary workers to clear the backlog quickly[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Unintended Consequence: The temporary workers delivered sloppy, low-quality responses[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
Reinforcing Loop: The poor responses frustrated customers further, triggering a new wave of complaints and expanding the backlog[1:IN:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
This dynamic often connects to the Shifting the Burden archetype as well[2:OUT:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations]. In that pattern, relying repeatedly on a quick symptomatic fix (like temporary staffing) distracts from or defers investing in fundamental, long-term solutions—such as root-cause problem solving, process improvement, or proper training[2:OUT:ssdl-systems-thinking-foundations].
