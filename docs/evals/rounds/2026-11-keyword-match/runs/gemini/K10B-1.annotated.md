# K10B-1

Our board can't settle on a strategy, and nobody knows how the market will react. Is there a tool that plots issues by how sure we are of the facts and how much we see eye to eye?

Yes, the framework you are looking for is the Stacey Matrix (also referred to as the Agreement/Uncertainty Matrix)[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook]. Developed by Ralph Stacey, this visual tool is designed specifically to help groups evaluate decisions and choose the right management approach by plotting issues along two key dimensions[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook]:
The Two Axes
Degree of Certainty (Horizontal Axis): How sure you are of the facts, cause-and-effect relationships, and how the market or future will respond[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook].
Degree of Agreement (Vertical Axis): How much the stakeholders (e.g., your board members) see eye to eye on the goals, values, or actions to take[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook].
Matrix Quadrants & Recommended Approaches
By mapping your strategic topics onto this grid, the board can align on the appropriate decision-making strategy for each situation[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook][3:OTHER:open-practice-library]:
High Certainty, High Agreement (Simple): The outcome is predictable and everyone agrees[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook]. Traditional, plan-driven, or command-and-control management approaches work best here[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook].
High Certainty, Low Agreement (Complicated - Consensus Focus): The underlying facts and data are clear, but board members hold differing perspectives[1:OTHER:open-practice-library]. This calls for negotiation, compromise, and a consensus-based approach to align viewpoints[1:OTHER:open-practice-library].
Low Certainty, High Agreement (Complicated - Adaptive Focus): The board agrees on the desired outcome, but market responses remain unpredictable[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook]. This quadrant calls for an organic, adaptive approach that allows plans to adjust as data comes in[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook].
Low Certainty, Low Agreement (Complex): Neither the facts nor stakeholder alignment are clear[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook]. Traditional long-term strategy fails here because outcomes are emergent and unpredictable[4:IN:liberating-structures-handbook]. Instead, the board should adopt an experimentation and learning approach—running small probes or pilots to test the market and generate real facts before committing[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][3:OTHER:open-practice-library].
Far from Certainty, Far from Agreement (Anarchy / Chaos): Extreme unpredictability combined with total disagreement[2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook]. Standard planning breaks down entirely, requiring immediate action to stabilize the situation before long-term strategy can even be discussed[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][2:IN:liberating-structures-handbook].
How This Resolves Board Deadlock
Board deadlocks frequently happen when members try to treat a Complex issue (where facts and agreement are both low) as if it were a Simple or Complicated one, arguing endlessly over unprovable predictions[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][4:IN:liberating-structures-handbook]. Plotting issues on the Stacey Matrix shifts the conversation from "Who has the right strategic prediction?" to "What category of problem is this, and do we need an experiment or a consensus building process?"[1:OTHER:open-practice-library][3:OTHER:open-practice-library].
