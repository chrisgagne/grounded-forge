Yes. It's called **friction**. The term comes from Carl von Clausewitz, and the corpus covers it in the U.S. Marine Corps doctrine manual *Warfighting* (MCDP 1, Ch. 1, "Friction").

MCDP 1 says war looks simple when you picture it as two opposing wills. In practice it gets very hard because of the countless small factors acting on it, and those factors together are called friction. It quotes Clausewitz's definition: friction is "the force that makes the apparently easy so difficult." The manual adds: "Friction is the force that resists all action and saps energy. It makes the simple difficult and the difficult seemingly impossible."

Your relocation maps closely onto the four kinds the manual lists:

- **External:** caused by outside forces like terrain, **weather**, or plain chance. That's your rain and the late truck.
- **Physical:** an obstacle you have to get past. That's the missing key.
- **Mental:** for example, indecision about what to do next.
- **Self-induced:** caused by unclear goals, poor coordination, overly complicated plans, or tangled lines of authority. These are the ones worth checking for in your next move.

Your nervous crew fits too. MCDP 1 says that because the work is done by people, friction always hits morale as well as the physical job. The chapter opens with Clausewitz's line that everything is simple but the simplest thing is difficult, and that small difficulties pile up.

The manual's practical advice: cut down the friction you cause yourself, but accept that you can't get rid of the rest. The bigger task is to work well despite it, and that takes persistence and willpower. It also says you only really understand friction after living through it. So a plan that "looked straightforward on paper" is exactly where it shows up.

The corpus discusses friction only in a military setting. Applying it to an office move is my own extension.
