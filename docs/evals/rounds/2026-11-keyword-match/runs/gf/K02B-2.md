Yes. It's called **friction**, a term from the military theorist Clausewitz. In this corpus it comes from the US Marine Corps doctrine *MCDP 1: Warfighting* (1997), which quotes Clausewitz describing friction as "the force that makes the apparently easy so difficult." The manual adds: "Friction is the force that resists all action and saps energy. It makes the simple difficult and the difficult seemingly impossible."

The manual's starting point fits your move closely: on paper, war "appears a simple enterprise," and it only gets hard because of "the countless factors that impinge on it." Friction is the name for those factors taken together.

**The manual's types of friction, applied to your move:**
- **External: weather, chance, or something imposed from outside.** The rain, and probably the late truck.
- **Self-induced: unclear goals, poor coordination, or plans that are too complicated.** The missing key is likely this kind, since someone had to own the handover.
- **Mental: indecision and hesitation.** The manual says friction always affects people psychologically as well as physically, which covers the nervous crew.

**What the manual says to do about it:**
- **Reduce the self-induced friction.** This is the part you control: clear owners, simple plans and fewer handoffs.
- **Plan to work through friction rather than expecting to remove it.** "While we should attempt to minimize self-induced friction, the greater requirement is to fight effectively despite the existence of friction." Its main answer is persistence: "One essential means to overcome friction is the will."
- **Expect experience to help.** The manual says you can't fully appreciate friction until you've been through it. Experience gives you a realistic sense of what's possible, and no rehearsal fully reproduces the real thing. Your next move will probably go more smoothly because of this one.

Applying this to an office move is my extension. The corpus discusses friction only in this one military source.
