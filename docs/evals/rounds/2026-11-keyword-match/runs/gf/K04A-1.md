The answer comes from the 2020 *Scrum Guide* by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland. The Definition of Done is covered in its "Increment" section.

## What the Definition of Done is

The Guide defines it as "a formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product." It is the commitment attached to the Increment artifact, in the same way the Product Goal belongs to the Product Backlog and the Sprint Goal belongs to the Sprint Backlog.

It does three things:

- **It decides what counts as an Increment.** "Work cannot be considered part of an Increment unless it meets the Definition of Done." Meeting it is the dividing line: "The moment a Product Backlog item meets the Definition of Done, an Increment is born."
- **It makes the work transparent.** It "creates transparency by providing everyone a shared understanding of what work was completed as part of the Increment."
- **It is binding.** "The Developers are required to conform to the Definition of Done."

**Who sets it:** If the organization has a Definition of Done as a standard, every Scrum Team must follow it as a minimum. If not, "the Scrum Team must create a Definition of Done appropriate for the product." When several Scrum Teams work on one product, "they must mutually define and comply with the same Definition of Done."

## What happens to an item that doesn't meet it

The Guide says:

> "If a Product Backlog item does not meet the Definition of Done, it cannot be released or even presented at the Sprint Review. Instead, it returns to the Product Backlog for future consideration."

So unfinished work:
1. can't be released,
2. can't be shown at the Sprint Review, even as work in progress, and
3. goes back to the Product Backlog, where the Product Owner can reorder it for a later Sprint.

## Where else it appears in Scrum

- **Sprint Planning:** for each selected item, the Developers plan the work needed "to create an Increment that meets the Definition of Done."
- **Sprint Retrospective:** the team inspects how the Sprint went, including "their Definition of Done," so the definition itself can be improved over time.
