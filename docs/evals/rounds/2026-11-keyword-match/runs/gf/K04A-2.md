The Definition of Done comes from the Scrum Guide (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020). It is the commitment attached to the Increment, the usable piece of product work the team produces.

**What it is**

The Guide defines it as "a formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product." It is the line that decides what counts as finished: "The moment a Product Backlog item meets the Definition of Done, an Increment is born." The reverse also holds: "Work cannot be considered part of an Increment unless it meets the Definition of Done."

It exists to create transparency. It gives "everyone a shared understanding of what work was completed as part of the Increment."

Who sets it and who must follow it:
- **Organisation or team.** If the organisation has a Definition of Done as a standard, "all Scrum Teams must follow it as a minimum." If it doesn't, the Scrum Team must write one that suits the product.
- **Developers.** They "are required to conform to the Definition of Done." Several teams working on the same product "must mutually define and comply with the same Definition of Done."
- **Throughout the Sprint.** Developers plan their work against it at Sprint Planning. The team reviews it at the Sprint Retrospective.

**What happens to an item that doesn't meet it**

The Guide states this directly:

> "If a Product Backlog item does not meet the Definition of Done, it cannot be released or even presented at the Sprint Review. Instead, it returns to the Product Backlog for future consideration."

This has three consequences:
1. **It can't be released.** Unfinished work can't go to users or stakeholders.
2. **It can't be shown at the Sprint Review.** The team can't present it as progress, even as "nearly done."
3. **It goes back to the Product Backlog.** It doesn't carry over automatically into the next Sprint. The Product Owner, who orders the Backlog, decides when or whether it comes back.

So "partly done" work never counts as part of the Increment.
