The term you want is **work mandate**. It comes from Krivitsky, Larman and Flemm's *Org Topologies Primer* (2025). Their map of an organisation has two axes. The vertical one is the "scope of work mandate": how much of the business a unit is allowed to own. In their words, "The higher a unit is on the map, the bigger is its field of play."

**The five rungs, bottom to top:**

1. **Tasks.** The unit gets fine-grained instructions that match its expertise. Their examples are a sous-chef told "chop onions" and a designer told "design graphic". "Tweak this button" is a Tasks-level ticket. The authors stress that this is a choice made about the team, not a limit of the team: "It's an org design decision to limit their mandate, even though they may be capable of learning and doing more."
2. **Capabilities.** The unit owns a whole feature, function or service element within a product, like a Web Search Team or a claims-processing group. The cost they flag: "Common consequences include silo thinking and local optimizations."
3. **Partial-Business.** The unit owns a whole business line with its own profit and loss, like Uber Ride versus Uber Eats. Teams at this level work together across team lines because they share in the business's success or failure, so they care about outcomes.
4. **Whole-Business.** The unit works on problems and customer value for the whole business, with no permanent internal split. Their example is a two-person startup building something like Instagram.
5. **Unbounded.** The unit goes wherever it sees undiscovered customer needs, even into new markets. Their example is the failing games company that kept pivoting until it found Slack.

**Why it matters, in their terms:**
- The authors frame the axis economically as "the switching costs to work on something different." Lower rungs make it harder for the organisation to change direction.
- They also argue that "The larger the scope of work the units own, the closer they are to holistically understanding and solving real problems."
- Moving units up this axis (and along the skills axis) is what they call **elevation**.

**Using it with your squads**

Work mandate is only half the description. The horizontal axis, **skills mandate**, runs Functional → Multi-Skill → End-to-End → Expanding → Unbounded. The Primer's shorthand names combine the two axes: "CAPS-2", for example, describes a unit that owns a capability and is "multi-skilled yet incomplete." So you could say something like:

> "Our squads are TASKS-2: multi-skilled, but handed button-level tickets. We want to move them to CAPS or PART."

That describes both what your squads can do and how much they're trusted to own. The authors present the names as a shared language for describing where you are now and where you want to get to.
